Northern Norway · 21 May–7 June 1940

What kept the anchorage working?

Ships continued to unload and support the expedition from Harstad even as German aircraft attacked. The RAF history credits its two fighter squadrons and the anti-aircraft batteries together. The distance German aircraft had to fly also mattered.

26–28 May · Harstad and Narvik

Cover for the assault

On 26 May German bombers sank HMS Curlew and damaged HMS Southampton. On 28 May Allied troops took Narvik. Nos. 263 and 46 Squadrons provided fighter cover. The RAF area table records 95 sorties that day and no RAF aircraft lost, yet HMS Cairo was hit and German aircraft bombed French positions. The fleet and the troops could still come under attack on the busiest day of fighter cover.

Air Historical Branch, Table 2, printed pp. 95–96 · Derry, The Campaign in Norway, final assault on Narvik

2 June · Naval anchorage

What did the fighters accomplish?

The area return reports 75 sorties and 24 combats. No. 263 Squadron's reconstructed diary counts 55 sorties by its own pilots. The RAF history describes the incoming bombers being engaged and reports that the attack caused little harm. General Auchinleck sent the squadrons his thanks.

The same history found that raids had continued after the fighters arrived. Its judgment rested on the army and fleet retaining a usable base. Weather, guns and the attackers' distance from their airfields complicate any estimate of damage the fighters prevented.

RAF narrative, printed pp. 97–99 · No. 263 Squadron, Form 540, entry for 2 June
