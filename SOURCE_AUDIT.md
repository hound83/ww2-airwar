# WW2 European Air War Atlas v6 — historical source audit

**Research status:** 24 October 2026. This document records what was actually checked and what remains a bibliographic lead. The distinction is intentional: identifying an author in a Wikipedia footnote does **not** validate any number in that book, and a source for Normandy cannot stand in for worldwide German fighter strength.

### Key observations with precise scope

- RAF VII/107 **26 August 1939:** 788 all-front German single-engine fighters on strength, **686 serviceable**.
- RAF VII/107 **17 August 1940:** 1,076 on strength, **853 serviceable**. Not the count committed solely to the Battle of Britain.
- RAF VII/107 **31 August 1943:** 1,581 on strength, **1,019 serviceable**. The two numbers cannot be interchanged.
- RAF VII/107 **30 September 1944:** **1,984** on strength, **1,413 serviceable** (scan p. 41/PDF p. 42). Before this source was extracted, September was interpolated between August and October; replacing that extrapolation materially changes the chart and avoids a spurious smoother climb.
- RAF VII/107 **31 October 1944:** 3,055 on strength, **2,487 serviceable**. This worldwide return does not refute the 431 **Western command** fighter figure at another September date.
- RAF VII/107 **13 April 1945:** 2,173 on strength, **1,648 serviceable**. No compatible May 1945 original fighter table was located: May original charts are left **blank**, while the cohort simulator may contain a separately labeled scenario value.
- Official USAAF history ch. 17: September 1944 **daily average** fighters on hand/operational: Ninth AF 1,502/968, RAF Second TAF 999/879, Eighth AF 1,234/904, Fifteenth AF 559/402. Total 4,294/3,153 across these four formations. These are geographically and doctrinally diverse, not one directly opposing frontline.
- Eighth Fighter Command entire wartime aerial victory credits: Spitfire 13; P-47 1,550.5; P-38 278.5; P-51 3,328; sum 5,170. **Credited victories**, not matched German destroyed aircraft. Ground credits are a separate population.

## Other primary archives worth following

1. [RAF AHB original German translated loss index](https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/our-history/air-historical-branch/ahb-german-translations/). It lists Germany-wide losses 1939–41, then detailed **defense of Reich, Western Front, Eastern Front, Norway, Mediterranean** returns in 1943–44. Individual PDFs can supply finer-grained loss types, dates and front roles if digitized in the same units.
2. [Bundesarchiv guidance on downed aircraft and aircrew files](https://www.bundesarchiv.de/en/research-our-records/research-by-topic/the-fate-of-downed-airplanes-and-their-crews-in-the-second-world-war/) plus German BA/MA source codes given by Murray. A finding aid establishes **where** the data are held; it does not yield numeric observations without record access.
3. [US National Archives RG 18](https://www.archives.gov/research/guide-fed-records/groups/018.html) (Eighth/Ninth unit reports and tactical mission records) and [Air Force Historical Research Agency](https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/). Unit-level credits and losses could be parsed by month/model but need original mission and victory-award reconciliation.
4. [USAAF official history Volume II](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/AAF/II/) for pre-1944 European operations, [Volume III](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/AAF/III/) for all 22 chapters through VE-Day, and [US Strategic Bombing Survey](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/AAF/USSBS/ETO-Summary.html) for German industry/fuel/transport. Different scope; separate ledgers.
5. [Official USAAF Statistical Digest](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/AAF/StatDigest/index.html): tables **69** fighter crew losses, **104** first-line and other aircraft loss categories, **128** sorties by role, **159** combat mission airplane losses by cause, **167** enemy fighter-class claims. Digests use organization/theater and *airplane class*, not necessarily fighter **model**.

## Source uncertainty and replication rules

- **Recorded:** directly reported, original date/category/denominator. Different archival registers can disagree without one necessarily being fraudulent.
- **Source-compiled:** traced to unit/claim/MACR documents via an identifiable compiler; author choices may affect loss cause categorization. Separate from primary contemporary return.
- **Derived:** arithmetic using recorded values; exact original categories remain visible.
- **Higher-confidence estimate:** short (four-month or less) interpolation between compatible German all-front fighter serviceability reports; NOT a reported date.
- **Lower-confidence estimate:** longer interpolation, mixed-class allocation or inferred fighter type share. It is never substituted as a known loss/claim figure.
- **Scenario only:** manually adjustable pilot hazard, exposure and allocation assumptions; never confused with official 1936 national strength or imaginary May 1945 catastrophic losses.
- **Unavailable:** when the historical reporting series has no matching date/category, the corresponding cell/line is empty, even if a simulator still runs for the date.

The comparison between Allied **claims** and German **losses** is particularly sensitive to theater, date, cause and mission accounting. An Eighth Command type's share of all its wartime victory credits is not automatically its share of the whole war's German aircraft losses. Likewise a German production total is not an aircraft-ready inventory total.
