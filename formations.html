Spitfire fighter squadron

No. 92 Squadron RAF

In Kent the squadron intercepted raids. In Sicily it supplied cover over the beaches and then for a named naval force. Its diary lets us follow the change in work across individual days.

Battle of Britain · Sicily

Operations and evidence

  1. 9 Sept 1940

    Biggin Hill

    The squadron intercepted fighters close to its airfield. Two pilots were wounded and a third returned in a damaged aircraft.

    Form 540 Oct 1939- Sept 1940 | 92 Squadron · Biggin Hill, 9 September

  2. 13 Jul 1943

    Luqa to Pachino

    Eleven pilots patrolled the beaches on the way to their new field. The day's 41 sorties include flights from both sides of the move.

    No. 92 Squadron · published Form 540 transcription · Form 540, 13 July

  3. 14 Jul 1943

    Pachino

    Two patrols preceded the arrival of the ground party. A damaged Spitfire came back on the second patrol and three more patrols followed.

    No. 92 Squadron · published Form 540 transcription · Form 540, 14 July

  4. 16 Jul 1943

    Off Syracuse

    Eight aircraft covered shipping. The squadron identified Nelson and Rodney and associated them with a bombardment of Catania that evening.

    No. 92 Squadron · published Form 540 transcription · Form 540, 16 July

  5. 16 Jul 1943 – 17 Jul 1943

    Force H off Sicily

    Nelson’s ship chronology places Rodney back with Force H on the 16th and Nelson’s bombardment after the torpedoing of Indomitable early on the 17th. The two accounts agree on the ships’ presence but differ on the sequence.

    HMS Nelson, British battleship, WW2 · Service chronology, July 1943, 14th–20th

What remains uncertain

The diary establishes the protection task. Neither account measures attacks prevented or proves that the patrol enabled a particular salvo.

  • Luqa · 13 July 1943 departure
  • Pachino · 13–16 July 1943
  • Cassibile · 17 July 1943

USAAF Spitfire group

31st Fighter Group

The group provided western landing cover from Gozo, then followed the advance across Sicily. Three different records date the transition at Ponte Olivo.

Sicily

Operations and evidence

  1. 10 Jul 1943

    Gozo and the western beaches

    The group's Spitfires flew assault cover from Gozo. The naval report describes uneven cover across the American sectors.

    RAF Air Historical Branch · Sicilian Campaign, June–August 1943 · Preparation of Tactical Bases and Western Task Force Landings

  2. 13 Jul 1943

    Ponte Olivo

    The RAF narrative dates the group's arrival to 13 July, the day after the infantry took the field.

    RAF Air Historical Branch · Sicilian Campaign, June–August 1943 · The Landing of XII Air Support Command

  3. 15 Jul 1943 – 16 Jul 1943

    Ponte Olivo

    The tactical air force lists operational use on 15 July and the 31st stationed there on the 16th.

    Northwest African Tactical Air Force · campaign report · Appendices H and I, printed pp. 157–158

  4. 27 Jul 1943

    Termini

    The group moved to the north coast under XII Air Support Command.

    RAF Air Historical Branch · Sicilian Campaign, June–August 1943 · XII Air Support Command after 24 July

What remains uncertain

The records date arrival, field readiness and unit presence separately. None gives a daily group total for protection over the beaches.

Operating stations

  • Gozo · Opening landings, July 1943
  • Ponte Olivo · Listed 16 July 1943
  • Termini · 27 July 1943

USAAF P-40 fighter group

33rd Fighter Group

Pantelleria put American fighters within reach of the western landings. The move to Licata brought the group to the invasion coast while Axis aircraft were still attacking it.

Sicily

Operations and evidence

  1. 10 Jul 1943

    Pantelleria and the western beaches

    The group provided P-40 cover for the landings. The naval assessment of patrol coverage covers several fields and formations.

    RAF Air Historical Branch · Sicilian Campaign, June–August 1943 · Western Task Force Landings and XII Air Support Command

  2. 16 Jul 1943

    Licata

    The group moved from Pantelleria to Licata. Axis aircraft attacked Licata and Ponte Olivo that night, with little reported damage.

    RAF Air Historical Branch · Sicilian Campaign, June–August 1943 · The Landing of XII Air Support Command, 16 July

What remains uncertain

The group supplied cover even while ships were hit. The theatre-wide cover rate cannot be assigned to its P-40s.

Operating stations

  • Pantelleria · Opening landings, July 1943
  • Licata · 16 July 1943

    Syracuse approaches

    No. 92 Squadron supplied eight aircraft for shipping cover. This identifies one unit's contribution within the wing.

Hurricane and Spitfire fighter squadron

No. 249 Squadron RAF

The squadron carried its air-defence role from southern England to Malta, where it operated the first successful Spitfire delivery.

Battle of Britain · Malta

Operations and evidence

  1. 16 Aug 1940

    Southern England

    Two days after moving to Boscombe Down, Flight Lieutenant Nicolson attacked during the fighting despite his Hurricane catching fire. He survived and received the Victoria Cross.

    No. 249 Squadron (RAF) during the Second World War · Battle of Britain narrative, 14–16 August

  2. 1 Sept 1940

    North Weald

    The squadron moved into No. 11 Group and continued defending the London approaches through the attacks on its station.

    No. 249 Squadron (RAF) during the Second World War · North Weald, September 1940

  3. 7 Mar 1942

    Malta

    Eagle flew off 15 Spitfires and all reached Malta. No. 249 operated the first arrivals. A prior attempt had turned back after fuel-system trouble.

    Arnold Hague · The Supply of Malta, aircraft reinforcements · Spotter and Spotter II, 7 March; see Malta case

What remains uncertain

Fifteen arrivals count delivered aircraft. The delivery record gives no squadron serviceability or interception total for that day.

  • North Weald · September 1940–May 1941
  • Ta’ Qali · May 1941–November 1942

No. 601 Squadron RAF

The same squadron designation connects three different jobs: defending English sector stations, reinforcing Malta by carrier and covering the Sicily invasion.

Battle of Britain · Malta · Sicily

Operations and evidence

  1. 19 Aug 1940

    Debden

    After defending from Tangmere, the squadron relieved No. 85 at Debden during the attacks on Fighter Command's stations.

    No. 601 Squadron (RAF) during the Second World War · Battle of Britain, 17 June–7 September

  2. 20 Apr 1942

    Malta

    Pilots of Nos. 601 and 603 flew Spitfires from Wasp. Forty-six aircraft reached the island across the delivery, followed by damaging raids on the airfields.

    Arnold Hague · The Supply of Malta, aircraft reinforcements · Calendar, 20 April; Malta campaign narrative

  3. July 1943

    Luqa to Sicily

    The squadron covered the invasion from Malta and moved to Pachino, then Cassibile, during July.

    No. 601 Squadron (RAF) during the Second World War · Invasion of Sicily narrative and station list

The carrier reported the combined delivery. A separate No. 601 return would be needed to allocate arrivals, damage and subsequent sorties.

Auxiliary Air Force fighter squadron

No. 603 Squadron RAF

The move to Malta split the squadron. Its flying personnel reinforced island defence while its ground personnel took a different route and serviced other units.

Battle of Britain · Malta

Operations and evidence

    Hornchurch

    The move south brought the squadron into sustained combat. Its published history records the loss of 12 pilots and 16 aircraft by 5 September.

    No. 603 Squadron (RAF) during the Second World War · Hornchurch, 27 August–5 September

  2. 20 Apr 1942

    Malta

    Spitfires and flying personnel arrived with Calendar. The ground echelon travelled separately to the Middle East and later Cyprus.

    No. 603 Squadron (RAF) during the Second World War · April–June 1942, separate flying and ground echelons

  3. 3 Aug 1942

    Malta

    The flying element was absorbed into No. 229 Squadron. Following the original number alone would lose sight of those pilots.

    No. 603 Squadron (RAF) during the Second World War · Malta merger, 3 August 1942

The shared fly-off total gives no 603-only loss figure. The station list and narrative also differ on the merger year. The dated narrative places it in 1942.

Canadian Hurricane fighter squadron

No. 1 Squadron RCAF

The Canadian squadron reinforced the defence of London. Three successive scrambles on 27 September show how many aircraft it could put back into the air as the day wore on.

Battle of Britain

Operations and evidence

  1. 17 Aug 1940

    Northolt

    The squadron was declared operational after training at Croydon.

    History - Battle of Britain - Royal Canadian Air Force - Canada.ca · Timeline, 17 August

  2. 26 Aug 1940

    North Weald and southern England

    Ordered to relieve another unit, it met bombers on its second sortie. It claimed three destroyed and four damaged, lost three Hurricanes and suffered its first fatal combat casualty.

    History - Battle of Britain - Royal Canadian Air Force - Canada.ca · Timeline, 26 August

  3. 27 Sept 1940

    London approaches

    The first scramble sent up twelve aircraft, the second eight and the third six. Some of the day's victory claims were shared with other squadrons.

    History - Battle of Britain - Royal Canadian Air Force - Canada.ca · Timeline, 27 September

What remains uncertain

The three scramble sizes measure what flew. They do not identify how many aircraft were under repair, or why each pilot missed a later scramble.

Operating stations

  • Croydon · July 1940, training
  • Northolt · From 17 August 1940

Polish Hurricane fighter squadron

No. 303 (Polish) Squadron RAF

Polish pilots added experienced crews to Fighter Command. Their ground personnel also returned damaged Hurricanes to service in time for the next day's operations.

Battle of Britain

Operations and evidence

  1. 30 Aug 1940

    Northolt area

    During a training flight, Ludwik Paszkiewicz engaged an enemy aircraft. The RAF Museum places this encounter before the squadron’s formal operational declaration.

    Polish No. 303 Squadron and the Hurricane 80K - RAF Museum · Paszkiewicz training flight, 30 August

  2. 31 Aug 1940

    Northolt

    The squadron was declared operational. Its later victory tally covered 42 days of fighting.

    Polish No. 303 Squadron and the Hurricane 80K - RAF Museum · Operational declaration and claims period

  3. 15 Sept 1940 – 16 Sept 1940

    Northolt

    Following the fighting on 15 September, Flying Officer Wiorkiewicz’s ground team restored nine damaged Hurricanes overnight for the next day.

    Polish No. 303 Squadron and the Hurricane 80K - RAF Museum · Ground personnel and the night after 15 September

What remains uncertain

The repair count measures renewed availability. The squadron's victory claims would require matching German losses before assessing destruction.

Czechoslovak Hurricane fighter squadron

No. 310 (Czechoslovak) Squadron RAF

Pilots assembled at Duxford were flying in defence of No. 11 Group’s airfields by late August. By mid-September they were taking part in larger Duxford formations.

Battle of Britain

Operations and evidence

  1. July 1940

    Duxford

    Czechoslovak airmen formed a fighter squadron and trained on Hurricanes before joining the southern air fighting.

    Czech Ministry of Defence · Czech airmen in the Battle of Britain · Czechoslovak airmen, formation at Duxford

  2. 26 Aug 1940

    Approaches to No. 11 Group airfields

    The Czech account dates the squadron's first combat mission to this raid and credits its members with three enemy aircraft.

    Czech Ministry of Defence · Czech airmen in the Battle of Britain · First combat mission, 26 August

  3. 15 Sept 1940

    Duxford wing

    Flight Sergeant Bohumir Furst of No. 310 flew twice, once against the morning raid and once in the afternoon. His two flights identify an individual contribution to the larger Duxford effort.

    Imperial War Museums · Duxford Operations Block · Personal stories, Flight Sergeant Bohumir Furst, 310 Squadron

What remains uncertain

The five-squadron claim cannot establish No. 310’s share or the German losses on 15 September.

USAAF Fifteenth Air Force B-24 group

449th Bombardment Group

From southern Italy, the group attacked aircraft industry targets on three Big Week dates. Its account distinguishes enemy losses, damaged aircraft lost on return and a takeoff accident.

Defence of the Reich

Operations and evidence

  1. 22 Feb 1944
