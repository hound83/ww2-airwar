# Campaign evidence review

At the starting revision, two of the 20 campaign dossiers had an opening historical question and a direct link to a dated study. This review follows each route from campaign selection to an observed effect, a reporting source and an open question. The case link opens the selected event in the chronology or the specialist study at the named case.

| Campaign | Question and observed effect | Dated case | Reporting source and locator | Open question |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| [Spanish Civil War · Condor Legion](../grand-air-war-v80.html?campaign=spain#campaign-dossier) | **How did aircraft change the opening of the Spanish Civil War?** Much of the fleet remained loyal to the Republic, leaving the insurgents' Army of Africa across the strait in Morocco. German transport aircraft gave those troops a route to mainland Spain. The agreement in July began an airlift that took weeks. | [1936-07-26 · The decision to supply the airlift](../grand-air-war-v80.html?campaign=spain&event=1936-07-26#moment-detail) | [Bundesarchiv, account of the July transport decision](https://www.bundesarchiv.de/im-archiv-recherchieren/archivgut-recherchieren/nach-themen/das-deutsche-militaerische-engagement-im-spanischen-buergerkrieg-1936-1939/) | Which units arrived in time for which operations, and how many men and supplies also crossed by sea? |
| [Invasion of Poland](../grand-air-war-v80.html?campaign=poland#campaign-dossier) | **What could Polish airmen still do after the invasion began?** Dispersal let Polish crews keep flying. The Polish Aviation Museum follows three Karaś aircraft from a field strip at Klimontów to an attack on a German column near Orawka. The crews found the column and bombed it. How long the Germans were delayed remains open. | [1939-09-03 · Follow the raid from Klimontów to Orawka](../grand-air-war-v80.html?campaign=poland&event=1939-09-03#moment-detail) | [Polish Aviation Museum, Orawka crew account](https://muzeumlotnictwa.pl/en/aviation-traces/aviation-disasters/g-39-orawka/) | Can the German column's march record establish damage or delay after the attack? |
| [Norwegian campaign](../grand-air-war-v80.html?campaign=norway#campaign-dossier) | **Could RAF fighters keep the northern expedition supplied under air attack?** The RAF's history credits the fighters and anti-aircraft guns with helping to keep Harstad and the fleet anchorage usable. Raids continued. On 2 June the RAF reported an attack on the anchorage that did little harm after repeated interceptions. | [1940-06-02 · Read the defence of the anchorage](../narvik.html#anchorage) | [RAF Air Historical Branch, pp. 95–99 and Table 2](https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/our-history/air-historical-branch/second-world-war-campaign-narratives/campaign-in-norway-april-june-1940/) | What do ship logs and German raid reports say about the damage and disruption on 2 June? |
| [Battle of France and the Low Countries](../grand-air-war-v80.html?campaign=france1940#campaign-dossier) | **Could air attack stop the German crossing at Sedan?** RAF bombers attacked the Meuse bridges while German forces were building up their bridgehead. The crossings remained usable and the advance continued. The RAF Museum records 40 aircraft failing to return from a force of 71. | [1940-05-14 · Follow the attack on the Meuse bridges](../grand-air-war-v80.html?campaign=france1940&event=1940-05-14#moment-detail) | [RAF Museum, Battle of France, 14 May](https://www.rafmuseum.org.uk/our-collection/supporting-research/online-exhibitions/the-battle-of-france/) | Which bridge or crossing was interrupted, for how long, and which RAF formations do the differing loss totals include? |
| [Battle of Britain](../grand-air-war-v80.html?campaign=britain#campaign-dossier) | **Could Fighter Command still contest daylight raids over London?** Fighter Command continued to intercept the raids after weeks of attacks on its bases. In Parliament in 1947 the government compared 185 British claims for 15 September with 56 aircraft destroyed in German records. The interceptions demonstrate a functioning defence. The claims alone exaggerate its cost to the attackers. | [1940-09-15 · Examine the raids and the two loss accounts](../grand-air-war-v80.html?campaign=britain&event=1940-09-15#moment-detail) | [Hansard, 14 May 1947, Battle of Britain loss comparison](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/1947-05-14/debates/fa295090-0de7-4cfe-bf9a-7cf2739a70b4/RoyalAirForce) | How much bombing did particular interceptions disrupt, according to the formation and target records? |
| [Siege and air battle of Malta](../grand-air-war-v80.html?campaign=malta#campaign-dossier) | **How did fuel and fighter cover affect Malta's ability to keep flying?** Axis attacks sank many of Pedestal's ships. The survivors brought cargo and fuel into Grand Harbour, including the tanker Ohio. The convoy gave Malta's air forces time to continue operating while the supply route was still contested. | [1942-08-15 · Follow the surviving Pedestal ships](../malta.html#pedestal) | [RAF campaign narrative and Admiralty return in the Pedestal case](https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/our-history/air-historical-branch/second-world-war-campaign-narratives/middle-east-campaigns-vol-xi-malta-june-1940-may-1945/) | How quickly did fuel landed in August reach individual squadrons and change their flying programme? |
| [Operation Barbarossa](../grand-air-war-v80.html?campaign=barbarossa#campaign-dossier) | **What advantage did the Luftwaffe gain by attacking Soviet airfields?** The opening attacks caught many Soviet aircraft on the ground. German forces gained freedom of action in the opening battles, while surviving Soviet formations continued to fly. The USAF historical study's initial loss estimate covers only the opening attack. | [1941-06-22 · Examine the opening airfield attacks](../grand-air-war-v80.html?campaign=barbarossa&event=1941-06-22#moment-detail) | [Kreis, Air Warfare and Air Base Air Defense, opening attack in the USSR](https://media.defense.gov/2025/Jun/10/2003736254/-1/-1/0/AIR%20WARFARE%20%26%20AB%20DEFENSE.PDF) | Which Soviet formations recovered soonest, and how did their ability to fly compare with their reported aircraft strength? |
| [Dieppe: Operation Jubilee](../grand-air-war-v80.html?campaign=dieppe#campaign-dossier) | **What did air cover achieve at Dieppe?** Fighters covered the ships and the boats sent to recover troops. Close to shore, the landing craft still came under fire. No. 403 Squadron and the boat crews recorded different parts of the withdrawal. | [1942-08-19 · Follow the withdrawal](../dieppe.html#withdrawal) | [Canadian Army, Six Years of War, pp. 383–385](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/UN/Canada/CA/SixYears/SixYears-11.html#page383) | Where do the ships' accounts and the squadron diary record the same attack during the withdrawal? |
| [Stalingrad air operations](../grand-air-war-v80.html?campaign=stalingrad#campaign-dossier) | **Could the Luftwaffe sustain the encircled Sixth Army?** The airlift brought supplies into the pocket and carried some wounded out. When Soviet troops took Pitomnik, the Germans lost a landing ground equipped to handle those movements. Deliveries through the remaining fields and by parachute fell short of the army's needs. | [1943-01-16 · Follow the loss of Pitomnik](../grand-air-war-v80.html?campaign=stalingrad&event=1943-01-16#moment-detail) | [Air & Space Forces, Turning Point at Stalingrad, loss of the pocket airfields](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/article/turning-point-at-stalingrad/) | How much of each day's load actually reached troops in the pocket as one landing ground after another was lost? |
| [Defence of the Reich](../grand-air-war-v80.html?campaign=reichdefense#campaign-dossier) | **Where could German fighters still break up American daylight raids?** Beyond Allied escort range, German fighters repeatedly attacked the bomber formations flying to Regensburg and Schweinfurt. The two bomber forces met different opposition. A single loss rate across both conceals how vulnerable particular formations were. | [1943-08-17 · Follow the two bomber forces](../reich-defence.html#double-strike) | [National Museum of the USAF, Regensburg/Schweinfurt, 17 August](https://www.nationalmuseum.af.mil/Visit/Museum-Exhibits/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/1519655/regensburgschweinfurt-august-17-1943/) | Which bomber groups lost formation or missed the target during specific interceptions? |
| [Sicily: Operation Husky](../grand-air-war-v80.html?campaign=sicily#campaign-dossier) | **What difference did Allied fighters make in the battle for Sicily?** Fighters covered the beaches and ships from the first morning, though Axis aircraft still reached the fleet. Inland, fighter and fighter-bomber attacks slowed Axis troops moving by road while the Allies secured their beachheads. Later, fighter-bombers struck the ferries at Messina, but the Axis kept the crossing open. | [1943-07-10 · How much cover did the landing fleet receive?](../sicily.html?case=landing-cover#case-detail) | [USAAF official history, chapter 14, pp. 450–453](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/AAF/II/AAF-II-14.html#page450) | Did shorter flights from captured strips increase the time fighters spent over the same ships or troops? |
| [Kursk: Operation Citadel](../grand-air-war-v80.html?campaign=kursk#campaign-dossier) | **Could air support keep the German offensive moving at Kursk?** German aircraft supported the armoured attacks while Soviet air units contested the battlefield and supported their own counteroffensive. By 12 July fighting around Prokhorovka coincided with a Soviet offensive toward Orel. Local German air support could not secure the wider breakthrough. | [1943-07-12 · Follow the fighting on the two sectors](../grand-air-war-v80.html?campaign=kursk&event=1943-07-12#moment-detail) | [Air & Space, The Air Battle for Kursk · see also the dossier's USAF campaign study](https://www.smithsonianmag.com/air-space-magazine/kursk-180954670/) | Which reported vehicle losses can ground-unit returns attribute to aircraft in either sector? |
| [Schweinfurt–Regensburg raids](../grand-air-war-v80.html?campaign=schweinfurt#campaign-dossier) | **What did the price of reaching Schweinfurt change for the Eighth Air Force?** After American escorts turned back, German fighters repeatedly engaged the B-17s. Sixty bombers were shot down. Further deep daylight raids on this scale had to wait for better escort. Factories suffered damage, but American bomber losses alone say little about the supply of bearings. | [1943-10-14 · Follow Black Thursday](../reich-defence.html#black-thursday) | [National Museum of the USAF, Black Thursday, 14 October](https://www.nationalmuseum.af.mil/Visit/Museum-Exhibits/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/1519661/black-thursday-schweinfurt-october-14-1943/) | How far did bearing stocks and alternative suppliers cushion the interruption at the plants? |
| [Italian campaign air operations](../grand-air-war-v80.html?campaign=italy#campaign-dossier) | **Why did the bombing of Cassino fail to open the town to the Allied advance?** Bombing smashed much of Cassino. Rubble obstructed Allied armour, and German defenders held positions among the ruins. The US Army account follows what the assault troops encountered after the bombers left. | [1944-03-15 · Follow the ground assault after the bombing](../grand-air-war-v80.html?campaign=italy&event=1944-03-15#moment-detail) | [US Army, Salerno to Cassino, chapter XXV, the March attack](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/USA/USA-MTO-Salerno/USA-MTO-Salerno-25.html) | Which routes could the infantry and tanks actually use after the attack, and when did engineers reopen them? |
| [Big Week: Operation Argument](../grand-air-war-v80.html?campaign=bigweek#campaign-dossier) | **What did Big Week change for German factories and fighter defence?** Raids disrupted individual plants and drew German fighters into costly combat with escorted bombers. Germany dispersed production and raised output again in March. Factory damage, aircraft production and the loss of experienced aircrew tell different parts of the story. | [1944-02-24 · Follow Gotha, Steyr and Schweinfurt](../big-week.html#february-24) | [Craven and Cate, vol. III, chapter 2, pp. 38–39](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/AAF/III/AAF-III-2.html) | What do individual factory deliveries and fighter-unit personnel returns show about recovery after the raids? |
| [Normandy: Operation Overlord](../grand-air-war-v80.html?campaign=normandy#campaign-dossier) | **What did the Cobra bombing do for the American breakout?** The bombardment disrupted German defenders west of Saint-Lô and also struck American troops waiting to attack. The breakthrough developed as ground formations exploited the opening. The accounts of troops on both sides help explain why results varied along the front. | [1944-07-25 · Follow Cobra on the ground](../grand-air-war-v80.html?campaign=normandy&event=1944-07-25#moment-detail) | [National Museum of the USAF, Tactical Support · US Army breakout account in the dated episode](https://www.nationalmuseum.af.mil/Visit/Museum-Exhibits/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/196159/tactical-support/) | Where can a named ground unit's report be matched to the time and place of a specific air strike? |
| [Operation Bagration](../grand-air-war-v80.html?campaign=bagration#campaign-dossier) | **How did air operations affect the German withdrawal around Bobruisk?** The Soviet 16th Air Army supported the pincers closing on the German Ninth Army and attacked the retreat. Its history records the effort and its own losses. German monthly records cover only some local units, leaving the enemy cost of that day uncertain. | [1944-06-26 · Read the Bobruisk return](../bagration.html?case=bobruisk#campaign) | [16th Air Army history, chapter 3, Bobruisk operations](https://militera.lib.ru/h/16va/03.html) | Which German ground and air units recorded the effect on the Berezina crossings on 26 June? |
| [Market Garden](../grand-air-war-v80.html?campaign=marketgarden#campaign-dossier) | **Why did aircraft that reached Arnhem still fail to supply the airborne troops?** The RAF Museum records 152 aircraft dropping at their designated zones on 20 September. German troops had overrun the zones during the delay in passing their locations to England. A successful drop at the assigned place could put supplies in enemy hands. | [1944-09-20 · Follow the resupply attempt](../grand-air-war-v80.html?campaign=marketgarden&event=1944-09-20#moment-detail) | [RAF Museum, Operation Market Garden, chapter 4, 20 September](https://www.rafmuseum.org.uk/blog/the-royal-air-force-and-operation-market-garden-chapter-4/) | How much cargo did the airborne troops actually recover from each zone? |
| [Operation Bodenplatte](../grand-air-war-v80.html?campaign=bodenplatte#campaign-dossier) | **How lasting was the damage from the New Year's Day airfield attacks?** At Eindhoven, the Dutch military history institute records 45 aircraft destroyed and 35 damaged. Aircraft caught on the ground made a striking target, but damaged machines, replacement aircraft and lost German pilots followed different paths back to service. | [1945-01-01 · Inspect Eindhoven airfield](../bodenplatte.html?airfield=eindhoven#airfields) | [Netherlands Institute of Military History, Eindhoven airfield, 1 January 1945](https://www.nimh.nl/militaire-geschiedenis-van-nederland/historische-overzichten/vliegvelden-in-oorlogstijd/eindhoven) | When did each affected Allied unit recover its sortie rate, and which German crews returned to duty? |
| [Final air operations over Berlin](../grand-air-war-v80.html?campaign=berlin#campaign-dossier) | **What can the Soviet flying effort tell us about the breakthrough toward Berlin?** The 16th Air Army continued to fly intensively as Soviet ground forces broke through the defences on the approaches to Berlin. Hiestand's daily table records the scale of the commitment. It leaves open how much a particular strike contributed to taking a defended position. | [1945-04-19 · Examine the 16th Air Army's daily return](../grand-air-war-v80.html?campaign=berlin&event=1945-04-19#moment-detail) | [William E. Hiestand, Eastern Front 1945, 16th Air Army daily sortie table](https://www.ospreypublishing.com/us/eastern-front-1945-9781472857842/) | Which army or corps reports identify positions where air attack materially changed the ground fighting? |
