**Next release · v10.2, deployed forces and their influence across all 20 campaigns.** The opening and Channel campaigns, Mediterranean, Eastern Front and western air offensive ship together. ## Where coverage is weakest

The live roster contains **44 dated lists and 297 named memberships**. Four campaigns account for 238 of those memberships (80%). Five campaigns currently show totals only in their selected roster returns. Their specialist studies can already contain named formations and detailed missions. A membership is one unit's appearance in a particular return. One unit can appear on several dates. These counts describe the Atlas and cannot measure the percentage of historical forces deployed. ## When v10.2 is ready to publish

All four regions must meet their completion tests. Publish one before-and-after coverage table spanning all 20 campaigns, broken down by reporting side. Give every campaign a dated case, an identifiable source and an account of what the evidence establishes about its effect. For gaps such as unnamed Dieppe allocations or disputed stations, state the bounded total, unresolved identity or conflicting records as applicable. A regional result can be reviewed as soon as it is ready. The public Atlas advances once, after the four sets of cases, dossier links, maps and filtered exports have been checked together.

## How to measure progress

For each campaign and reporting side in the coverage table, track **formation identities, dates, stations, strength/readiness, effort by role, losses/damage and operational effect**. Show named/expected only when the source enumerates a bounded population at the same echelon, date and geography. Otherwise label the finding as a lower bound, total with unresolved names, conflicting accounts or an open question. Never add a headquarters to its subordinate aircraft total twice.

Start with a dated primary return, diary or log when accessible. Classify official histories, postwar compilations and specialist reconstructions separately. A published transcription can preserve a wartime observation. Record who made it, its locator, and whether an apparent second account cites the same document. Give the reader the earliest supported date for the contribution and the evidence for its effect. A flying unit's report can establish an escort while the ship's record may leave the benefit uncertain.

Each region contributes its dated cases to v10.2. v11.0 draws on those cases where the sources support a comparison.
