## Comparing claims with recorded losses

Use the opponent's own records for the same period and geography, then check the claimant, target formation, airframe class and destruction definition. Keep air and ground claims, probable victories, damage and write-offs separate. A secondary historian's reconciliation can supply useful matched figures when original returns are unavailable. Give each published comparison its source and scope.

The [14 May 1947 Parliamentary table](https://hansard.parliament.uk/Commons/1947-05-14/debates/c2ffba1e-313d-44ea-a198-29b573d9441e/CommonsChamber) gives five periods and seven selected intense days. Display recorded destroyed per 100 contemporary RAF claims, rounded to whole aircraft per 100. The reported period ratios span approximately 51 to 125 per 100. The printed period rows sum to 1,760 destroyed, while the headline states 1,733. Keep both as published. The damaged column has a separate discrepancy and remains distinct from destruction. The intense days are part of the periods. The comparison describes report totals and cannot identify individual claims.

For a check within the September London phase, withhold 15 September. The other listed September days give 40 destroyed for 100 claims (7 September) and 55 for 153 (27 September). Applying their two ratios to 185 claims yields 66–74 after rounding outward. The published 15 September result is 56 destroyed. The Atlas presents this as a test calculation. Even this narrow period needs a larger, more comparable sample before filling a missing historical figure. RAF Museum's 183-claim account uses a different tally from Hansard's 185. The table uses the same published series on both sides.

On 14 October 1943 the [National WWII Museum's Schweinfurt study](https://www.nationalww2museum.org/war/articles/black-thursday-october-14-1943-second-schweinfurt-bombing-raid) reports 186 U.S. bomber-gunner claims and 31 German fighters destroyed, about 17 per 100 claims in that published comparison. It lists 12 additional write-offs and 34 damaged separately. This is a distinct claimant, target, mission and period from RAF fighters during the Battle of Britain.

The [16th Air Army narrative](https://militera.lib.ru/h/16va/03.html), Bobruisk chapter, gives 13 German aircraft claimed and 19 Soviet own losses on 26 June 1944. The two values describe opposite populations. An equivalent German return for the Soviet claim area is still needed. In the other direction, the [German Normandy report](https://www.history.navy.mil/content/history/nhhc/research/library/online-reading-room/title-list-alphabetically/g/gaf-invasion-normandy.html), paragraph 22, gives 1,305 claimed Allied aircraft over the occupied West across day fighters, night fighters and anti-aircraft fire. A matched Allied return for that scope and accounting window is needed. Neither report provides a transferable coefficient for the eastern or western theatre.

## Where a future estimate fits

Use the best available primary or secondary loss record first. In a claim-only case, retain the claim and use a calculated historical range only after validating it on comparable independent dates. Store the claimant and target, period, region, loss category, peer rows, test error and calculation. Display the resulting range as estimated evidence with a link to its inputs. Keep it separate from documented losses when adding totals. For Cobra, the unverified 12 air and 2 ground claims remain inspectable while German mission-scope returns are sought.
