# Aircraft inventories and loss ceilings

## How to select a range

Use the same force, reporting area, aircraft categories and inventory definition at both ends of the period. The closing count is aircraft on hand, including aircraft still held by the unit while unserviceable. Receipts are new deliveries to that force, returns from repair and transfers in. Factory output can supply a receipts figure when delivery to the reporting force during the period is established.

Subtract closing inventory and known departures outside the target loss category from opening inventory plus receipts. The result is an upper bound on the target losses. Use documented accident and other non-enemy removals, overhaul, and transfers out where these aircraft left the reporting inventory. A completed return can reconcile the remaining departures to a recorded loss figure. Directly attributable loss records take precedence over a model.

Intersect the modeled range with the upper bound. If a modeled 600–1,000 losses meets a ceiling of 650, the surviving range is 600–650 and its midpoint is 625. If the model gives only 800, the evidence supports “up to 650” and the model conflicts with the balance. Here 650 comes from 500 on hand plus 500 receipts less 350 on hand at the end. A further 50 documented non-target departures would lower the ceiling to 600. An incomplete receipts series needs an independently defensible upper bound before it can constrain a range.

## Bobruisk, 26 June 1944

The German aircraft movement reports cover the full month of June for III./JG 51 and I./SG 10, based at Bobruisk on 21–27 and 23–27 June respectively. The month sets a ceiling for enemy-action removals on 26 June within these two groups. The final column of the German returns is end-of-month inventory. The I./SG 10 mirror labels that column “Operational,” which would imply a different measure. The existing working estimate of about two enemy-action removals on 26 June uses 29 of 141 reported Soviet air engagements and an assumed 50–100% share of the German groups’ June enemy-action removals in the opening battles. The modeled values span 1.2–2.5, inside the inventory ceiling of 12. The activity series spans 24 June–4 July and is a proxy for the share of June losses. Unit-level June flight and loss dates would allow a better allocation. Enemy-action removals can include several causes. The 13 Soviet air-combat claims cover the wider 16th Air Army activity, so measuring claim accuracy requires establishing which German formations and airframes were involved. ## Cobra and Battle of Britain coverage

Cobra’s Mission 494 estimates cover German aircraft destroyed in air combat by Eighth Air Force escorts. The German calendar reports ten German aircraft lost in a wider area and covers day and night activity. The escort encounters include locations away from the bombing area. A usable ceiling needs complete coverage of those encounters and a reconciled airframe return for the relevant German population. The Battle of Britain campaign comparison uses separately recorded German destruction totals, which already provide the observed outcome at its published scope.
