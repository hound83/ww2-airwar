## Narvik

The RAF associated the continued use of the northern bases with both fighters and anti-aircraft defences. Its account credits interceptions with disrupting the attack of 2 June and records General Auchinleck's response. It also examines weather and the range from German airfields. Raids continued on other days. [Air Ministry, <i>The Campaign in Norway</i>, printed pp. 95–99](https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/our-history/air-historical-branch/second-world-war-campaign-narratives/campaign-in-norway-april-june-1940/).

All 16 printed dates in Table 2 were checked, including the blank combat and loss cells for 4–6 June, the explicit nil entries, and the approximation on 7 June. The [daily transcription](../transcriptions/narvik-daily-1940.json) retains 21 May and 1 June because the squadron diary contains entries even though the area table omits those dates. On 25 and 26 May the squadron lower bounds exceed the corresponding area figures. On 2 June it records 55 sorties against 75 for the area. The diary groups 3–5 June as no flying and 6–7 June as operations without a daily count. The 594 exactly counted area sorties plus about 50 on 7 June do not reconcile to the separate unit summaries of 329 and 249 flights. [Reconstructed No. 263 Squadron Form 540, AIR 27/1547, published transcription, PDF pp. 59–73](https://263squadron.weebly.com/uploads/9/2/1/7/92171046/air271547.pdf#page=59). A matching No. 46 Squadron day book and German or shipboard returns remain open.

## Big Week

The 24 February case follows the Eighth's mission 233, including the 445th Bomb Group, the Fifteenth's Steyr force and the group diverted to Fiume, and the night return for RAF Bomber Command at Schweinfurt. The separate RAF No. 205 Group operation at Steyr is documented in USAF Historical Study 122, printed p. 109. The underlying [Atlas mission records](../transcriptions/operations-january-june-1944.json) are `us8-1944-02-24-233`, `us8-445-1944-02-24`, `us15-1944-02-24` and `raf-5-28827`. The RAF night is indexed by take-off date. [RAF Air Historical Branch, volume V, printed p. 148 and Appendix 10, Schweinfurt row](https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/our-history/air-historical-branch/second-world-war-campaign-narratives/raf-in-the-bombing-offensive-against-germany-vol-v-the-full-offensive-february-1943-to-february-1944/) gives 734 dispatched, 635 attacking and 33 missing. Photographs showed damage at all five ball-bearing plants. The history attributes the observed damage to the combined raids and cannot assign it between the day and night attacks. The Reich daily return includes other engagements. [Official USAAF history, vol. III, chapter 2](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/AAF/III/AAF-III-2.html#page38) · [Combined Bomber Offensive, Study 122](https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/Portals/16/documents/Studies/101-150/AFD-090522-061.pdf).

Six candidate figures were checked against [Wueschner's Air University account](https://www.maxwell.af.mil/News/Display/Article/1754049/operation-argument-big-week-the-beginning-of-the-end-of-the-german-luftwaffe/). The [USAF Museum](https://www.nationalmuseum.af.mil/Visit/Museum-Exhibits/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/1519678/big-week-february-20-25-1944/) counts US heavy-bomber sorties and losses. Subtraction from coalition totals would require comparable inclusion rules. The production study gives 1,555 fighters for January, 1,104 for February and 1,638 for March. It distinguishes actual output from production foregone and discusses additional shifts and dispersal. Personnel readiness needs a separate series. [Case Studies in the Achievement of Air Superiority, figure 6–4 and p. 292](https://media.defense.gov/2010/Oct/12/2001330116/-1/-1/0/AFD-101012-038.pdf).

## Other decisions affecting interpretation

- **Poland.** Kreis's table 1 gives two German serviceability counts. Table 2 describes operational-unit holdings on the Polish side. The two sides' loss rows have separate date and damage definitions. The Polish museum account at Orawka describes a specific mission and cannot establish the enemy column's losses. [Kreis, tables 1–2, pp. 59–60](https://media.defense.gov/2025/Jun/10/2003736254/-1/-1/0/AIR%20WARFARE%20%26%20AB%20DEFENSE.PDF) · [Polish Aviation Museum](https://muzeumlotnictwa.pl/en/aviation-traces/aviation-disasters/g-39-orawka/).
- **Spain, Norway and France.** Approximate Soviet deliveries to Spain span the war. The Norway estimates of 242 and 260 describe different accounts with an unresolved common scope. Murray's France chapter explicitly reports RAF fighter losses of 474 and lists German all-type losses separately. The German damaged count has its own table. [Case Studies, Spanish assistance and France chapter, table 2–1 and note 85](https://media.defense.gov/2010/Oct/12/2001330116/-1/-1/0/AFD-101012-038.pdf) · [Deutsches Historisches Museum, Norway](https://www.dhm.de/lemo/kapitel/der-zweite-weltkrieg/kriegsverlauf/besetzung-von-norwegen-1940) · [Gray, p. 128](https://www.airuniversity.af.edu/Portals/10/AUPress/Books/B_0122_GRAY_AIRPOWER_STRATEGIC_EFFECT.PDF).
- **Britain and Malta.** The 31 British air-combat losses on 18 August are included in 68 RAF and Fleet Air Arm losses. The RAF Museum's late-August period belongs to a different window. The proposed all-Axis label for 2,400 October sorties at Malta remains unresolved against the specialist study's German reporting scope. The released Spotter case already identifies HMS <i>Eagle</i> and preserves the differing delivery counts. [Hardest Day](https://www.rafmuseum.org.uk/our-collection/supporting-research/online-exhibitions/the-hardest-day/) · [Phase Three](https://www.rafmuseum.org.uk/research/online-exhibitions/history-of-the-battle-of-britain/the-battle-of-britain-phase-three) · [Malta source audit](atlas-malta-air-campaign-2026-11-13.md).
- **Arnhem and Stalingrad.** A supply aircraft might reach its assigned drop zone while its cargo went to the enemy. The 23 transport losses on 21 September have a different day and population from campaign totals. At Stalingrad, the account specifies combat causes for the Ju 52 and He 111 losses and mentions accidents separately. [RAF Museum, chapter 4](https://www.rafmuseum.org.uk/blog/the-royal-air-force-and-operation-market-garden-chapter-4/) · [RAF Museum, chapter 6](https://www.rafmuseum.org.uk/blog/the-royal-air-force-and-operation-market-garden-chapter-6/) · [Stalingrad airlift](https://www.airandspaceforces.com/article/turning-point-at-stalingrad/).
- **Dieppe, Schweinfurt and the Reich.** The previously published studies already preserve the conflicting Dieppe totals and the 31/12/34 German categories at Schweinfurt. The Eighth's March write-offs and the Luftflotte Reich monthly return cannot be assigned to an individual raid. [Dieppe study](../../dieppe.html#losses) · [Black Thursday](../../reich-defence.html#black-thursday) · [Davis, March 1944](https://media.defense.gov/2025/Jun/10/2003736234/-1/-1/0/DAVIS_BOMBING_EUROPEAN.PDF).
