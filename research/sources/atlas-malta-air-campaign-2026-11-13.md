# Siege and air battle of Malta · source audit

The [Malta explorer](../../malta.html) covers 16 selected cases from 11 June 1940 to 20 November 1942. It follows fighter deliveries, damage and recovery at the airfields, and supply at sea. The [machine-readable ledger](../transcriptions/malta-air-campaign-1940-1942.json) gives each figure a source, locator and reporting scope. Map pins identify the case's reference location. For offshore operations the location can be the convoy assembly point, receiving port or a documented incident within the operation.

## Air operations and reporting scope

- The [Italian Air Staff memorandum, 10 May 1942](https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/our-history/air-historical-branch/ahb-translations-from-captured-german-documents/memorandum-on-the-malta-situation-10-may-1942/) gives the German monthly sortie series in Appendix B. The January role counts sum to 1,993 against a printed 1,973. February's 2,497 differs from the RAF chronology's 2,447. In April the role counts sum to 9,599 and agree with the RAF total. Appendix C covers 865 Italian sorties for 20 March–10 May. Appendix A's 98 on strength and 69 serviceable refer to the German single-engine fighter row in Sicily on 10 May. The 87 aircraft in §14 are the Italian assessment of aircraft visible on Malta that day.
- The RAF April chronology separates 44 aircraft destroyed on the ground, 82 damaged there and 20 fighters destroyed in combat. Its 43 German aircraft lost in action and the [RAF Museum's 45 reported losses](https://www.rafmuseum.org.uk/research/research-enquiries/history-of-aviation-timeline/british-military-aviation/1942-2/) have different wording and potentially different coverage. The Luqa and Hal Far entries cover specific dates. Hal Far's 11 April report gives one combined destroyed-or-badly-damaged figure.
- The RAF chronology for 11–19 October attributes about 2,400 sorties to the Luftwaffe. The RAF Museum describes the same headline figure as Axis sorties. The [Italian Air Staff's 31 October assessment](https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/our-history/air-historical-branch/ahb-translations-from-captured-german-documents/the-air-and-naval-bases-in-malta-june-1940-to-october-1942/), sixth period, reports 168 aircraft visible at the beginning and 113 on 20 October. Photo counts can change with concealment, replacement and aircraft movements. The RAF chronology records 46 serviceable Spitfires and 125 defensive sorties on 16 October. These are an availability count and a count of flights, respectively.

## Convoys and claims

- [Hague's June convoy chronology](https://www.naval-history.net/xAH-MaltaSupply01b.htm) gives Harpoon 2 of 6 merchant ships arrived and Vigorous 0 of 11. The Harpoon merchant total excludes HMS Welshman's separate cargo run. The Vigorous dispatch count excludes HMS Centurion. The [Italian Navy's June account](https://www.marina.difesa.it/media-cultura/Notiziario-online/Pagine/20210615_la_battaglia_di_pantelleria_una_storica_vittoria_italiana.aspx) and [RAF in Maritime War, vol. VI](https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/our-history/air-historical-branch/second-world-war-campaign-narratives/raf-in-maritime-war-vol-vi-the-mediterranean-and-the-red-sea/) provide the opposing fleet and air-cover context.
- [Hague's August and November convoy chronology](https://www.naval-history.net/xAH-MaltaSupply03.htm) gives 5 of 14 merchant ships through for Pedestal and all 4 through for Stoneage. The Mediterranean Fleet diary for 16 August lists fleet fighter claims of 39 certain and 5 probable and Malta aircraft claims of 10 certain and 2 probable. It separately lists anti-aircraft claims of 17 certain and 10 probable. These are British claims. The Malta Command August summary gives approximately 32,000 tons general cargo, 12,000 tons furnace fuel and 3,600 tons diesel, in the diary's units. Its November summary dates the easing of the siege to 26 November, after unloading. The diary calls the convoy M.W. 12 in that summary, whereas Hague identifies the same four named ships under M.W. 13.
- The [Italian Navy's August account](https://www.marina.difesa.it/media-cultura/Notiziario-online/Pagine/20220812_battaglia_di_mezzo_agosto_1942.aspx) and [Francesco Mattesini's Pedestal study](https://www.societaitalianastoriamilitare.org/COLLANA%20SISM/MATTESINI%20L%27operazione%20Pedestal.%20Mezzo%20Agosto%201942.pdf) give Axis operational context. Matching claimed aircraft to German and Italian unit loss records remains open.

## Reference trail and remaining evidence

The [Siege of Malta](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Siege_of_Malta_(World_War_II)), [Club Run](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Club_Run) and [Operation Pedestal](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Pedestal) entries were used to navigate operation names and references. The published figures cite the independent museum, official histories, diaries and operational studies listed above.

Further work would require dated RAF operations record books for receiving squadrons and serviceability returns, the original carrier returns for Spotter and Bellows, and date-and-unit-level Luftwaffe and Regia Aeronautica loss and claim records. The current cases support comparisons within their documented populations. Missing or differently scoped observations remain visible in the explorer.
