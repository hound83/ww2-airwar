# Kursk and Bagration formation rosters

## Kursk

The Soviet General Staff's monthly battle-composition table records the air armies on 1 July 1943. The 16th Air Army had 12 aviation divisions on the Central Front. The 2nd Air Army had 11 on the Voronezh Front. The 17th Air Army on the adjacent Southwestern Front had nine. The extracts retain corps-level parentage where the table supplies it. Separate regiments, squadrons and ground establishments fall outside these divisional counts. The source gives no station for these division rows. [General Staff, *Combat Composition of the Soviet Army*, part III, 1 July tables](https://www.soldat.ru/files/f/boevoj_sostav_sa_1943.pdf) · [Searchable July transcription](https://rkka.wiki/index.php/РККА:Боевой_состав_на_01.07.1943)

A wartime order on 5 July by the Central Front commander addressed the commanders of the 2nd Tank Army and 16th Air Army about a counterattack. In the south, Kozhevnikov identifies the 2nd and 17th Air Armies as contributors to the 5 July airfield strikes. The 1 July rosters supply the parent commands. They do not identify which of their regiments flew that morning. [Central Front order 00366/op, 5 July](https://docs.historyrussia.org/ru/nodes/189939-boevoe-rasporyazhenie-komanduyuschego-voyskami-tsentralnogo-fronta-locale-nil-00366-op-komanduyuschim-2-y-tankovoy-i-16-y-vozdushnoy-armiyami-na-nanesenie-kontrudara-5-iyulya-1943-g) · [Kozhevnikov, chapter 5](https://militera.lib.ru/h/kozhevnikov_mn/05.html)

Newton's 1 July Luftflotte 4 appendix labels VIII. Fliegerkorps as supporting Citadel. It names five numbered medium-bomber Gruppen, six dive-bomber Gruppen and two assault Gruppen under the corps. KG 100 appears without a Gruppe number. The same appendix gives a separate Hungarian 2nd Air Brigade under this corps, with two fighter, two reconnaissance, one bomber and one dive-bomber squadron. The Kharkov area is a brigade-level location. The Slovak, Croatian and Romanian units under the preceding I. Fliegerkorps heading belong to a different command section. The 5 July German day-fighter return in the original benchmark remains separately dated. [Newton, ed., *Kursk: The German View*, Appendix 6A, pp. 195–199](https://www.39-45.org/files3945c/4510_Newton_-_Kursk_-_The_German_View.pdf) · [Frieser, German opening dispositions](https://ebrary.net/116699/political_science/german_potential_kursk_salient)

## Bagration

The original five-air-army allocation identifies the commands supporting the four fronts during the offensive. The 1 July 1944 General Staff table identifies 52 divisions in those five commands and puts the Polish army's two aviation regiments in a separate row. The French Normandie regiment served under the 303rd Fighter Aviation Division. Its unit journal documents a 23 June escort mission. The 1st Belorussian Front's two air armies operated on different wings and timelines. [General Staff, *Combat Composition*, part IV, 1 July tables](https://www.soldat.ru/files/f/boevoj_sostav_sa_1944.pdf) · [Searchable July transcription](https://rkka.wiki/index.php/РККА:Боевой_состав_на_01.07.1944) · [US Army study of Bagration](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-D110-PURL-LPS68395/pdf/GOVPUB-D110-PURL-LPS68395.pdf) · [Normandie wartime journal, p. 56](https://bibert.fr/Joseph_Bibert_fichiers/Gabriel%20MERTZISEN_fichiers/Normandie%20Niemen%20JM.pdf)

On 27 June the 16th Air Army ordered the 283rd, 286th and 1st Guards Fighter Aviation Divisions to patrol the perimeter of the Bobruisk pocket and cover the aircraft striking inside it. The army history reports 134 fighters in the evening operation and gives no division-level breakdown. Earlier that day, aircraft from the 569th Assault Aviation Regiment attacked vehicles moving toward Titovka, and nine Pe-2s of the 24th Bomber Aviation Regiment attacked the Berezina railway bridge. These two named examples give a lower bound for daytime regimental participation. The damage assessment comes from the Soviet account. [Prussakov et al., *16th Air Army*, chapter 3, pp. 170–174, including the order reproduced at note 14](https://militera.lib.ru/h/16va/03.html)

The 1 July return records the Polish 1st Fighter Regiment and 2nd Night Bomber Regiment as assigned to the Polish 1st Army. A Polish retrospective dates their entry into combat to 23 August and 20 August respectively in the Warsaw area. The July rows establish assignment only. [Polish account, section on the air force of the Polish Army](https://pbc.gda.pl/Content/90944/Nr_18_19.pdf)

The 26 June Luftflotte 6 table identifies four numbered day-fighter Gruppen under Jagdabschnittführer Nr. 6, five numbered assault Gruppen under two Fliegerdivisionen, and ten numbered bomber Gruppen under IV. Fliegerkorps. These counts apply to the specified sections. Smaller detachments, reconnaissance, night fighters and staffs also appear elsewhere on the page. The bare figures in the transcription carry no clear readiness definition. The 12./NAGr. 12 reconnaissance Staffel at Mogilev appears under Fliegerführer Nr. 1, whose headquarters was at Minsk. [Eastern Front order of battle, 26 June 1944](https://www.lexikon-der-wehrmacht.de/OrdersofBattle/Juni1944-R.htm)

Three station checks qualify the fighter roster. Holm places IV./JG 51 at Mogilev from 22 to 27 June and III./JG 11 at Dokudovo from 24 June to 3 July, in agreement with the 26 June table. For III./JG 11 the table gives Bf 109s while Holm gives Fw 190As. The table gives Orsha for I./JG 51 while Holm has it moving through Dokudowo to Bojary by 26 June. The absence of a number for IV./JG 51 and III./JG 11 in the order of battle cannot be read as zero serviceable aircraft. [Holm, JG 51, I., III. and IV. Gruppe station histories](https://www.ww2.dk/air/jagd/jg51.htm) · [Holm, JG 11, III. Gruppe station history](https://www.ww2.dk/air/jagd/jg11.htm)
