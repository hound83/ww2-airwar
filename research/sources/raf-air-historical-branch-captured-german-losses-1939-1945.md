# RAF Air Historical Branch: captured German quantitative records, 1938–1945

**Status:** archival source inventory, not imported aircraft incidents. Identified in the RAF Air Historical Branch's official [Translations from Captured German Documents](https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/our-history/air-historical-branch/ahb-german-translations/) catalogue (entries carry the original AHB translation number). The RAF prepared these English translations after the war from original German material; they are **translated primary-source evidence**, not independent RAF assessments of enemy losses.

The translated series comprises **20 theatre/date-specific loss reports** plus a separate **aircraft strength/serviceability series**. Some major 1942 and early-1943 loss returns are absent from this *particular* online series; this cannot support uninterrupted monthly German losses or an assumption that zero records means zero losses. Dates below are the claimed scope of the translated documents, not verified coverage of each loss table inside.

## German strength and serviceability (particularly valuable)

**AHB 107**, [*Luftwaffe strength and serviceability tables August 1938–April 1945*](https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/our-history/air-historical-branch/ahb-translations-from-captured-german-documents/luftwaffe-strength-and-serviceability-tables-august-1938-april-1945/), 44-page PDF, compiled from German Air Ministry **Quartermaster General Department, VI Abteilung** returns. It is a long dated time series of **on-hand aircraft and serviceable aircraft**, by close/long reconnaissance, single/twin-engine fighters, later night fighters and fighter-bombers, bombers, dive bombers, ground attack, coastal and transport. The printed strength rows distinguish **total excluding transport** and **total including transport**. Between-period service categories changed, and there is at least one important OCR/reproduction issue: the printed table for **November–December 1943** contains a header OCR misreading as 1945; it belongs to 1943 from sequence, not 1945. Another late-1944 total is suspect and needs original-image checking before inclusion. A reproducible *selective verified transcription* should preserve original scan-page number, printed month/day, on-hand and serviceable, and avoid projecting this **whole Luftwaffe**, not Europe-subtheater-specific, strength onto one map front.

**Critical handling instructions:** AHB 83/92 give a different whole-service national scope from the 1943–44 theatre records. Do not add a theatre record to an overlapping national total or conflate operations lost/damaged/captured. These captured German **own-loss** reports are the appropriate independent check against **RAF/USAAF/Soviet claimed enemy aircraft destroyed**, but matching dates, geography, damage threshold and later amendments is necessary. The primary German Generalquartiermeister [Bundesarchiv RL 2-III](https://invenio.bundesarchiv.de/) unit-loss records, 80 digitized volumes, provide the stronger future plane-and-crew-fate source; this RAF English translation collection can be digitized more quickly as a complementary cross-check. No incident counts have been represented as fully transcribed merely because the PDF is linked here.
