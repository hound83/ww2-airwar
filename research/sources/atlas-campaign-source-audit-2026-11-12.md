# Campaign source and data audit, 12 November 2026

The Atlas had 20 campaign dossiers, 135 overview observations, 61 campaign chapters and 119 chronology entries at the start of the audit. The v9 evidence already included the Battle of Britain week and phase comparisons and two reporting directions for Normandy on 1–18 July. This pass adds **343 dated, period, formation or airframe rows** in **66 series** across all 20 dossiers. The rows contain **1,059 numeric fields** from **41 named source records**. Some series contain components and totals or a second account of the same episode. The counts describe records added to the research dataset.

## Reference recheck

The reference ledger covers 143 source IDs across the main campaign registry and the v8.6, v8.7, v8.8 and Battle of Britain v9 research files. It records 132 distinct public URLs after grouping links to the same document and one internal route. The renewed direct check opened 81 public links, encountered four redirects and returned 47 retrieval failures. Direct access indicates that the linked document or page was reachable. Indexed excerpts, archived editions, catalog entries and partially readable scans are identified in the follow-up column. A retrieval failure can mean a size limit, timeout, access block or changed URL.

Alternate copies recovered the full MAAF report, the U.S. chronology, the Army's Bagration study, Kreis's official history, Murray's study and the RAF AHB Cobra narrative. The original source registries include RAF Museum timeline links that redirected or remained inaccessible. The National Museum of the U.S. Air Force and NHHC pages often had indexed entries when direct retrieval failed. The alternate German I./SG 10 transcription was readable. The original Mission 494 listing identified the report, including field orders 913 and 917. Its fighter annex remains an unresolved access gap.

The audit used the published v9 evidence and the proposed roadmap's wider campaign, unit and operational-balance priorities. The German 30 command-month rows, USAAF monthly loss and claims counts and the 68 USAAF factory-acceptance months already in the Atlas informed the search. Earlier source ledgers sometimes still describe numerical transcription as pending. The established legacy rows remain the reference for those covered months.

## Reconciliations that affect later use

- **Kursk, 2nd Air Army.** Populated daily rows in Table 3.1 sum to 10,197 day sorties, 2,552 night sorties and 433 losses. The printed totals are 10,336, 2,562 and 437. On 9 July, the two armies' night counts are 60 and 174 while the combined cell reads 77. Table 3.3 has five usable balanced formation lines. The printed aggregate movement yields 1,162 aircraft against a reported closing stock of 1,154. Table 2.5 has 73 aircraft entries in four regiments, including forced landings with unknown final disposition. The 286th Division's separate July accounting line reports 71 losses. [Gorbach, Tables 2.1–3.3](https://history.wikireading.ru/amp135360)
- **Italy, MAAF.** The p. 13 table gives 365 own losses for Strangle and 438 for 12 May–22 June. The nearby prose uses 803, exactly their sum, beside the later period's 72,946 sorties. The dataset uses the two table rows. [Wartime HQ MAAF report](https://znaci.org/00002/315.pdf#page=13)
- **Cobra, 25 July.** The RAF AHB narrative gives 480 escorts, six heavy bombers and two fighters attributed to flak. The American chronology gives 483 escorts, five heavy bombers lost and two B-24s damaged beyond repair. The Luftflotte 3 calendar's 22 claims and ten own losses cover the wider command area. AHB VII/144 uses cause columns, but the inverted 25 July page could not be read reliably in the available OCR. The original American fighter annex still needs direct reading. The German airframe candidates in the v8.9.1 note cannot yet be attributed to the escort encounters. [AHB Cobra narrative, p. 64](https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/our-history/air-historical-branch/second-world-war-campaign-narratives/liberation-of-north-west-europe-vol-iv-the-breakout-and-advance-to-the-lower-rhine-12-jun-30-sep-1944/) · [Original mission report catalogue](https://cgsc.contentdm.oclc.org/digital/collection/p4013coll8/id/4574/) · [German cause return](https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/our-history/air-historical-branch/ahb-translations-from-captured-german-documents/luftwaffe-losses-on-the-western-front-luftflotte-3-1st-july-31st-august-1944/#page=22)
- **Bobruisk.** The existing ceiling is 12 enemy-action removals across III./JG 51 and I./SG 10 over June. I./JG 51 and IV./JG 51 account for 14 and five enemy-action removals in their own June movements. These units have separate geographical scopes and full-month balances. II./JG 51's Balkan basing gives another reason to check unit locations before assigning a return to Bagration. The 27 June mass strike observation covers a single episode. [I./JG 51 return](https://www.ww2.dk/oob/bestand/jagd/bijg51.html) · [IV./JG 51 return](https://www.ww2.dk/oob/bestand/jagd/bivjg51.html) · [16th Air Army history](https://militera.lib.ru/h/16va/03.html)
- **Early fighter losses.** The AHB West May 1940 single-engine fighter damage cells give 24 from enemy action and 67 from other operational causes. The source's printed operational subtotal is 88. The raw cells are retained and the inconsistent subtotal is omitted. The Polish campaign's German loss-by-role table has its own VII/33 source and dates. [RAF AHB VII/83](https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/our-history/air-historical-branch/ahb-translations-from-captured-german-documents/german-aircraft-losses-september-1939-december-1940/) · [Kreis, Poland tables](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-D301-PURL-gpo113228/pdf/GOVPUB-D301-PURL-gpo113228.pdf)
- **Market Garden and Berlin.** The 49 Air Despatch Group's 163 takeoffs on 20 September count a different event from the RAF Museum's 162 aircraft attempting drops. At Berlin the 16th Air Army reports 357 own aircraft lost and 791 enemy aircraft claims internally confirmed. Confirmation here refers to the Soviet accounting process. The same HQ report's 5,342 sorties on 16 April differs by ten from the sum of its role cells. [49 Air Despatch Group diary](https://www.pegasusarchive.org/arnhem/war_49adg.htm) · [Berlin documentary edition, no. 143](https://militera.lib.ru/docs/da/berlin_45/07.html)

## Archive leads with the largest remaining gaps

1. Open the original Cobra report at the [direct CARL file](https://cgsc.contentdm.oclc.org/digital/api/collection/p4013coll8/id/4574/download) and locate the fighter annex for both 492 and 494. Match mission, airframe, time, place and cause after transcribing the relevant German images. The [Bundesarchiv RL 2-III guide](https://www.bundesarchiv.de/im-archiv-recherchieren/archivgut-recherchieren/nach-themen/das-schicksal-abgestuerzter-deutscher-flugzeuge-und-deren-besatzungen-im-zweiten-weltkrieg/) describes substantial 1944 loss-list gaps. Use unit and formation records, surviving staff records and regional airframe registers for cases those gaps affect.
2. Obtain the original 2nd Air Army Kursk returns and North-Western Front Barbarossa return pages. The exact TsAMO locators above and in the dataset identify the underlying files. Use the [Russian historical document catalogue](https://docs.historyrussia.org/ru/nodes/151966-iz-otcheta-o-boevoy-rabote-vvs-severo-zapadnogo-fronta-22-iyunya-locale-nil-22-dekabrya-1941-g) for the Northwestern Front. The existing Bagration and Berlin findings identify the reporting commands for parallel checks.
3. Use [RAF AHB campaign narratives](https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/our-history/air-historical-branch/second-world-war-campaign-narratives1/), [UK Operations Record Books](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/royal-air-force-operations-record-books-1911-1963/), the [Admiralty diary catalogue](https://www.royalnavy.mod.uk/locations-and-operations/bases-and-stations/naval-historic-branch), and the [Italian Air Force archive guide](https://www.difesa.it/assets/allegati/43022/guida_ai_fondi_am.pdf) for Malta, Sicily and Italy. Match ground losses, permanent losses and temporary unserviceability to the original unit terms.
4. In the Low Countries, build dated case lists from [NIMH collection 409](https://www.nimh.nl/actueel/nieuws/2025/05/23/index), the [Belgian War Heritage Institute](https://warheritage.be/) and the [SGLO airframe and glider registers](https://www.verliesregister.studiegroepluchtoorlog.nl/). A regional crash can be tied to a local action only when the service, unit and mission evidence supports it.

Each JSON series supplies its source, location in the source, country, reporting force, theater, reporting geography, date window, measurement definition, status and confidence. It is a research transcription for later import. The existing claim-loss model uses the already validated v9 inputs and can adopt a new row once the required date, cause, force and geographic match is established.
