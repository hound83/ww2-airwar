# Bagration air operations and evidence audit

## Scope

The campaign follows the opening reconnaissance-in-force on 22 June, the full offensives from 23–24 June, and the strategic accounting window through 29 August 1944. The operation dates vary by definition. For the Soviet equipment total use the 23 June–29 August dates printed by Krivosheev. Case markers locate towns and operational sectors. Named station rows preserve the German place as printed. A unit’s station is evidence of presence. Flight or loss attribution requires a mission or loss record.

The [machine-readable campaign evidence](../transcriptions/bagration-air-campaign-1944.json) records date, unit, measure, scope and locator. The separate case date is the narrative window. A case may also contain supporting evidence from a wider date or geography. Before this milestone the public dossier had three broad chapters and the Bobruisk calculator. The older bilateral PR #20 used the same 16th Air Army source and opening comparison already represented on main. The 16th Air Army and inventory findings are reused from the [existing archive ledger](atlas-german-soviet-original-operational-ledgers-2026-11-10.md) and the [Bobruisk inventory audit](atlas-aircraft-inventory-loss-ceilings-2026-11-11.md).

## Wikipedia reference review

The table records the passages screened in this pass. A numbered note can repeat the same work. Titles concerning land operations, occupation, partisan operations and casualties were screened for aviation relevance. Follow-up concentrated on citations that could support chronology, aviation activity, air strength or losses. The key inaccessible editions or pages are recorded below. The [earlier repository sweep](wikipedia-source-coverage-2026-11-12.json) also records the English and Russian campaign articles and the unit pages. The Belarusian campaign page did not render in this pass. Kaunas and Belostok article summaries were consulted, but their citation tails did not render on the follow-up. Their operational context is supported separately by Dyakov and Ziemke.

## Additional source work

- The German monthly movement returns contain opening and closing aircraft on hand, new production receipts, repair receipts, transfers and separate departure categories. Each of the ten displayed rows reconciles. Holm’s station histories put III./JG 51 at Bobruisk, Machulishchy, Minsk-Süd, Pinsk and then Kaunas across the opening weeks. Tessin and Holm both place Luftflotte 6 headquarters in the Minsk area before the move to Warsaw, with different date precision. [Tessin](https://www.bundesarchiv.de/assets/bundesarchiv/de/Downloads/Tessin/Bd_14_ocr.pdf) · [Holm, Luftflotte 6](https://www.ww2.dk/air/hq/lfl6.htm) · [Holm, JG 51](https://www.ww2.dk/air/jagd/jg51.htm) · [Holm, SG 10](https://www.ww2.dk/air/attack/sg10.htm)
- Henry deZeng’s airfield gazetteer identifies several strips around Bobruisk. The operation map uses a sector marker because unit listings often use only the city name. French ministry and memorial material corroborate the unit’s Soviet deployment. The 15 July loss was a transfer accident. [Gazetteer](https://ww2.dk/Airfields%20-%20Russia%20and%20Ukraine.pdf) · [French unit journal](https://bibert.fr/Joseph_Bibert_fichiers/Gabriel%20MERTZISEN_fichiers/Normandie%20Niemen%20JM.pdf) · [Memorial](https://memorial-normandie-niemen.fr/deuxieme-campagne/)
- The German capture-document register at RAF AHB lists an Eastern Front loss translation for September–December 1943. It supplies no equivalent 1944 Bagration daily return on the cited index. The German-Russian OKL collection and the Bundesarchiv catalogue remain leads. Further priority is the day-by-day Luftflotte 6 or division reports, TsAMO 290/3284/796 for the 1st Air Army and TsAMO 46/2394/727 for the 50th Army air-support assessment. Christer Bergström’s *Bagration to Berlin* and Rudenko’s 1973 *Aerospace Historian* article are bibliographic follow-up. The specialist pages needed for independent loss reconciliation were not accessible in this pass.
