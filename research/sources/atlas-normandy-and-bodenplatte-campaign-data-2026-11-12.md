# Normandy 1944 and Operation Bodenplatte — bilateral campaign data (12 November 2026)

Same style of extraction as the Battle of Britain notes, covering both Allied (USAAF / RAF 2nd TAF) and German sides for the two campaigns already in the Atlas: the Western air war / Normandy 1944 (including Cobra) and Operation Bodenplatte (1 January 1945).

## Operation Bodenplatte (1 January 1945)

### Scale and order of battle
- German force: ~850–900 aircraft (mostly Bf 109 and Fw 190, plus a few Me 262 pathfinders), attacking ~16–17 Allied airfields in Belgium, Netherlands and France.
- Allied forces present: RAF 2nd TAF (Typhoon, Tempest, Spitfire wings) and US Ninth Air Force (P-47, P-51 groups) on the targeted fields.

### Losses (bilateral)
**German (Manrho & Pütz / specialist consensus)**
- 271 fighters destroyed, 65 single-engine damaged; 9 twin-engine destroyed, 4 damaged.
- 143 pilots killed/missing, 70 captured, 21 wounded (including 3 Geschwaderkommodore, 5 Gruppenkommandeure, 14 Staffelkapitäne).
- Friendly-fire component: modern studies put certain German Flak losses at ~17–35 aircraft (the “one-third friendly fire” figure is treated as myth by Manrho & Pütz).

**Allied**
- Official RAF/USAAF reports: ~173 destroyed + 137 damaged on the ground (likely undercount, excludes some 8th AF).
- Manrho & Pütz consensus: 305 destroyed + 190 damaged (almost all on the ground); plus ~15 shot down and 10 damaged in the air.
- Allied pilot losses were low (handful killed); aircraft were replaced within days/weeks.

### Narrative for Atlas
Tactical surprise was achieved, but the operation was a strategic disaster for the Luftwaffe: irreplaceable experienced pilots and leaders were lost, while Allied material losses were quickly made good. Galland: “We sacrificed our last substance.” The existing Atlas bilateral ledger for Eindhoven, Asch, Evere, Melsbroek and Metz-Frescaty can use the Manrho & Pütz totals as the current best specialist reference.

## Western air war / Normandy 1944 (Luftflotte 3 vs Allied air forces)

### Opening strength (early June)
- Luftflotte 3 total fighting strength on 5 June: 481 aircraft (including ~100 fighters, 64 reconnaissance). Operational efficiency ~40%.
- On 6 June, German records put Allied strength at ~2,100 four-engined, 600 two-engined and 3,500–4,000 single-engined aircraft. Luftflotte 3 could counter with ~319 aircraft (relative strength ~20:1).
- One secondary order-of-battle snapshot (early June) lists Luftflotte 3 at 891 strength / 496 serviceable across all roles.

### Sorties and losses (June–July)
- 6 June – 1 July: Luftflotte 3 and elements of Jagdkorps I flew ~13,983 sorties (3,568 bombers; 9,151 day fighters/fighter-bombers/recce; 1,101 night fighters; 153 supply).
- German claims in the same window: 414 by day fighters, 219 by night fighters, 672 by AA → 1,305 Allied aircraft claimed.
- German own losses in the same window: 458 fighters/fighter-bombers/recce, 224 bombers, 7 supply, 137 destroyed on the ground → ~826 aircraft.
- July: ~15,545 German sorties (10,728 fighter); 524 German aircraft lost (including ground). Allied sorties in the broader period cited at ~96,000.
- Reinforcements: 6 June – 7 July, Luftflotte 3 received 1,105 aircraft (998 fighters, 83 bombers, 24 recce).

### Operation Cobra (24/25 July 1944)
- US 8th Air Force: 1,503 of 1,581 B-17/B-24s bombed; total air effort (heavies + mediums + fighter-bombers) >4,100 tons.
- Friendly-fire short bombs: 111 US killed (including Lt Gen Leslie McNair), 490 wounded.
- Existing Atlas notes already hold the Luftflotte 3 cause-of-loss return for 25 July and the Mission 494 escort attribution issues.

### Narrative for Atlas
Luftflotte 3 was heavily outnumbered from D-Day onward but maintained a sortie rate through continuous reinforcement. AA accounted for a large share of Allied losses in the period. The existing June/July command-return audits and the Cobra calculator can be reviewed against these German-side strength and claim figures.

These bilateral figures are secondary or tertiary but provide usable campaign-scale denominators and narrative anchors for the two remaining Atlas milestones. Unit-level or airfield-level ledgers (especially for Bodenplatte) still require the specialist sources already flagged (Manrho & Pütz, Davis workbooks, Frappé, CAMO).
