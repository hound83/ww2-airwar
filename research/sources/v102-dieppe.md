# Dieppe air forces, 19 August 1942

Ross Mahoney's order of battle identifies all **74 squadron allocations** in nine categories. Each row specifies one squadron and its primary task. No. 129 Squadron, for example, appears under air cover and also flew in support of No. 4 Commando. Counting it again under close support would change the historical return. The RAF separately identifies Nos. 59 and 502 Squadrons on overnight Coastal Command flank searches. Those patrols explain why the 74 cannot stand for every squadron that contributed. The 74 names come from a published reconstruction that cites AIR 20/5186 Appendix A and Norman Franks. Each membership gives the page, assigned base and sector or group. Mahoney calls the 307th, 308th and 309th “Eagle.” They were USAAF 31st Fighter Group squadrons under RAF operational control. The RAF's own Eagle squadrons were Nos. 71, 121 and 133. The US 308th operated from Kenley. The Polish No. 308 operated from Heston. [Mahoney, Appendix 1](https://etheses.bham.ac.uk/id/eprint/445/1/Mahoney09MPhil.pdf#page=171) · [31st Fighter Group history, August 1942](https://www.wwiiaircraftperformance.org/31st/31st.html)

For No. 414 RCAF, the allocation table gives Gatwick and the Canadian operational map shows Croydon. Pilot Hollis Hills remembered Croydon as the home station and recorded that he and his leader separately landed at Gatwick after their first reconnaissance. His account also describes the squadron's return to Croydon that day. Both locations appear in the membership with their different functions. ## German participation and attribution

JG 2 and JG 26 each committed three fighter Gruppen. The two dedicated fighter-bomber Staffeln attacked shipping, and surviving crew evidence identifies at least three bomber Staffeln. British wireless intelligence distinguished a minimum of 86 individual long-range bombers. This count cannot supply the number of bomber units in the operation. For 5./KG 2, the description of Franz Mai's Soldbuch gives his Staffel. A separate aircraft-loss entry identifies Mai, his pilot Helmut Kroll, the flight from Eindhoven and the lost Dornier, serial 4292. [Fighter deployments](https://erenow.org/ww/aces-luftwaffe-jagdfliegern-their-tactics-wwii/11.php) · [Goss, pp. 103–104](https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/centre-for-air-and-space-power-studies/aspr/apr-vol4-iss4-5-pdf/) · [KG 40 crew records, 19 August](https://www.airmen.dk/pdfs/p114v3reichwest.pdf) · [Air Ministry intelligence narrative, paragraph 60](https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/our-history/air-historical-branch/second-world-war-thematic-studies/air-ministry-intelligence-narrative/) · [Franz Mai's Soldbuch](https://crainsmilitaria.com/index.php?product_id=7578&route=product/product) · [Dornier U5+YN, aircraft and crew record](https://www.baaa-acro.com/index.php/crash/crash-dornier-do217e-4-dieppe-4-killed)

The clearest matches between air and surface accounts concern the 06:20 attack at Varengeville and the subsequent withdrawal at Dieppe. At Varengeville, the earlier 06:07 explosion generated conflicting reports. During the withdrawal, the naval commander's assessment applied to No. 11 Group as a whole. His despatch and the Admiralty diary establish the damage to the shipping. A separate squadron observation places one bomber's release about a mile from the returning convoy. [Canadian Army history, pp. 362–363 and 383–385](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/UN/Canada/CA/SixYears/SixYears-11.html) · [Naval force commander's despatch, paragraph 4](https://www.thegazette.co.uk/London/issue/38045/supplement/3823/data.pdf) · [Admiralty diary, 19 August](https://cd.royalnavy.mod.uk/-/media/rnweb/locations-and-operations/navy-historical-branch/pdfs/1942/war_diary_naval_1942_08---day-16-to-day-31.pdf?rev=49ee2f42961e4926a0ce04d43d15f77c) · [No. 411 Squadron's account in the RCAF history, pp. 236–237](https://www.canada.ca/content/dam/themes/defence/caf/militaryhistory/dhh/popular/crucible-war-1939-1945-6.pdf)

The largest outstanding German gap is the full strike and reconnaissance order, including units whose crews returned without a recorded loss or combat claim. On the Allied side, separating allocation, use of a station and individual sortie results still requires squadron records. The Canadian official history identifies 414 Squadron's report to No. 35 Wing of 22 August in AIR 37/351, the October assessment of tactical reconnaissance in AIR 39/19, and the operations chronology in AIR 16/765.
