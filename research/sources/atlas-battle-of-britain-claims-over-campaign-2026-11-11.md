# Claims and aircraft losses over the Battle of Britain

10 July–31 October 1940. The British campaign comparison covers all 114 days in five phases and 17 weekly reporting periods, with partial weeks at each end. The German comparison has a published campaign total and seven dated cases. [Machine-readable source transcription](../transcriptions/battle-of-britain-campaign-claims-1940.json).

## British claims and German losses by phase

The postwar parliamentary comparison pairs contemporary RAF destruction estimates with German Quartermaster General aircraft loss figures. The figures include aircraft destroyed by British artillery. A published reproduction identifies the German totals as losses from all causes. Use recorded destruction per 100 claims as a ratio of aggregates. Establishing individual claim accuracy would require matching airframes and loss causes. [Hansard, 14 May 1947, vol. 437, cols. 1482–1485](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1947/may/14/battle-of-britain-enemy-losses) · [Wood and Dempster, The Narrow Margin, appendix 16](https://erenow.org/ww/narrow-margin-battle-britain-rise-air-power-1930-1940/21.php) · [AERO 1/1993, German loss table and footnote](https://bc.pollub.pl/Content/14151/Aero%201%201993.pdf).

The three phases from 8 August to 30 September give 1,216 German aircraft destroyed from 2,244 claims, or 54.2 per 100. Their combined excess of claims over recorded destruction is 1,028. The opening phase and October have 69 more recorded losses than claims in total. September's London bomber phase has the largest absolute gap, at 411.

### Reconciliation of the online parliamentary table

The HTML transcription shows 430 destroyed for 8–23 August. Adding that row to the other four gives 1,760, against the published campaign total of 1,733. The Quartermaster General figures reproduced by Wood and Dempster and the AERO article give 403 for the period. Their destruction and damage columns reconcile to 1,733 and 643. Wood and Dempster's contemporary German public admissions column reconciles to 896. The values below preserve the divergent online cells.

| Period | Measure | Online Hansard | Value used |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: |
| 8–23 Aug | German destroyed | 430 | 403 |
| 24 Aug–6 Sep | German damaged | 127 | 115 |
| 7–30 Sep | German damaged | 163 | 161 |
| 7–30 Sep | German losses publicly admitted at the time | 134 | 243 |

The online rows sum to 657 damaged and 787 publicly admitted, against printed totals of 643 and 896. The reconciled values come from the published transcriptions. The original bound Hansard page remains unverified. The AERO table agrees on the disputed counts and prints 7 September as the end of the preceding phase. The dates here follow Hansard and Wood and Dempster. British claims exceed German recorded destruction in 12 weekly periods. The three largest gaps are 235 for 11–17 August, 188 for 1–7 September and 148 for 15–21 September. Their combined gap of 571 is 59.2% of the net campaign gap of 965. The four consecutive reporting periods from 29 September through 26 October show more German aircraft destroyed than British claims.

## What the seven intensive days tell us

All seven days selected in Hansard fall within the 54-day window from 8 August to 30 September. They contribute 565 of the campaign's net 959 excess British claims. Their 39.6 losses per 100 claims differs substantially from the other 47 days in the same window.

| Dates included | British claims | German destroyed | Destroyed per 100 claims |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Full campaign | 2,692 | 1,733 | 64.4 |
| All 54 days from 8 Aug to 30 Sep | 2,244 | 1,216 | 54.2 |
| Seven selected intense days within that window | 936 | 371 | 39.6 |
| Other 47 days in that window, by subtraction | 1,308 | 845 | 64.6 |

The seven-day figures and full campaign figures use the parliamentary comparison and the reconciled phase transcription. The 47-day row is the difference between the 54-day and seven-day aggregate counts. [Hansard, phase and intensive-day tables](https://api.parliament.uk/historic-hansard/commons/1947/may/14/battle-of-britain-enemy-losses) · [The Narrow Margin, appendix 16](https://erenow.org/ww/narrow-margin-battle-britain-rise-air-power-1930-1940/21.php).

### Hold out 15 September

The parliamentary return gives 185 British claims and 56 German aircraft destroyed on 15 September. Removing that day before estimating its losses from the remaining records gives the following point predictions.

| Reference sample after removing 15 September | German destroyed / British claims | Prediction for 185 claims |
| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Rest of the campaign | 1,677 / 2,507 | 124 |
| Rest of the 7–30 September phase | 379 / 661 | 106 |
| Other six selected intensive days | 315 / 751 | 78 |
| **Published loss count for 15 September** | | **56** |

The broader phase totals give a better basis for understanding the campaign's average discrepancy. The extreme daily case shows the residual error when even a phase-specific average is applied to one large engagement. ## German claims and British losses

Richards's official history compares 3,058 German claims with 915 British losses across the full campaign, or 29.9 recorded losses per 100 claims in that published comparison. Churchill identifies the 915 as the RAF fighter-loss tally. The German total has no target or cause breakdown in Richards's footnote, so the comparison is campaign-level context. [Richards, The Fight at Odds, ch. VI, p. 190 n. 4](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/UN/UK/UK-RAF-I/UK-RAF-I-6.html) · [Churchill, Aircraft Losses table](https://www.fadedpage.com/books/20191054/html.php).

Cox pairs German Air Staff intelligence reports with RAF loss counts for seven dated cases. Their pooled ratio is 32.4 losses per 100 claims. The German reports and the published RAF loss compilation are cited in note 49. [Cox, Air Power Review 3(3), dated claims and losses table and n. 49](https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/centre-for-air-and-space-power-studies/aspr/apr-vol3-iss3-3-pdf/).

| 28 Aug | 40 | 14 | 35.0 |

On 7 September the British ratio was 40.0 German losses per 100 British claims and the German ratio was 26.9 RAF losses per 100 German claims. On 15 September the British ratio fell to 30.3 while the German ratio rose to 37.2. These comparisons use Hansard and Cox's published day counts.
