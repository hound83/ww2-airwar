# Source audit: thin campaign operational records, 6 November 2026

## Selection and reporting rules

At the previous reviewed milestone the twenty campaigns collectively contained 56 provenance-scoped numerical observations. Norway, Barbarossa, Sicily and Berlin were four especially thin dossiers (one observation each). This work adds nine more observations and two dated source-accessible events. **These are not nine new aircraft loss totals.** Each number retains its unit, source, exact or approximate time window and operating population.

### Norway, 1940: 263 Squadron at Lesjaskog

- **Primary:** RAF Air Historical Branch, Squadron Leader J. W. Donaldson, *Report on Operations of No. 263 Sqn in Norway*, account of 25 April [RAF MOD report and original image transcription](https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/our-history/air-historical-branch/ahb-despatches-and-reports/report-on-operations-of-no-263-sqn-in-norway-by-sqn-ldr-j-w-donaldson-oc/): **13 Gladiators completely destroyed** by German attacks on the frozen lake airfield on **25 April 1940**. Losses on the ground, not in aerial combat. Do not add these to the 17 **different surviving aircraft** embarked in June, which belonged to Nos. 263 and 46 Squadrons and a later redeployed force.
- **Museum aircraft record:** RAF Museum [Gladiator N5628 historical file](https://www.rafmuseum.org.uk/documents/collections/72-A-472-Gladiator-II-N5628.pdf), narrative of initial Lesjaskog operations: **49 squadron sorties**, **37 separate enemy aircraft engaged**, and **six enemy aircraft *claimed* destroyed** over approximately two flying days in April. These are three distinct dimensions. Six claims are not six confirmed German aircraft destroyed. The source summarizes the period rather than providing an independently dated squadron sortie ledger. The UI explicitly says the time is a **multi-day period** rather than fabricating a 25 April one-day tally.
- RAF Museum [1940 chronology](https://www.rafmuseum.org.uk/research/research-enquiries/history-of-aviation-timeline/british-military-aviation/1940-2/) provides independent general chronology; its count of 37 interceptions and six claims is similar, but the operational report and museum record are the specific quantitative sources.

### Barbarossa, opening attack on 22 June 1941

- **USAF official historical synthesis:** [*Air Warfare and Air Base Air Defense*, chapter VI](https://media.defense.gov/2025/Jun/10/2003736254/-1/-1/0/AIR%20WARFARE%20%26%20AB%20DEFENSE.PDF). The retrospective Soviet historical account gives **at least 1,200 Soviet aircraft destroyed**, **approximately 800 on the ground**, in the German opening attack.
- **Critical subset relationship:** 800 ⊂ 1,200; the Atlas must not display 2,000 by adding them. Both are retrospective historical estimates, **not** a primary Soviet airfield-by-airfield June 22 register, and the established discrepancy with the Luftwaffe's higher claims remains separate. The first day is not the 1941 campaign total.

### Sicily, Operation Husky, 10–12 July 1943

- **Primary operational report:** US Navy [*Action Report of the Western Naval Task Force*, subsequently declassified, Section XXXVI](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/library/online-reading-room/title-list-alphabetically/s/the-sicilian-campaign-operation-husky.html). Reports **approximately 440 fighter-cover sorties per day** over CENT, DIME and JOSS landing areas; approximately two-thirds came from Malta and one-third from Pantelleria. The phrase "each day" is a rate for the landing-cover operation, not a total of aircraft on hand or a precise midnight-to-midnight 10 July tally.
- Same action report: **approximately 200 enemy flights plotted** by the Western Task Force between **02:45 on 10 July and 18:15 on 12 July 1943**. These are enemy flights/tracks on a command board, **not** 200 enemy planes destroyed or even necessarily 200 distinct aircraft. The Western Task Force observations are not all Allied and Axis flight activity in Sicily.
- **Cross-check:** US Navy [campaign overview](https://www.history.navy.mil/browse-by-topic/wars-conflicts-and-operations/world-war-ii/1943/sicilian-campaign.html) and RAF Museum contemporary chronology support the campaign period, but the primary action report controls the quantitative population.

### Berlin, 19–20 April 1945

- **Secondary, published study:** William E. Hiestand, [*Eastern Front 1945: Triumph of the Soviet Air Force*, Osprey Air Campaign 42](https://www.ospreypublishing.com/us/eastern-front-1945-9781472857842/), p. 87 daily sortie table: **4,398** Soviet **16th Air Army sorties on 19 April**, **4,054** sorties **20 April**. A sortie is a flown mission, not a unique airframe or a loss. Neither number represents the other Soviet air armies attacking Berlin.
- **Independent bibliographical context:** John Erickson, [*The Road to Berlin*](https://doi.org/10.4324/9780429314353), annotated reference to the Soviet 16th Air Army wartime history, reports the same 19/20 April sortie figures. We did **not** independently transcribe the original Soviet loss or sortie registers.
- **Unresolved totals:** The Osprey prose reports ~91,000 sorties for the wider Berlin operation; its printed tabular subseries totals do not transparently reconcile to that rounded headline and vary by definition of supporting air commands. **Neither 91,000 nor the earlier conflicting 2,738 aircraft/experienced-crew count enters the Atlas as a factual numeric observation.** An original Soviet source return and army-by-army denominator reconciliation remain needed.

## Checks before promotion

The nine new facts and two event dossiers must preserve the source links, chronological event ordering, multi-day period flags, original/synthesized distinctions, subset assertions and page-level browser navigation. The key legacy campaign cross-checks, German command reports, USAAF ETO/MTO tables, Soviet snapshots and global production series remain untouched.
