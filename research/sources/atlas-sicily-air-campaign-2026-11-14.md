# From Malta to Messina · Sicily air campaign source audit

The [Sicily explorer](../../sicily.html) adds a dated journey from the offshore fighter bases in June to the end of Operation Husky on 17 August. The [evidence data](../transcriptions/sicily-air-campaign-1943.json) carries the source, section locator and historical population for each record. The Atlas opens on the landing-cover case. Select the preceding chapter to trace how the offshore bases and pre-invasion strikes contributed to that cover.

| Evidence | What the source supports and where its coverage stops |
| --- | --- |
| Pantelleria and Gozo | The official U.S. air history’s [Pantelleria account](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/AAF/II/AAF-II-13.html) dates the island’s surrender to 11 June. The [RAF campaign narrative](https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/our-history/air-historical-branch/second-world-war-campaign-narratives/sicilian-campaign-june-august-1943/) places the 33rd Fighter Group at Pantelleria and the 31st at Gozo by early July. Its 3 July date concerns Malta operational control of Spitfire units. It does not date every advance party or the first sortie from either field. |
| Gerbini | The U.S. air history’s final pre-invasion offensive describes 1,323 and 197 tons dropped by the Northwest African and Ninth Air Forces respectively on 3–9 July, with the main Gerbini field and seven satellites assessed unserviceable around D-Day. The RAF’s Appendix 3, Ground Attack, gives site-specific aircraft strength and serviceability for parts of SKG 10 on 10 July. The snapshot is British intelligence. The available aircraft and airfield status measure different things. [Directory and approximate locations](https://www.ww2.dk/Airfields%20-%20Italy%20Sicily%20and%20Sardinia.pdf) |
| Beachhead cover | The [Western Naval Task Force action report](https://www.history.navy.mil/research/library/online-reading-room/title-list-alphabetically/s/the-sicilian-campaign-operation-husky.html), §XXXVI, reports approximately 440 fighter sorties per day across CENT, DIME and JOSS and roughly 200 enemy tracks from 02:45 on 10 July to 18:15 on 12 July. Tracks are observations, and the sortie rate applies to the reported landing operation. Dashed map lines for the 31st and 33rd relate the RAF air plan’s named group assignments to representative sectors. |
| Gela follow-up, night of 11–12 July | The [U.S. Army history](https://ibiblio.org/hyperwar/USA/USA-MTO-Sicily/USA-MTO-Sicily-9.html), chapter IX, gives 144 departed, 23 failed to return and 37 badly damaged. These figures cover the follow-up carrier mission from Tunisia. The inquiry records fire from both army and naval batteries without establishing which opened fire first. The pin marks the intended drop area. |
| Airfield capture and use | The RAF narrative’s *Comparison with the Air Plan*, *The First Fighter Wing in Sicily* and *The Landing of XII Air Support Command* give the capture and use sequence for Pachino and Ponte Olivo. Licata records the 33rd Fighter Group’s arrival on 16 July. Comiso’s 108 is a later inspection count of aircraft at the field. It should stay separate from a combat loss ledger. |
| Biscari | The RAF narrative dates occupation to 12 July and explicitly excludes Biscari from the fields serviceable by 13 July. The [U.S. Military Academy study](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/NHC/NewPDFs/USArmy/United%20States%20Military%20Academy/USMA%2C%20Operations%20in%20Sicily%20and%20Italy%2C%201950.pdf) dates the 45th Division’s capture to 14 July. The records do not establish a common threshold for occupation. Both observations are retained. |
| Messina | The [U.S. air history](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/AAF/II/AAF-II-14.html) describes daytime and night attacks. The [U.S. Army history, chapter XXI](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/USA/USA-MTO-Sicily/USA-MTO-Sicily-21.html), notes 9 and 21, gives the Allied claims for craft destroyed, separately reports Axis craft losses, and cites the German evacuation figures. The Axis account lists eight Italian and seven German craft sunk, with only one reported lost in action. The 39,569 evacuated German personnel include 4,444 wounded and exclude the Italian evacuation. These are distinct reporting populations and support no matched attrition rate. |
| Campaign totals | [HQ MAAF’s wartime compilation](https://znaci.org/00002/315.pdf), printed p. 13, gives 25,070 operational sorties, 16,243 tons of bombs, 293 Allied claims and 177 Allied aircraft losses for 10 July–17 August. See the [prior MAAF audit](atlas-original-raf-daily-returns-and-maaf-sicily-2026-11-06.md). |

The [invasion](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Allied_invasion_of_Sicily) and [Fustian](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Fustian) reference trails were used to orient the research. Original Axis daily operational and loss returns are still needed to match individual engagements. The map shows sourced or representative locations and does not reconstruct exact flight paths or individual crash sites.
