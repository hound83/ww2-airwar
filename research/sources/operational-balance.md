# Operational balance studies · v9.10

The [six studies](../../operational-balance.html) ask how much of a force could actually fly, what it did and which effects the records establish. The [source transcription](../transcriptions/operational-balance.json) holds 77 figures and the input IDs for 19 calculations. The [combined CSV](../exports/operational-balance.csv) and six study downloads carry the figures, dates, units, populations, sources and passage locators. A percentage uses the dispatched force or the relevant stock in its own report. The generated percentages are rounded to one decimal place.

| Study | Finding and source decision | What another source could resolve |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Normandy | The [416th Group’s Mission 103](https://www.416th.com/missions/mission103.html) cites subsequent photographs of cuts at Bourth. No. 430 Squadron’s Dives report is another location and date. The [Army ground account](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/USA/USA-E-Breakout/USA-E-Breakout-13.html) explains why armour still went forward with assault troops on 26 July. Mission 494’s attacking total is 1,503 in the chronology and 1,507 in the AAF narrative. Compare the chronology only to its own 1,581 dispatched. The Army and AAF friendly-casualty findings are also kept separately. | Railway repair and traffic records after Bourth, plus a headquarters file showing receipt or use of the Dives intelligence. |
| Battle of Britain | The [USAF case study](https://media.defense.gov/2010/Oct/12/2001309632/-1/-1/0/AFD-101012-038.pdf), Battle of Britain chapter, discussion of fig. 3-3, gives the Hurricanes and Spitfires in maintenance units ready for issue on 10 August and 7 September. The decline from 289 to 125 describes that reserve. The 201-pilot shortfall, No. 303 Squadron’s overnight repairs and No. 1 RCAF’s three scramble sizes have different denominators. | Daily Northolt readiness sheets with serial and pilot identities, particularly on 16 and 27 September. |
| Defence of the Reich | The [Gotha mission](https://445bg.org/mission-28.html) and the [following day at Fürth](https://445bg.org/mission-29.html) establish continued operations after the loss of thirteen B-24s. The latter report lists 17 dispatched, five aborts and 12 over the primary. The Eighth Air Force denominator already includes the 445th. III./ZG 26’s single Liberator claim lacks an individual counterpart. | Group operations and maintenance returns identifying crews and airframes reused, borrowed, repaired or newly arrived. |
| Bagration | The Soviet [16th Air Army history](https://militera.lib.ru/h/16va/03.html), chapter 3, assigns 175 bombers, 217 assault aircraft and 134 fighters to the 27 June evening attack. The three roles reproduce its total of 526. The [German-side command chronology](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/USA/USA-EF-Defeat/USA-EF-Defeat-15.html) describes the pocket already closing. The separate 1st Air Army headline of 119 and component sum of 179 concern the opening stage through Minsk. | Original 1st Air Army return for category overlap or transcription, and German unit reports separating evening strike losses from encirclement and artillery. |
| Malta and Sicily | Hague’s two Bowery carrier totals and the RAF arrival return support the calculation 60 of 64. The 134 sorties in the RAF account cover the defence that day. Malta Command’s Pedestal unloading entry gives general cargo in tons and identifies furnace fuel and diesel separately. The [No. 92 Squadron diary](https://www.92-squadron.com/copy-of-form-540-oct-1941-sept-1942) records the later shipping cover. [Nelson’s service chronology](https://www.naval-history.net/xGM-Chrono-01BB-Nelson.htm) dates her bombardment differently. | Serial-level readiness after Calendar, airfield fuel issues, and individual ship logs for the July 1943 patrol. |
| German replacement capacity | The [USSBS European summary](https://www.anesi.com/ussbs02.htm) reports synthetic aviation gasoline output of 175,000 tons in April, 30,000 in July and 5,000 in September 1944. The survey’s Oil section explicitly connects the shortage to further training cuts and shorter engine run-in. [Murray, table LIX](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/AAF/AAF-Luftwaffe/AAF-Luftwaffe-7.html), gives comparable dated single-engine fighter counts for one command. The first-quarter 1945 all-type production total of 7,367 is from the published monthly series. | Original USSBS monthly table and unit-level receipts of aircraft, trained crews and fuel. The 4,103/4,130 September 1944 production discrepancy remains in the previous [production audit](v75-german-production.md). |

## Reconciliation and release

Previously published formation diaries, campaign findings and operations records supply a baseline. The new group next-day return, Fighter Command issue reserves, synthetic gasoline figures and receiving-side accounts establish follow-through. PR #5's cross-force and airfield inventories have different populations from the matched reserve and mission returns. PR #20's aggregate candidate evidence was previously reviewed across the 20 dossiers. Neither candidate is counted as independent confirmation of the numbers used here.

The public site already contained PR #67’s 26 formation histories at `e59c4ca`. Its last named release was v9.9 and its default explorer still displayed v9.0 in the browser title. This delivery names the public version v9.10 and updates the title and masthead. The v10 milestone is the connected European narrative on the [roadmap](../../docs/ATLAS_ROADMAP_TO_V10.md).
