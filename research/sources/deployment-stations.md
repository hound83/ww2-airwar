# Deployment geography and dated activity

The station supplement joins 104 named entries in ten benchmark populations. It maps 34 historical airfields linked to 59 entries. The other entries retain the resolution of their sources, including operational sectors, conflicting locations and personnel-only camps. Twenty-six entries have separately sourced, dated activities.

## Battle of Britain · 7 September 1940

Basil Collier lists 21 numbered Luftflotte 2 fighter Gruppen and eight headquarters under single-engined fighters. A separate table gives both sector and actual war station for 23 No. 11 Group squadrons. No. 257 has two stations. The Canadian and British No. 1 Squadrons occupy separate rows. ## Sicily · 9–16 July 1943

The RAF campaign narrative identifies 23 squadrons under five Commonwealth wings on 10 July. HQ Malta’s preliminary operational report gives the previous day’s airfield distribution. Most members of 244, 322 and 324 Wings can be placed at Luqa, Ta Kali, Hal Far or Safi. No. 260 RAF and No. 450 RAAF appear at Camp No. 6 as personnel only. No. 3 RAAF’s station chronology independently places it at Ta Kali from 9 to 13 July. No. 7 SAAF Wing’s three children are in the command table, while refit histories put them in Tunisia. The July date of the two squadrons’ transfer to El Haouaria remains unresolved. [RAF campaign narrative, Appendices 2–3](https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/our-history/air-historical-branch/second-world-war-campaign-narratives/sicilian-campaign-june-august-1943/) · [HQ Malta, Appendices A–B](https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/our-history/air-historical-branch/ahb-despatches-and-reports/preliminary-report-of-op-husky-operations-by-malta-based-aircraft/) · [3 Squadron Association, bases 63–65](https://www.3sqnraafasn.net/indexpages/dates.htm) · [7 Wing refitting](https://samilitaryhistory.org/diaries/rosy/rosy.html) · [5 Squadron stations](https://www.historyofwar.org/air/units/SAAF/5_wwII.html)

The German return counts four numbered Gruppen based on Sicily. A station chronology also puts II./JG 51 at Trapani starting on 10 July, after its move from Sardinia. The same day’s transfer of JG 53 headquarters from Comiso to Catania illustrates the unresolved reporting time. II./JG 53 is firmer. Holm names Ramacca, a satellite in the Gerbini complex, and de Zeng records aircraft of that Gruppe destroyed there on 15 July. For the Italian 21° Gruppo, Chinisia and the detachment at Palermo-Boccadifalco belong to one unit. [JG 51](https://www.ww2.dk/air/jagd/jg51.htm) · [JG 53](https://www.ww2.dk/air/jagd/jg53.htm) · [de Zeng, Gerbini satellite No. 12](https://www.lasiciliainrete.it/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2022/08/Airfields-Italy-Sicily-and-Sardinia.pdf) · [Italian disposition on 9 July](https://cefalunews.org/2022/06/16/la-regia-aeronautica-in-sicilia-alla-vigilia-dello-sbarco-degli-alleati-9-luglio-1943/)

The US command had aircraft on Gozo, Pantelleria and the Tunisian mainland. Its 33rd Fighter Group patrolled the shipping off Licata on 10 July. A separate report names the 27th and 86th together on strafing missions on 13 July. Assigning individual targets to each group requires their mission reports. [USAAF, Air Operations in the Sicilian Campaign](https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/Portals/16/documents/Studies/1-50/AFD-090602-085.pdf) · [USAF, 86th group stations](https://www.ramstein.af.mil/Portals/6/documents/AFD-160205-007.pdf) · [USAF group histories](https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/Portals/16/documents/Studies/101-150/AFD-090529-055.pdf)

## Kursk · 4–11 July 1943

The assembly list for 4 July puts four northern Gruppen in the Orel area. Its four southern Gruppen have more specific entries. The de Zeng gazetteer confirms both I./JG 52 and III./JG 3 at Bessonovka. For III./JG 52 Holm identifies Ugrim, where the assembly list offers two alternatives. The dated activity entries identify individual participants within their Gruppe. Pilot claims retain their original status. ## Bagration · June–July 1944

Connor’s Annex C lists five air armies across four fronts. The right and left wings of the 1st Belorussian Front had separate air armies, and the left wing’s advance began later. The displayed locations represent campaign sectors. Dated activity comes from separate army histories or archival studies, with a locator on each entry. Soviet army-wide totals can include several types of aircraft and repeated flights by the same crews. [Connor, Annex C and operational plan](https://www.armyupress.army.mil/Portals/7/combat-studies-institute/csi-books/connor.pdf)

## Map evidence

Each member’s source establishes its reported location. Each station has a separate source for its geographical coordinates. Coordinates mark approximate airfield positions. In particular, the Gozo pin follows the historical Ta’ Lambert landing ground and the Heath Row pin follows the wartime Great West Aerodrome. [Gozo field](https://www.forgottenairfields.com/airfield-ta-lambert-386.html) · [Great West Aerodrome](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Great_West_Aerodrome) · [Finocchiaro, Sicilian airfields in 1943](https://lambadoria.it/bunker/articoli/finocchiaro-c1/finocchiaro%204%20ott%20%20AEROPORTI%20SICILIA%20ANNO%201943.pdf)

The Sicily study and the roster map both use the Ta’ Lambert airfield area for Gozo. The historical account in *Perspettivi* places the later landing strip approximately along the first wartime runway. The pin identifies the airfield area, without claiming a particular aircraft parking position. [Gozo Regional Development Authority, Perspettivi 2](https://grda.mt/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/Perspettivi-Issue-2-30.03.23.pdf)
