# Battle of Britain — UK-side, strengths, production and campaign data (12 November 2026)

Broader extraction for the Atlas covering RAF/UK figures, campaign phases, strengths, production and storytelling elements. Drawn from Wikipedia Battle of Britain page and the secondary sources it cites (Overy, Bungay, Wood & Dempster, official returns). Complements the earlier German-claim and German-loss notes.

## Campaign frame
- Official period: 10 July – 31 October 1940.
- Five commonly cited phases:
  1. 26 June – 16 July: Störangriffe (nuisance raids), armed reconnaissance, mine-laying; Kanalkampf begins 4 July.
  2. 17 July – 12 August: Intensified Channel battles against shipping, ports and coastal airfields.
  3. 13 August – 6 September: Adlerangriff (Eagle Attack) — main assault on RAF airfields in southern England.
  4. 7 September – 2 October: Shift to London (Blitz begins).
  5. 3 – 31 October: Large-scale night bombing; daylight limited to small fighter-bomber raids.
- Major named days: 15 August (“Greatest Day” / largest Luftwaffe sorties), 18 August (“Hardest Day”), 15 September (Battle of Britain Day).

## Opening strengths (late June / 1 July 1940)
- Luftwaffe (Quartermaster General 6th Battalion, 29 June): 4,074 available — 1,107 single-seat fighters, 357 two-seat fighters, 1,380 medium bombers, 428 dive-bombers, 569 reconnaissance, 233 coastal.
- RAF: 1,963 available — 754 single-seat fighters, 149 two-seat fighters, 560 bombers, 500 coastal (fighter figure at 0900 1 July; bomber figure 11 July).
- Fighter Command pilot operational strength: 1,200 (establishment 1,482) at week ending 30 June.

## Losses and claims (campaign scale, multiple sources)
- RAF Fighter Command losses 10 July–31 October: ~1,087 aircraft (including 53 twin-engined); plus 376 Bomber Command and 148 Coastal Command (one secondary total gives RAF overall ~1,547).
- Luftwaffe losses: 1,733 (official German records / Hansard) to 1,977 (some secondary totals) aircraft destroyed.
- RAF claims: 2,692–2,698.
- German claims: ~3,058–3,198.
- Aircrew: RAF ~1,495 killed (449 fighter pilots); Luftwaffe ~2,585 killed / 925 captured (Wikipedia summary figures).
- Civilian: 23,002 killed, 32,138 wounded (July–December).

## Storytelling / Atlas-relevant notes
- Fighter Command flew ~80,000 sorties July–October; Bomber Command 9,180.
- ~2,936 aircrew awarded the Battle of Britain clasp; 595 non-British (145 Polish, 127 New Zealand, 112 Canadian, etc.).
- 303 (Polish) Squadron claimed 126 German aircraft.
- Pilot wastage peaked at ~120 per week in the 24 August–6 September airfield phase; Fighter Command was short ~150 pilots by 31 August.
- By 15 September, Fighter Command could still put up 269 Spitfires and 533 Hurricanes; 11 Group had 310 fighters.
- German single-engined fighter pilot strength fell from 906 (1 June) to 673 (1 November).
- The home advantage (pilot recovery, shorter range, radar-controlled interception) is the recurring narrative explanation for why Fighter Command held while the Luftwaffe did not achieve air superiority for Sealion.
