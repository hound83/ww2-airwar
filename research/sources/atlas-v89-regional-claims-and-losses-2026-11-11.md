# v8.9 regional aircraft claims and losses

## Cobra, northern France, summer 1944

v8.8 showed 1–8 by allocating the wider German day report in two ways. The [v8.8 audit](atlas-v88-claims-and-losses-2026-11-11.md) preserves that calculation. v8.9 uses two earlier engagements with US day-fighter claims and reconstructed German losses. The numerator is aircraft assessed lost in air combat. The denominator is claims of destruction in the air.

| Engagement | US reporting population | Air claims | German losses used | Source |
| --- | --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| 27 June evening, northern France | Eighth Air Force fighters against I. and II./JG 27 | 14 | 9 Bf 109s | [Reconstruction citing David Clark, Angels Eight p. 94 and daily data](https://forum.dcs.world/topic/144168-the-air-battle-over-normandy/) · [Jure encounter report, 20:30–20:45 near Esternay](https://www.8af.org/james-jure-56th-fighter-group-27-june-1944.cfm) |
| 18 July morning, Évreux–Mantes | Ninth Air Force 474th Fighter Group | 11 | 6 German fighters, plus one uncertain forced landing | [Steve Blake, Anatomy of a Dogfight](https://raf-warmwell.co.uk/anatomy-of-a-dogfight/) |
| 15 August, Rambouillet | Ninth Air Force 373rd Fighter Group against II./JG 26 | 3 | 2 reconstructed Fw 190 losses | [Donald Caldwell, JG 26: Top Guns of the Luftwaffe, ch. 11](https://electronicsandbooks.com/edt/manual/Publischer/G/German%20Miltary%20WW2/JG%2026-Top%20Guns%20Of%20The%20Luftwaffe%20c20091007%20%5B255%5D.pdf) |

The 27 June comparison comes from a published discussion citing Clark's research. The American encounter report verifies unit, time and combat, and the wider [official chronology](https://b24.net/8thAAFcombat.htm) reports additional 27 June missions. The source for the paired 14 and nine is the reconstruction. The July 18 total counts the shared Bf 109 once. Blake's breakdown gives three in the 429th, six including the shared aircraft in the 428th, and two further in the 430th. Blake reports at least seven German losses. The six used here are the three losses in I./JG 26 and III./JG 54 and three III./JG 1 aircraft. The forced landing of I./JG 5 pilot Günther Wimsche has an unresolved final aircraft disposition. For 15 August, Caldwell reports two German pilots lost and describes the repair of the damaged aircraft flown by Stein. The two inferred aircraft losses are the check, with a separate later encounter near Dreux.

The first two rows give 15 ÷ 25 = **60 losses per 100 destruction claims**. Mission 494's 12 claims × 0.60 = 7.2, displayed as **about 7**. Including Wimsche gives 16 ÷ 25 = 64 per 100, or about 8 for Cobra. This change illustrates one reporting choice. It does not specify a statistical interval. Holding out the August case gives 3 × 0.60 = 1.8, which rounds to its reconstructed two losses. The sample comprises selected fights, with both escort and tactical fighter units. It is a provisional comparison for US daylight fighter claims over northern France in summer 1944.

The German [wider calendar](https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/our-history/air-historical-branch/ahb-translations-from-captured-german-documents/calendar-of-operations-in-north-west-europe-july-1944/#page=46) reports ten aircraft lost on 25 July across its area. That is geographic and operational context. For example, [Foy’s Mission 494-era encounter report](https://www.8af.org/robert-foy-357th-fighter-group-25-july-1944.cfm) describes combat southeast of Paris that morning. The German loss table's inverted scan still needs visual transcription and attribution. Cobra's original claims, including the separate two ground claims, remain sourced observations. The JSON export includes the point estimate, the alternate treatment of the forced landing, the training rows and the August check.

Sources and notes for each are linked from the comparison in the calculator. The 6 June account is summarized by [Frappé’s Normandy study discussion](https://falkeeins.blogspot.com/2018/11/herbert-huppertz-jg-2-6-8-june-1944.html) and the Clark citation above. The 11 June and 15 August comparisons come from Caldwell, chapter 11. Blake describes the 18 July losses of Holland, Harless and Goodrich. He reports three P-38s shot down. The first two are supported by combat accounts. Goodrich’s aircraft was on fire when last seen, and the cause is less clear in the witness account. The table retains the disputed third cause. The mixed US and RAF fight and the different stages of claim approval are visible by case.

## Bobruisk, 26 June 1944

The [Soviet 16th Air Army history](https://militera.lib.ru/h/16va/03.html) gives 13 claims in 29 air engagements and 19 Soviet own losses on 26 June. Its Bobruisk summary for 24 June through 4 July gives 95 claims and 141 air engagements. The [16th Air Army HQ report excerpt](https://docs.historyrussia.org/ru/nodes/279190) identifies the original archive series. It does not enumerate comparable German opponent losses for the day.

| Bobruisk-based group | Base dates | June aircraft removals through enemy action |
| --- | --- | ---: |
| III./JG 51 | 21–27 June | 8 Bf 109G-6 |
| I./SG 10 | 23–27 June | 4 Fw 190s, two F-8, one G-3, one G-8 |

German aircraft movement records are transcribed by Michael Holm. The "durch Feindeinw." column contains removals through enemy action. The June counts come from the [III./JG 51 return](https://ww2.dk/oob/bestand/jagd/biiijg51.html) and [I./SG 10 return](https://www.ww2.dk/oob/bestand/schlacht/bisg10.html). The dates are from the [JG 51](https://www.ww2.dk/air/jagd/jg51.htm) and [SG 10](https://www.ww2.dk/air/attack/sg10.htm) station lists.

The Atlas assumes half to all of these 12 June removals fell in the opening battles and allocates them using 29 ÷ 141, the day’s share of the 16th Air Army’s recorded engagements. The result is 1.23–2.47 under those scenarios. The midpoint rounds to **about 2 enemy-action removals from these two groups**. The reporting interval for the activity proxy extends to 4 July. The 50–100% concentration is an assumption about where within June the known removals fell. Group basing establishes relevance, while the unit returns do not identify the daily sector, cause breakdown or Allied unit responsible. This is a low-confidence estimate for those groups, separate from the 13 Soviet air claims and 19 Soviet losses. Other Luftwaffe formations require separate records.

A source that establishes complete comparable dated loss coverage for either displayed estimate takes priority through the existing `lossScopeId` mechanism. The Atlas preserves the operation and event reporting scope on screen.
