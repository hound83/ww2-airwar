# Operation Bodenplatte — operation-wide evidence and chronology

Bodenplatte was the Luftwaffe's mass attack on Allied airfields on **1 January 1945**. This research ledger covers the raid and its context from **16 December 1944 to 14 January 1945**. The dates outside New Year's Day describe preparation, earlier independent attacks, Allied recovery and the subsequent movement of I./JG 1.

The [operation data](../transcriptions/bodenplatte-operation-scope-1944-1945.json) hold **20 locations**, **16 dated timeline entries**, **81 quantitative observations** and **11 RAF ground-defence site reports**. The [additional airframe CSV](../transcriptions/bodenplatte-operation-airframes-1945-01-01.csv) has **47 serial or Werknummer entries** absent from the previous 106-row extract. The [previous unit audit](atlas-bodenplatte-unit-reports-2026-11-13.md), [106-row extract](../transcriptions/bodenplatte-unit-airframes-1945-01-01.csv) and [five-field ledger](../transcriptions/bodenplatte-1945-01-01.json) provide the earlier evidence. The earlier [37 formation listings](../transcriptions/wikipedia-followup-evidence-2026-11-12.json) give order-of-battle leads. They do not establish that all listed formations took off.

## Raid geography

The 20-location inventory includes the 17 locations in Chant's broad list and adds **Helmond**, **Étain–Rouvres** and **Knokke / Le Zoute**. The list mixes actual attacks, reported diversions and intended targets. Use the status, formation and source fields for each location. Published counts of attack locations use different definitions.

| Sector | Locations and distinctions |
| --- | --- |
| Western Belgium | **Ghent–Sint-Denijs-Westrem, Maldegem and Ursel** were in the JG 1 sector. The Polish combat report provides the best directly accessible loss account at Ghent. **Knokke** is a reported possible diversion with no secured loss count. |
| Brussels and Antwerp | **Evere, Grimbergen and Melsbroek** are separate airfields. At Grimbergen, the RAF reports 24 attackers and no resident flying wing, while a secondary account lists about six parked aircraft destroyed. **Antwerp–Deurne and Woensdrecht** have separate RAF gun reports. |
| Netherlands | **Eindhoven, Gilze-Rijen, Heesch, Helmond and Volkel** have distinct evidence. No. 401 Squadron scrambled ten Spitfires from Heesch and claimed six German aircraft. The RAF records an attack at Volkel by more than 20 aircraft, while Rickard says JG 6 missed the field and no based aircraft were damaged or lost. A separate table gives one aircraft destroyed. These are preserved as different claims about attack, attribution and outcome. |
| Eastern Belgium | **Asch, Ophoven and Sint-Truiden** were affected by formations crossing or missing their assigned routes. **Le Culot** was JG 4's assigned target. The wartime U.S. annex records no attack at A-89. A later account describes a small element reaching the field. |
| France | **Metz–Frescaty (Y-34)** was attacked by Stab, II. and IV./JG 53. **Étain (A-82)** was the objective of III./JG 53, intercepted en route by the 358th Fighter Group. A-90 is identified as **Toul–Croix de Metz** in the U.S. airfield catalogue and Lowe. Some later summaries use A-90 for Frescaty. |

## New figures with the strongest provenance

- **Ghent, B-61.** The wartime Polish report gives **31 takeoffs** across Nos. 302, 308 and 317 Squadrons, **18 Spitfires destroyed and five damaged on the ground**, and **two Spitfires and two pilots lost in air combat**. Seven Spitfires forced-landed because of fuel shortage. The report's 20 destroyed, one probable and four damaged Fw 190s are claims. Breyner's personal account of two observed crashes, with no ammunition expended, belongs inside the same reporting population. The two Polish airframe identities, **MJ281** and **MK190**, are later case reconstructions linked to the report.
- **RAF Regiment reports.** The Air Historical Branch prints attack times where available, defending units, ammunition expenditure, claims and some casualties for **11 sites**. The Antwerp entry describes three waves, so the aircraft figures are retained as 16, 14 and three. The site rows reproduce the AHB total of **43 destruction claims and 28 damage claims**. The July Regiment report gives **27 down and 59 damaged**. Neither can be treated as a verified German loss total, and unspecified site fields are left null.
- **Sint-Truiden, A-92.** An indexed U.S. tactical excerpt reproduced in Frederick Bealke's 1988 report enumerates **six aircraft destroyed and 33 damaged**. Its categories are two P-47s destroyed, 11 P-47s category AC and 16 category A, one UC-78 destroyed and four command aircraft damaged, one B-24 and two B-17s destroyed, and two B-17s category AC. The **10 destroyed and 31 damaged** figures come from the annex to XXIX TAC A-2 Periodic Report No. 90, 2 January 1945, reproduced in the 404th Fighter Group diary and quoted by Lowe. The two figures remain unreconciled. [Archival follow-up](atlas-bodenplatte-original-sources-2026-11-13.md)
- **German assessment and Allied reconstruction.** AHB cites German translation **AHB5/234, Box 485** for a wartime German reconnaissance estimate of **479 Allied aircraft destroyed** in air and ground combat and **114 damaged**. It separately cites Manrho and Pütz, pp. 460–462, for **290 Allied aircraft destroyed on the ground and 180 damaged on the ground**. This confirms the scope of 290/180 in the RAF citation. The original book and air-combat annexes remain needed to settle the competing interpretations of **305 destroyed / 190 damaged**.
- **Named aircraft.** The new CSV expands the German evidence to additional entries for JG 1, JG 2, JG 4, JG 6, JG 77 and SG 4. It includes the Dutch register's No. 4 Squadron **PL765** ground loss at Gilze-Rijen, the two Polish air losses and **WNr 784993**, a 13./JG 53 forced landing after U.S. ground fire on the Frescaty route. The latter appeared previously in the audit prose. Register locations are crash locations and generally do not identify the attack target or the responsible gunner or fighter.

## From the delay to the aftermath

NIMH places the intended opening on **16 December** with the ground offensive, while Chant places the intended air support from the following day. Both describe a weather delay until New Year's Day. AHB records distinct attacks on **17–18 December**, two Me 262s targeting Helmond on **23 December**, and an attack on Eindhoven's communications dispersal on **25 December**, when four Austers were destroyed and five airmen wounded. These incidents are outside the 1 January loss account.

On **1 January**, the Ninth Air Force chronology records **190** A-20s, A-26s and B-26s striking targets elsewhere in the theatre. The 365th Fighter Group account says pilots from all three squadrons flew again that afternoon after sharing aircraft. The 368th chronology records armed reconnaissance on **2 January** and a group landing at Metz on **5 January**. NIMH records the replacement of I./JG 1's commander on **3 January**, limited subsequent activity and the move east on **14 January**. No. 350 Squadron's published diary reports its Spitfire strength restored on **13 January**. Each entry describes the named unit or command and cannot establish the recovery time of every attacked field.
