# Defence of the Reich · raid, escort and interception evidence

Baseline: Atlas v9.6, main at `4af8d9e8e2d14d6553045f2f1ce8a3c3e910f097`. The previous dossier gave phases and monthly figures. The v9.7 explorer links thirteen cases from July 1943 to February 1945. The dates bracket the evidence sampled here. The Reich defence campaign began earlier and continued into the final months of the war.

## How to read the records

- The map identifies a raid target or target area. The pins do not locate aircraft losses or draw flight paths. The official records can cover multiple targets.
- The case catalog sets out dates, reference IDs, source-specific figures and the extra observations. It links to the existing [1944 operations archive data](../transcriptions/operations-january-june-1944.json) by stable record ID. The printed whole-command monthly totals come directly from the published [v7.1](../../dist/atlas-v71-data.js) and [v7.2](../../dist/atlas-v72-data.js) series. A case with no transcribed month shows an explicit gap.
- Losses, write-offs and damage follow each source’s definitions. Claims and probable claims remain claims. Formation or airframe records within a wider mission are subsets. Missing aircraft are already included in the German command's enemy-action destruction. Other operational and non-operational causes are counted separately from enemy action. Daily and monthly German command totals include all types and the entire reporting area.
- The [June original-return audit](atlas-v91-june-loss-returns-and-missions-2026-11-12.md) documents differences between the sum of daily returns and the printed monthly return. Enemy destruction is 208 in either view, while enemy damage is 88 daily versus 90 printed. Other operational destruction is 44 versus 42 and non-operational damage is 415 versus 405. The printed June all-cause destruction total is 433. Individual type and role rows in the operations archive remain subsets.
- [Bundesarchiv](https://www.bundesarchiv.de/themen-entdecken/online-entdecken/themenbeitraege/bomben-auf-berlin/) distinguishes Luftwaffenbefehlshaber Mitte from the Luftflottenkommando Reich name adopted in February 1944. The Atlas follows the command labels and scope of the captured-return series for longitudinal context. A named command, a city’s flak defence and a German fighter formation describe different populations.

## Case audit

| Case | Sources checked and treatment |
| --- | --- |
| 24/25 July 1943 · Hamburg | The [Hamburg chronology](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Hamburg_in_World_War_II#Timeline) cites the RAF campaign diary and gives 791 dispatched and 728 bombing. The [contemporary Personal Report No. 7](https://ibccdigitalarchive.omeka.net/collections/document/30164) reports 751 in six phases after Pathfinder marking and 12 missing. The dispatch difference is open. The [RAF Museum’s Knilans account](https://www.rafmuseum.org.uk/research/online-exhibitions/americans-in-the-royal-air-force/americans-in-the-british-flying-services-1914-1945/hubert-nick-knilans/) identifies No. 619 Squadron. The first use of Window and the RAF night force belong to the opening night. Later US day operations are separate. |
| 17 August 1943 · Regensburg and Schweinfurt | [Craven and Cate, vol. II, ch. 20](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/AAF/II/AAF-II-20.html) and [Haywood Hansell’s ch. 3](https://ibiblio.org/hyperwar/AAF/Hansell/Hansell-3.html) document the two waves and North Africa landing plan. The display keeps 146/24 and 230/36 as subsets of 376/60 across the double mission. There is no matched German formation tally in this case. |
| 14 October 1943 · Black Thursday | The [National Museum of the US Air Force](https://www.nationalmuseum.af.mil/Visit/Museum-Exhibits/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/196143/black-thursday/) reports the final headquarters count of 251 departures, 60 failed returns, five crashes in England and twelve aircraft scrapped. [The National WWII Museum](https://www.nationalww2museum.org/war/articles/black-thursday-october-14-1943-second-schweinfurt-bombing-raid) cites German fighter figures of 31 destroyed, 12 written off and 34 damaged. Its underlying German ledger is not reproduced. The operational chronology and official narrative use other counting points. A ratio would imply equivalence across records that use different populations and statuses. |
| 20 February 1944 · Big Week | [Craven and Cate, vol. III, ch. 2](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/AAF/III/AAF-III-2.html) and the USAF Museum’s Big Week historical account establish the campaign context. Mission 226’s 835 escort count is partial and includes Ninth Air Force fighters. The ZG 26 account gives 16 launches, 11 airborne losses and nine aircraft damaged on the ground at Wunstorf. AHB VII/138 gives 62 enemy-action destroyed for the command day. |
| 24/25 February 1944 · Schweinfurt and Gotha | Craven and Cate pp. 38–39 establish the common Schweinfurt target across the day and night raids. The RAF AHB Appendix 10 gives 734 dispatched and 33 missing. The 445th Bomb Group Gotha report gives 25 dispatched and 13 lost, within Mission 233. The ZG 26 diary describes B-24s near Holzminden. The aircraft, area and date allow a possible link, and the formation/times remain unresolved. |
| 6 March 1944 · Berlin | [RAF AHB VII/138](https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/our-history/air-historical-branch/ahb-translations-from-captured-german-documents/luftwaffe-losses-in-the-area-of-luftflotte-reich-january-april-1944/), 6 March, Total (all types). The command reports 69 enemy-action destroyed and 12 damaged, including 60/12 in air combat, eight missing and one ground loss under other causes. It reports 1/5 other operational and 4/7 non-operational. Mission 250 also records 69 lost in its own separate American bomber population. ZG 26’s nine sorties, five losses and one damaged come from [Murray ch. 6](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/AAF/AAF-Luftwaffe/AAF-Luftwaffe-6.html), pp. 242–243, note 135 (BA/MA RL 10/257). |
| 30/31 March 1944 · Nuremberg | The RAF AHB vol. VI Appendix 10 target row has 795 dispatched, 616 attacking and 94 missing. The [British official history](https://www.generalstaff.org/WW2/Hist_UK/VictoryintheWestVol1.pdf) gives 710 attacking and 94 lost. The [Bomber Command Museum of Canada](https://bombercommandmuseumarchives.ca/s%2Cnuremberg.html) gives 95 lost, including 82 on the outward leg. The [RAF Museum’s Cyril Barton account](https://www.rafmuseum.org.uk/blog/for-valour-pilot-officer-cyril-joe-bartons-victoria-cross/) ties No. 78 Squadron’s Halifax LK797 to the raid. The attacking and loss counts remain attached to their sources. Each German return covers a whole calendar day, and this night ran across two dates. |
| 12 May 1944 · first oil attacks | [Craven and Cate, vol. III, ch. 6](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/AAF/III/AAF-III-6.html), ‘The First Oil Attacks’, gives 935 bombers across 15 combat wings. The Eighth Air Force chronology’s Mission 353 totals 886 dispatched, 813 attacking, 46 bomber losses and seven escort losses across the mission target listings. The 49-aircraft dispatch difference is unresolved. AHB VII/134 provides the printed monthly command context; no additional day total was promoted for this date. |
| 9 June 1944 · Munich | [55th Bombardment Wing Mission Summary 42](https://15thaf.org/55th_BW/Missions/June/9th/PDFs/Text%20Mission%20Summary.pdf) gives 112 airborne, 89 bombing and two missing. The report lists three losses including a friendly-field landing. Interim group reporting uses different definitions. The [AFHRA 332d Fighter Group study](https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/Portals/16/documents/Studies/AFD-141118-047.pdf) puts the two 459th Bomb Group B-24s (42-78219 and 42-52318, MACRs 6317 and 6179) inside the escort window of the 304th Wing, a separate formation. The German command day reports 3/5 by enemy action, including 2/2 in air combat. |
| 12/13 June 1944 · Nordstern | RAF AHB gives 294 dispatched, 276 attacking and 17 missing. The No. 115 Squadron ORB extract says eight detailed and only HK545 failed to return. A cited secondary reconstruction tentatively associates Modrow’s 1./NJG 1 claim with this aircraft. The claim location northwest of Duisburg and reported crash at Gelsenkirchen warrant confirmation in the original claim report. The 12 and 13 June command returns each represent a whole day. |
| 20 June 1944 · Misburg | The official oil history describes continued attacks on the Hanover and other oil targets. Mission 425’s 49 losses cover the mission, including other targets. Its indexed 44 escorts are explicitly partial. AHB VII/139 PDF pp. 17–18 reports the command totals, with 41 destroyed and 18 damaged by enemy action. |
| 2 November 1944 · Leuna | The [US chronology](https://etherit.co.uk/1944/11/02.htm) gives 1,174 bombers and 968 fighters dispatched, 571 bombers attacking Leuna, and mission losses of 40 bombers and 16 fighters. The [398th Bomb Group mission file](https://kajh.398th.org/Missions/Dates/1944/November/MIS_441102.html) and [MACR 10156](https://kajh.398th.org/History/398th_Aircraft/MACRs/MACR_10156_398th_601st_19441102_Campbell.html) identify a single 601st Bomb Squadron loss. [AHB VII/143, PDF p. 25](https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/our-history/air-historical-branch/ahb-translations-from-captured-german-documents/luftwaffe-losses-in-the-area-of-luftflotte-reich-october-november-1944/#page=25) gives 142/42 enemy action, 11/20 other operational and 6/7 non-operational. Enemy action splits into air combat 97/27, ground bombing 22/15 and 23 missing. There is no inference that all 142 were fighter losses or attributable to Leuna. |
| 3 February 1945 · Berlin | [384th Bomb Group archive, Mission 264](https://www.384thbombgroup.com/_content/_pages/One384thMission.php?MissionKey=279) reproduces the Mission 817 breakdown. The Berlin force dispatched 1,003 B-17s, with 937 attacking the Tempelhof rail target and 23 lost. The 1,437 dispatched and 25 bomber losses belong to the wider mission including Magdeburg. [Bundesarchiv’s Berlin material](https://www.bundesarchiv.de/themen-entdecken/online-entdecken/themenbeitraege/bomben-auf-berlin/) identifies the January–April air-raid records for Berlin. A matching February 1945 Reich airframe-loss return remains open. |

## Wikipedia reference trail and independent checks

The [existing coverage register](wikipedia-source-coverage-2026-11-12.json) captures the full reference tails of the campaign overview and several seed articles. This pass screened the relevant endnotes and bibliographies again, then checked the cited passages and material leads where publicly accessible. The register measures reference coverage. It does not imply that every cited book, archived page or press article was read in full.

| Reference trail | Follow-up and availability |
| --- | --- |
| [Defence of the Reich](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Defence_of_the_Reich#References) | The prior register captures 201 numbered citation lines. The central quantitative sources are Murray’s official history, Craven and Cate, USSBS and the British official historians. Followed Murray’s report locator and the published USAF volumes. Caldwell and Muller’s *The Luftwaffe over Germany*, Boog’s *Germany and the Second World War* and several unit histories were bibliographic leads. Their cited editions and relevant pages were not all freely accessible. |
| [Schweinfurt–Regensburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Schweinfurt%E2%80%93Regensburg_mission#References) and [second Schweinfurt](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Second_Schweinfurt_raid#References) | The register captures 44 and 26 numbered lines respectively. Followed the official American history and Hansell, then compared the official Air Force museum and National WWII Museum loss counts. The German unit-return source behind the latter tally remains open. Donald Caldwell and Richard Muller and Alfred Price remain follow-up leads. |
| [Big Week](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Big_Week#References) | The register captures 47 citation lines. Followed the official American history to the daylight/darkness Schweinfurt crossover. The independently published AHB VII/138 and 445th reporting provide the command and Gotha formation denominators. Secondary industry, pilot and production figures were screened separately. |
| [Hamburg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Hamburg_in_World_War_II#References) and [Battle of the Ruhr](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Battle_of_the_Ruhr#References) | Followed the RAF Campaign Diary lead, Webster and Frankland vol. | [Bombing of Berlin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Berlin_in_World_War_II#References) and [Bombing of Nuremberg](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bombing_of_Nuremberg_in_World_War_II#References) | Followed Craven and Cate, and screened Webster and Frankland and Martin Middlebrook’s Nuremberg raid study. The Bundesarchiv Berlin city and air-district file guide independently anchors the 1945 date. |
| [Oil campaign](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oil_campaign_of_World_War_II#References) | The [official oil history](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/AAF/III/AAF-III-6.html) was checked for the May raid and the autumn target series. The linked USSBS and Kreis’s *Piercing the Fog* are sources for oil intelligence and production chronology, a different population from raid and aircraft losses. The 398th archive and the German VII/143 scan supplied the new November comparison. |
| [JG 3](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jagdgeschwader_3#References) | The article associates IV.(Sturm)/JG 3 with the 91st Bomb Group on 2 November; the [German unit chronology](https://www.lexikon-der-wehrmacht.de/Gliederungen/Jagdgeschwader/JG3-R.htm) and [Sturmjäger study](https://luftkrieg-ueber-europa.de/en/sturmjaeger-cuirassiers-of-the-air/) help identify formations. The German command figure is directly observed. Individual Sturmgruppe claims were not promoted without a matched dated unit report and bombing-group roster. |

The [older bilateral PR #20](https://github.com/hound83/ww2-airwar/pull/20) was reviewed for overlap. Its Davis March figures describe whole-month Eighth Air Force heavy bomber write-offs, a different population from the Reich command or Mission 250. The Black Thursday museum lead was independently rechecked. No paid archives were used.

Remaining source targets include the original German formation return for 14 October, primary claim and crew crosswalks for Nuremberg and Nordstern, missing May daily rows and 1945 airframe returns. These gaps limit raid-level match rates. Where the evidence supports only a calendar-day or calendar-month comparison, the explorer retains that scope.
