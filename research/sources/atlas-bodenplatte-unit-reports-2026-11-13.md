# Operation Bodenplatte — Allied and Luftwaffe unit reports

**1 January 1945 · Research follow-up to Atlas v9.4**

This audit covers Eindhoven, Asch/Ophoven, Evere/Grimbergen, Melsbroek and Metz–Frescaty/Étain. The [airframe extract](../transcriptions/bodenplatte-unit-airframes-1945-01-01.csv) holds 106 dated serial or Werknummer entries from the printed Dutch loss register, 45 Allied and 61 German. The table below distinguishes contemporary testimony, catalogue references and later reconstructions. The linked records overlap the campaign ledger and cannot be added to its totals.

## Findings by airfield

| Airfield | Allied evidence | German evidence and limits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Eindhoven, B-78** | The SGLO register labels **40 named aircraft destroyed on the ground**, including eight No. 400 Squadron Spitfires, seven No. 430 Squadron aircraft (five Mustangs and two Spitfires), and six No. 440 Squadron Typhoons. It lists further airfield or local entries separately. No. 438 Squadron Typhoons **PD503** and **PD556** are listed with Keller and Wilson. For **MN569**, a transcription based on the RCAF accident report says No. 440 Squadron pilot E. T. Flanagan was wounded while taxiing. Keep the airframe disposition open. The Canadian official history's two airborne and 14 ground-hit Typhoons concern Nos. 438 and 440 Squadrons' takeoff group only. | Sixteen named **JG 3** register entries cover a larger area and mix dispositions. They cannot replace the specialist figure of 15 losses from 60 fighters sent. Two examples at the airfield are **682253**, 14./JG 3, and **782380**, 3./JG 3. The register also has **737385**, 2./JG 6, at the field. A separate **2./JG 26** entry, 600147 northwest of Eindhoven, illustrates why the crash location alone cannot establish the attack target. |
| **Asch / Ophoven, Y-29 / Y-32** | The 366th Fighter Association identifies eight airborne P-47s from the **390th FS** and twelve P-51s taking off from the **487th FS**. The 487th's published claim tally is distinct from own damage and from German losses. The underlying combat reports and Distinguished Unit Citation text were not recovered. A reference identifies William Whisner's 1 January combat log; its archived unit-site copy was inaccessible. | Ten serial entries in the SGLO extract include the **JG 11** staff and the II. and III. Gruppen. **739250** is recorded at Heerlen and **784958** at Niel-bij-As. These locations establish individual losses in the wider assignment. They do not identify the attacking U.S. pilot or allocate the entire 28-aircraft JG 11 tally to Asch. The German unit history gives differing Gruppe strength figures, which are kept as research leads rather than used to revise the published 61 dispatched. |
| **Brussels–Evere, B-56** | The Canadian Warplane Heritage Museum reproduces a contemporary diary entry by **No. 421 Squadron pilot Thomas DeCourcy**. His on-the-day estimate of 40 aircraft put out of action refers to what he understood about the airfield, with no damage/write-off split. The Canadian official history's **24 Spitfires affected** concerns the wing population. The two figures can coexist. The 421 Squadron ORB is catalogued as MIKAN **5032273**. | Dutch register entries identify I., II. and III./JG 26 Staffeln. **210953**, 2./JG 26, and **400233**, 3./JG 26, were lost at Polsbroek and Blokland respectively. The Kracker compilation attributes both to German anti-aircraft fire. The 2./JG 26 target label varies across accounts. Keep both as outbound route cases without counting either as an Evere airfield loss or adding either to the existing combined friendly-fire figure. |
| **Brussels–Melsbroek, B-58** | A veteran account records the destruction of **No. 69 Squadron** aircrew member Stanley Hayward's Wellington. A published photograph caption places No. 69 Squadron Wellington wreckage at the field that afternoon. A No. 140 Squadron family history gives **five No. 69 Squadron ground crew killed and 25 injured**. This personnel figure remains secondary until checked against the ORB and casualty list. Bergström reports **47 British aircraft destroyed and 14 damaged**, a different scope or threshold from the Atlas's 35/9 table. The source does not establish how visiting U.S. aircraft were counted in each figure. | The SGLO extract has **ten JG 27 Werknummer entries** on the route and **734038**, 13./JG 54, at Alverna. The latter's cause is disputed. Nijmegen's loss entry cites anti-aircraft fire, while Kracker cites a No. 401 Squadron Spitfire. SGLO lists an Fw 190 A-8. The register and the competing attributions do not resolve the formation summary's 20 versus 21 losses. |
| **Metz–Frescaty, Y-34, and Étain, A-82** | The **368th Fighter Group** chronology identifies the **395th Fighter Squadron** as the unit with several aircraft destroyed or seriously damaged. It gives no count. The **365th Fighter Group** total remains 22 P-47s destroyed and 11 badly damaged, with the **386th Fighter Squadron** dispersal singled out as hit. The 386th's exact share is unavailable. Frederick Bealke's **412th Fighter Squadron** account is a 1988 retrospective, pages G-1–G-4. Its search index was readable, but the full page returned an error. | A German unit history gives **nine aircraft lost each for II. and IV./JG 53** on the Frescaty assignment, and **ten for III./JG 53** on the Étain assignment. These are reconstructed Gruppe figures, with no established link for the gap between the two nine-aircraft figures and the wider 19–20 Frescaty strike estimate. Lowe describes the **367th FS, 358th FG** intercepting III./JG 53 en route to Étain and claiming 13 destroyed, one probable and six damaged. The 366th FS also engaged. Keep these claims separate from the German loss count and from the Frescaty raid. On the Frescaty route, **13./JG 53's Bf 109 G-14/AS WNr 784993** is documented as force-landed after U.S. ground fire. It is an aircraft-level example outside the Dutch extract. |

## Evidence rules and open reconciliations

- The extract transcribes the register's dated serial, unit and crash-location fields from printed pages **126–129**. An asterisk in the serial column is retained as printed. Only explicit ground-destruction entries are classified that way. The other entries retain a general register classification, even when an individual cause is suggested by a separate source. The sample is geographically selective and excludes entries without usable serials.
- The Dutch register incorporates specialist research, including contributions associated with Bodenplatte. Matching a register row to a later compilation can strengthen identification but does not automatically provide independent corroboration of loss cause. The two JG 26 friendly-fire cases and Köpp's conflicting cause are preserved at that evidence level.
- Earlier Grok research supplied group-level totals, the formation list and gaps. This audit adds source locators, Staffel- and squadron-level identities, and aircraft cases. It leaves the Evere station totals, the Melsbroek return discrepancy, the full German friendly-fire count, and the two interpretations of **305 destroyed / 190 damaged** open.
- The research does not assign individual German losses to Allied victory claims without a compatible time, location, aircraft and source trail.
