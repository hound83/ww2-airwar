# Western air war in May and July 1944

Atlas v9.3 extended the timeline through 31 July with 113 new dated records. The follow-up adds ten more and brings the timeline to 2,103. It completes the nine July loss columns, expands the May worksheet coverage and adds Ninth Air Force evidence. The target extract now contains 635 rows summarized in 56 timeline entries. The summaries and operation reports can cover the same aircraft.

## German loss returns

The July Luftflotte 3 return covers all aircraft types in the Western Front reporting command. Missing aircraft are included in enemy-action destruction. Air combat is also a subset of enemy action. A visual recheck of all 31 dates recovered the remaining cells on 9, 10, 16, 24, 25 and 30 July. Empty cause cells within the dated tables are zero. The 10 July enemy-action destruction subtotal reads three. Its other-operational damage subtotal is overtyped. The aircraft rows give five Fw 190s and two Ju 88s, yielding seven.

Seven columns agree with the printed summary. The daily air-combat totals exceed the monthly destruction figure by one and fall one below the monthly damage figure. Both readings are preserved. The 24 July return gives 29 enemy-action destructions, including 14 missing, seven destroyed in air combat and eight destroyed on the ground. The 25 July return gives 33 enemy-action destructions, including 11 missing and 22 destroyed in air combat. Its single-engined Bf 109 and Fw 190 rows account for 15 and four of the 22. The command report has no individual airframe or escort-encounter attribution. ## Davis workbook extraction

The Air University CD-ROM supplement supplies dated target observations rather than complete mission dispatch figures. Column G counts aircraft reported attacking. Under the AAF convention, lost aircraft and their bomb loads are included in the attacking force. Bomb weight uses U.S. short tons. The export preserves date, country, force, reported target, sighting method, attacking and lost counts, bomb types, total load, mission, area and the original spreadsheet row. Target spelling follows the worksheet.

| Worksheet | Date window | Country codes and rows | Distinct mission dates and numbers |
| --- | --- | --- | ---: |
| 1944-1 | May | France 93, Germany 132, Belgium 23, Netherlands 6 | 28 |
| 1944-2 | July | France 163, Germany 208, Belgium 5, Netherlands 5 | 28 |

The follow-up reads all 4,330 rows of the first worksheet and all 5,036 of the second, including their header rows. It selects numbered Eighth Air Force missions with a positive attacking count and bomb load, a recorded loss cell, and a country code for France, Belgium, Germany or the Netherlands. This adds 132 German and six Dutch May targets. Those rows report 8,328 aircraft attacks and 264 losses under the worksheet's conventions. The July extraction reconciles with the original 381 rows. The mission summaries use the worksheet's reported total. At Precy (1944-1 row 450) and Mittweida (1944-2 row 320), the bomb components differ from the printed total. Both columns retain the workbook values. Other forces, countries and non-bombing tasks are outside this extract.

## Archive follow-up

- CARL N5160 is the 54-page Eighth Air Force tactical report for Missions 492 and 494. The catalogue identifies Field Orders 913 and 917 and the statistics annex. The download remains unavailable through the tested routes. For the Ninth Air Force, the official history cites the July IX Bomber Command history, the 24 and 25 July Ninth Air Force operations journal and the July IX Tactical Air Command summary. AFHRA file 168.6005-85 is the command statistical summary covering October 1943 to May 1945. [N5160 catalogue](https://cgsc.contentdm.oclc.org/digital/collection/p4013coll8/id/4574/) · [AAF official history, ch. 8 notes](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/AAF/III/AAF-III-8.html) · [AFHRA file reference, p. 66](https://www.airuniversity.af.edu/Portals/10/AUPress/Papers/cp_0011_zentner_art_of_wing_leadership.pdf)
- The Eighth Air Force's report on 24–25 July dated 11 September 1944 is cited in the Carl Spaatz Papers, Library of Congress, Part I, Box 168. A second tactical bombing report is cited in Box 84. The Todd investigation is a separate NARA lead in RG 243, Box 71. [Spaatz Papers finding aid](https://findingaids.loc.gov/repositories/19/resources/2497) · [After D-Day, ch. 5 notes 35, 55 and 58](https://dokumen.pub/after-d-day-operation-cobra-and-the-normandy-breakout-9781626372856.html)
