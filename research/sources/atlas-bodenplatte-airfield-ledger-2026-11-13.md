# Operation Bodenplatte airfield and unit ledger

The ledger covers 1 January 1945 and five Allied airfields in the Netherlands, Belgium and France. It contains 70 airfield or formation observations and 17 operation-wide observations. The five target locations are approximate. The existing campaign remains the overview for the wider raid.

## What the sources establish

| Airfield | Best local anchor | How the German figures relate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Eindhoven, B-78 | The Canadian official history, pp. 341–343, describes 16 Typhoons of 438 and 440 Squadrons at takeoff, two shot down after lifting off and 14 hit on the ground. The latter account does not give a write-off split. Aircrew Remembered identifies 438 Squadron Typhoon PD503. | The 15 losses from 60 JG 3 aircraft refer to the unit’s assignment. The 53 single-engine and 11 twin-engine aircraft are German ground claims. The published station pairs 43/60 and 47/43 have unresolved coverage. |
| Asch, Y-29 | The 366th Fighter Association records eight 390th Squadron Thunderbolts airborne and twelve 487th Squadron Mustangs taking off. The published reconstruction gives the 487th zero air losses, two damaged in the air and one on the ground. | The reconstructed 28 JG 11 losses from 61 aircraft cover its full route, including the Ophoven diversion. A local crash register gives WNr 171742 for a 3./JG 11 Fw 190 near Opglabbeek. A matched U.S. claim and a base-wide Allied total remain open. |
| Evere, B-56 | The Canadian official history, p. 343, groups 24 affected Spitfires of about 60 at the base. One airborne and 11 ground Spitfire losses come from a separate reconstruction. The published 34/29 table and 60–61 narrative figures refer to a wider and incompletely reconciled airfield population. | II./JG 26 and III./JG 26 have separate Evere loss rows. The three German-flak losses are associated with I./JG 26 and III./JG 54 on the separate Grimbergen route. The Canadian official history’s mixed-group pilot sentence cannot be used to allocate individual German airframes to Evere. |
| Melsbroek, B-58 | The published table gives 35 destroyed and nine severely damaged. Unit evidence covers Wellingtons, Spitfires, Mosquitoes, Harrows and Mitchells. Stanley Hayward’s account independently establishes the ground destruction of his repaired Wellington. The published seven Harrows are a lower bound for aircraft put out of action. | The mission table gives 21 losses. The unit narrative gives 17 JG 27 and three IV./JG 54 losses, which sum to 20. Seven outbound losses are jointly attributed to Allied aircraft and German flak. The source gives no usable division by cause. |
| Metz–Frescaty, Y-34 / A-90 | The Smithsonian account and Bergström give 22 destroyed and 11 badly damaged P-47s of the 365th Fighter Group. Bergström notes additional 368th Fighter Group losses without a usable count. | JG 53’s 30 lost and eight damaged cover both assignments. Two secondary accounts give 19 or 20 losses on the Frescaty assignment. The RAF journal’s case 102 records an Allied Typhoon lost to a U.S. P-51, a separate friendly-fire event. |

## Operation-wide reconciliation

- The Canadian official history gives 127 destroyed and 133 damaged for Second Tactical Air Force. Rickard gives approximately 173 destroyed and 137 damaged on the ground for a broader RAF/USAAF report compilation with incomplete U.S. coverage. The Atlas campaign’s existing RAF Museum pair of 224 destroyed and 84 damaged beyond unit repair uses its own scope and damage threshold.
- The operation article gives 290 destroyed and 180 damaged on the ground, plus 15 and 10 in the air, yielding 305 and 190. The separate loss-statistics summary cites 305 and 190, then presents the 15 and 10 as additional. Both readings are retained with their locators. The underlying Manrho and Pütz Allied appendices are required to reconcile that discrepancy.
- The existing Manrho and Pütz aircraft breakdown remains 271 single-engine fighters destroyed and 65 damaged, plus nine twin-engine aircraft destroyed and four damaged. The same indirect source attributes at least 17 losses to German flak with certainty. The Canadian official history’s older one-third attribution uses a different and less specific reconstruction. The ledger does not distribute campaign-wide friendly fire across the five fields.
- A numeric zero is reserved for an explicit source statement. An unknown count remains null. Ranges and lower bounds retain their qualifier. The combined, local and individual rows overlap. No campaign total is derived by summing them.

## Grok research integration

The two latest source passes were already on main at [2801267](https://github.com/hound83/ww2-airwar/commit/2801267629d3d4e6f497c78e6826be2c3b418ddd) and [0d0b6ed](https://github.com/hound83/ww2-airwar/commit/0d0b6ed0024f885ee039a668152c8407aa994c28). PR #45 had already surfaced the four specialist German aircraft figures. This ledger checks and incorporates the supported Asch, Evere, Melsbroek and Metz figures and the Eindhoven claims. The Eindhoven scope follows the Canadian official history and the published station and wing accounts. The notes’ 305/190 ambiguity is preserved and the 30/8 German Metz figures apply to the full JG 53 sector. The dated Westerly and Eastern Front leads in those commits have their own research notes.

The remaining archival targets are the Manrho and Pütz airframe appendices, the Allied operational record books for the five fields and the JG 11, JG 26, JG 27, JG 53 and JG 54 individual German returns. The captured formations and named airframes provide starting points. A complete serial-by-serial ledger and a matched air-to-air exchange ratio remain unsupported.
