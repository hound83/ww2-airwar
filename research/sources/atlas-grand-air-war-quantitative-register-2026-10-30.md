# Atlas: Grand Air War quantitative research register

Research began 30 October 2026. Scope: **Europe, 1936–May 1945**, all belligerent air services and air branches, not just fighter aircraft. Covers aircraft strength, serviceability, unit order of battle and location, sortie output, combat claims, aircraft losses by cause/type, and aircrew/personnel casualties. **This is a research register, not an assertion that the live Atlas has these data.**

## Known contradictions and semantic traps

1. **Soviet 1944 aircraft-loss populations:** annual VVS KA combat-by-type series in [GA-01 Table 06-21](https://www.rkka.es/Estadisticas/VVS_stat/06/06_21.htm) reports **7,861**; [separate 1944 writeoff table](https://www.rkka.es/Estadisticas/VVS_stat/06/agregado_otros_documentos/bajas_totales_1944.htm) reports **8,036** combat/missing written off, **3,750** accident and **7,593** wear/obsolete (19,379 overall). **175 difference in 1944 combat**, and these are **not additive independent loss populations**. Audit original footnotes, scope, cutoff and type before selecting.
2. **USAAF all-theater dead count:** Statistical Digest Table 34 says **40,061** died, whereas [official Army Air Forces history Vol. VII ch. 13](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/AAF/VII/AAF-VII-13.html) is reported as **40,451** killed. Could be cutoff/definition/revision; no automatic correction. ETO **Table 36** all personnel **63,410 battle casualties** (19,876 died, 8,413 wounded/evacuated, 35,121 missing/interned/captured) and MTO **Table 37** 31,155 casualties are **not pilot fatality totals**.
3. **Soviet pilot deaths vs personnel deaths:** Krivosheev-derived tables have dissimilar definitions (pilots vs wider staff; casualties vs killed/missing; calendar and cohort). Preserve role and fate codes, don't add dissimilar counts.
4. **Claims are a *different object* from loss:** British AIR 50, Polish Bajana, Romanian government claimed victories, Soviet pilot ace lists and German claims may refer to the same aircraft, shared credits, or exaggerated observations. Maintain `claim_id` linked to potential loss incident, but never make `claim_total === enemy_loss_total`.
5. **Finnish unit casualties:** Official individual-record code `S` means death not caused by enemy action. Our **477** squadron-linked deaths include **210 `S`**, and the source has no direct pilot/aircrew role in the extract; presenting all 477 as fighter-pilot KIA would be seriously wrong. Match by precise `LLv`/`Le.Lv`, not generic text containing `Ilm` (e.g. **P/L Ilmarinen** was a ship).
6. **Timing and front:** Luftwaffe reported-date vs event-date, delayed corrections; Soviet annual air army migrating between fronts; Romania switching sides 23Aug1944; Greece/Poland/Free France/Commonwealth under RAF affiliation. Each unit needs a **dated parent command and theater**, otherwise aggregation can double-count or misplace sorties on the map.
7. **Publication rights:** numeric historical facts can be independently researched and cited. Government open data such as Finnish CC BY4 and Dutch RCE CC0 can be directly processed with attribution; other specialist commercial datasets, scans, images and full original editorial databases require rights-by-source examination. The catalogue is a lead, not a bundled clone.

## What the Atlas needs before claiming comprehensive coverage

The current displayed dataset is strong on a few fighter/Western Front checkpoints, but weak on **bombers, ground attack, reconnaissance, transport, naval/coastal, Mediterranean, East, Spanish prelude and smaller air forces**. Build a normalized ledger with these minimal record types (all allow **unknown/missing** values):

1. `unit`, `unit_alias`, `unit_parent_at_date`, `belligerent_alignment_at_date`; never identify only from a short numeric code.
2. `strength_return`: date, unit/theater, role/type/variant, establishment, on-hand, serviceable, operational crews, provenance.
3. `operation`: event date/time, unit, actual sorties versus missions, mission class, origin/target geography, source, confidence; counters for day/night.
4. `aircraft_incident`: type/serial, event/report dates, unit, damage/writeoff, combat/ground/accident/wear, crew fate, corroborating archival IDs.
5. `personnel_casualty`: unit, role (pilot, gunner, navigator, ground crew, unknown), date, died/wounded/missing/POW/returned/unknown, linked aircraft when known.
6. `claim`: reporting unit/person, claimed enemy type, claim date/location, destroyed/probable/damaged, original status, optional independently matched actual incident.
7. `source_observation`: original text/page/folio, version, compiler, rights, exact force+period+geographic **denominator**, recorded/compiled/estimated/modelled/missing confidence.

**Coverage discipline:** a date or unit is displayed only when actual observations exist. Unverified population totals must stay in research rather than live dashboards; unanswered weeks/months must stay blank. Use source-linked crosswalks to roll unit → group/air army → theater → force; de-duplicate before showing nation totals; make both `geographic theater` and `national/service` selectable without counting Commonwealth service twice.

## Recommended extraction sequence (research, not UI implementation)

1. **Immediate quantitative bulk with clean provenance:** finish RKKA.es Part 2 and 6 (air armies 1941–45, sortie mission roles, units & readiness, ground/aircraft losses, PVO/VMF/ADD), digitize USAAF Statistical Digest Tables 24/36/37/84/119/120, and review already imported rows first. Validate RKKA against Soviet 1962 original. Material already freely online.
2. **Deep pilot-scale unit test:** one complete campaign/month in RAF AIR 27/50, German Generalquartiermeister and Eighth AF mission/MACR records, with claims-versus-reported-loss reconciliation. Use geographic units and pilots only when verified. This establishes the import schema before a nationwide event flood.
3. **Broaden both axes:** Italian 1940–43 / post-Armistice, French 1940, Spain 1936–39, Romanian & Hungarian 1941–45, Poland, Greece and Finland. Extend 335 Squadron and Finnish 20 provisional squadrons from original diaries. Include Coastal/FAA and Mediterranean/Scandinavia.
4. **All forces access queue:** NIMH, Belgian museum, Yugoslav, Slovak, RCAF, RAAF, RNZAF, SAAF, Bulgarian and Free French archives; record which require access, payment or in-person research. Do not fill uncovered forces with zero.
5. **Only then** design a true *Grand Air War* map/timeline: dated unit stationing, fronts and theaters; aircraft on hand vs ready; sortie and loss rates; toggle self-reported claims versus verified incidents; source fidelity always visible.

### Minimal audit checklist for future transcriptions

- The exact unit, parent, mission type, date/window and operational theater are explicit.
- The unit count, category count and grand total mathematically reconcile, or the source discrepancy is logged.
- Loss fate and cause are not guessed, enemy claims never substituted for independently verified losses.
- Original URL, scan page/folio when available, transcription revision and rights are recorded.
- The first usable source is not assumed to be complete: check where the personnel, reserve, naval, PVO, POW and support branches went.
- No sample or derivative is promoted to public charts until its unit aliases, definitions and chronology survive cross-source checking.

## Original USAF THOR 1939–1945 air-operations dataset: map-target coverage and field audit

**Major new operational-map source.** The U.S. Air Force Research Institute's original **Theater History of Operations Reports (THOR) World War II** database, developed by Lt Col Jenns Robertson from the U.S. Strategic Bombing Survey, USAAF Evaluation Board and daily mission files, is now preserved on [GeneralStaff.org as the original 2013 CSV ZIP](https://www.generalstaff.org/THOR/THOR_WW2.zip) (6.2 MB compressed, 83.6 MB CSV); see its [rehosting/source description](https://www.generalstaff.org/THOR/THOR.htm), the [official USAF 2013 project announcement](https://www.af.mil/News/Article-Display/Article/466817/historic-airpower-database-now-online/), and especially the developer's [59-page THOR data dictionary](https://programminghistorian.org/assets/visualizing-with-bokeh/THOR-draft-data-dictionary-dec-2016.pdf). The original **178,742 individual CSV rows** contain dates, target names/coordinates, country, theater, aircraft types, force names, numbered air force, and sometimes aircraft counts, missions, units, takeoff bases/coordinates, losses and damage. **These are source *records*, not unique aircraft sorties:** multiple targets and munitions may create multiple rows for a single flight; `Sortie_Dupe` is intended as a de-duplication indicator but the 2013 file often leaves it blank. The original developers explicitly warn of inaccurate inflated *enemy aircraft destroyed* claim fields and coordinate-resolution limits. There is no justification for treating the whole CSV as validated aircraft claims, crew losses, daily sorties, unit order of battle or a Russian/German complete record.

The reproducible [original-CSV audit script](../../scripts/audit-thor-ww2-operational-geodata.py), run against the downloaded 2013 archive, created the [derived map and field audit](../transcriptions/thor-usaf-original-ww2-eto-mto-record-and-map-coverage-audit.json). ## RAF Coastal Command aircrew death causes and distinct Royal Navy Fleet Air Arm


The original **RAF Coastal Command AIR 15/162, Table I** is reproduced as Appendix 21 of Andrew Hendrie's Kent PhD thesis, [*Coastal Command 1939–1945: The Cinderella Service*](https://kar.kent.ac.uk/86293/1/404542.pdf). Its [normalized casualty ledger](../transcriptions/raf-coastal-command-aircrew-groundcrew-casualties-original-air15-162-1939-1945.json) separates aircrew/groundcrew and combat flying/accident/ground combat/other ground causes for **3 Sep 1939–8 May 1945**, amended through **31 May 1947**: **8,874 recorded killed** (**8,180 aircrew +694 groundcrew**); **141 missing** (of which 136 subsequently safe); **516 recorded POW** (515 subsequently safe); **2,601 wounded**. Another **23 aircrew +224 groundcrew natural-cause deaths** are explicitly **outside** that original 8,874 killed table. The **8,180 are all aircrew roles, not pilot deaths**; and the parenthetical later-safe entries are **included in**, not additional to, the missing/POW columns. Host RAF, Canadian, Australian, New Zealand and other allied personnel overlap national counts; Coastal activities are not all geographically European.

**Fleet Air Arm must appear as a separately identifiable Royal Navy aviation service:** David Hobbs's [*The Fleet Air Arm and the War in Europe* (Naval Institute Press 2022)](https://www.usni.org/press/books/fleet-air-arm-and-war-europe) cites **406 pilots and 232 frontline aircraft available in Sep 1939** and **3,243 pilots and 1,336 aircraft at the end of the war in Europe**. The two endpoints have **different aircraft availability definitions**, and the May 1945 whole-FAA force includes aircraft outside the European theater; not a valid Europe-only growth series. Original [UK TNA Fleet Air Arm research guidance](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/fleet-air-arm-personnel/) lists **ADM 207 squadron diaries**, [ADM 199 naval operations and ADM 335/1–4/63–65 FAA histories](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/royal-navy-operations-second-world-war/), plus [AIR 50 pilot/air-gunner combat claims](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/help-with-your-research/research-guides/raf-combat-reports-1939-1945/). The [structured FAA source lead](../transcriptions/royal-navy-fleet-air-arm-1939-1945-strength-and-original-record-leads.json) has **no invented sortie, claim, casualty or map position data**.

## New original USAAF sources: group stationing and aircraft victories vs own losses

**Wartime group stationing, Army Air Force Combat Units (1961).** U.S. Air Force Historical Division, Maurer Maurer (ed.), *Air Force Combat Units of World War II*, government-published official group histories. Public-domain scans: [part 1, 274 PDF pages](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/AAF/AAFHS/AAFHS-140.1.pdf) and [part 2, 246 PDF pages](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/AAF/AAFHS/AAFHS-140.2.pdf). Search-index verification against the [official DAF publication](https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/Portals/16/documents/Studies/101-150/AFD-090529-055.pdf) and [Library of Congress record](https://www.loc.gov/item/61060979). These primary-derived unit group histories give full **dated station sequences**, squadron membership, aircraft types, campaigns, and mission roles. A reproducible local scan/OCR extraction (not bulk-copying a proprietary airfield directory) found **292 detected printed group headings**, **144 groups with at least one western European/Mediterranean station**, **741 raw station candidates**, **655 candidates with parsable date token**, **544 dated within Atlas 1936–May1945 scope**, and **44 source OCR/extraction anomalies**; see [research-only candidate register](../transcriptions/usaaf-maurer-public-domain-european-group-station-candidates-1940-1945.json) and [extractor](../../scripts/extract-usaaf-public-domain-group-stations.py). Do not publish coordinates from these names before identifying the historical station and cross-checking the date. The OCR often substitutes `I` for `1`; e.g. 317th Troop Carrier Group's **post-1948 German bases** are caught by the wartime filter and must not appear in a 1944 map. Some groups have multiple OCR `STATIONS` headings, retained as anomalies, not resolved silently. A named station does not prove the group's combat missions took off there or that all squadrons were present simultaneously.

**USAAF Statistical Digest original scanned operations pages.** [1945 original official USAAF Statistical Digest scan](https://archive.org/download/ArmyAirForcesStatisticalDigestWorldWarII/ArmyAirForcesStatisticalDigestWorldWarII.pdf) (337 PDF pages), transcribed directly on printed pages **255–256** (Tables 159/160, original combat-mission own aircraft losses) and **262–264** (Tables 166/167/168, enemy aircraft *reported destroyed*). See [transcribed annual/role-ledger](../transcriptions/usaaf-official-eto-mto-actual-combat-mission-aircraft-losses-and-reported-victories-1942-1945.json). All arithmetic is tested; no assumption that American claims are independently verified German losses:

| Own losses attributed to enemy aircraft | 4,274 | 2,526 |

The official table's MTO 9,497 includes **69 claimed enemy aircraft** attributed to *unidentified USAAF aircraft type*, 15 air and 54 ground; annual aircraft-role cells sum **9,428**, not 9,497, and this difference must **never** be silently added to fighter claims. ETO role totals: B-17/B-24 heavy bombers 6,098 claimed air victories, medium/light bombers 103, fighters 14,218 (7,422 in air, 6,796 ground). Original source period is **calendar May 1945**, potentially beyond Atlas's 9 May cutoff; it cannot be exactly truncated without daily records.

**USAF Historical Study 85, 1978, pilot/group-level claim credit lead.** Original [*USAF Credits for the Destruction of Enemy Aircraft, World War II*](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/AAF/AAFHS/AAFHS-85.pdf), **687-page scan**. The [USAF Historical Research Agency introduction](https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/Sandbox/Fact-Sheets/Display/Article/433007/world-war-ii-aerial-victory-credits/) states that individual credit records store fighter pilot/crew name, unit, theatre, date, and fractional awards; credits were **not** issued systematically to heavy bomber gunners, night crews can count more than one credit per destroyed plane, and a pilot credit total is thus neither national enemy aircraft claims nor independently confirmed enemy losses. Original scan is image-only, with uneven OCR of small typed columns. Useful future extraction only with rigorous page and row-level verification, not automatically imported into public data.

**Additional official leads:** [USAF original WWII Combat Squadrons, 841 pages](https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/Portals/16/documents/Studies/51-100/AFD-090601-110.pdf) has **1,226 squadron lineages**, with time-specific locations, aircraft and parent groups, providing a finer-grained map layer than group stations. [USAAF 1941–45 Combat Chronology](https://www.toscananovecento.it/wp-content/uploads/2018/02/Combat-Chronology-of-USAAF-1944-1945.pdf) has **dated flights, bombing targets, separate bomber/fighter losses and sometimes daily claims**, and the [Eighth AF 1942–45 detailed operation summaries](https://b24.net/8thAAFcombat.htm) contain mission-date records including pilots KIA/WIA/MIA, dispatch and returns. Use the original chronology wherever possible; the specialist republication is a secondary research lead with possible usage restrictions.

## Canadian claim register, RAF full-command series, and smaller national air forces

**RCAF Association claim register, all-years pilot/unit source.** The [Royal Canadian Air Force Association *RCAF Combat Claims*](https://www.rcafassociation.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/RCAF-Combat-Claims.pdf) is a **176-page, June 2026 secondary compilation**. Contrary to every page's misleading “1939–40” header, the document includes WWII through **4 May 1945** and a later **postwar** section. Its rows supply reported squadron, exact claim date, named aircrew, affiliation to RAF/FAA/RCAF, claimed enemy type/status (destroyed, damaged, probable), claimed geographic place, fractions/shared credits, and citations to pilots' historical accounts. Machine extraction of the original PDF text detected **4,238 dated entry lines**, of which **4,211** fall between 1 September 1939 and 9 May 1945, and **27** fall outside. We resolved squadron designations in **3,951 / 4,211 dated wartime rows**, belonging to **131 distinct numeric squadron labels**. See [derived, *non-publication-ready* year/squadron counts](../transcriptions/rcaf-association-derived-annual-squadron-claims-entry-counts-1939-1945.json) and [reproducible claim-list counting script](../../scripts/aggregate-rcaf-association-claims-register.py). **These are claims-register row counts, not 4,211 verified enemy aircraft destroyed, not unique claims, and not exclusively the European theatre**: the compilation includes 1941–42 Asian/Pacific activity, so this preparatory cross-reference census **must not** feed national European totals or map without theatre filtering and entry deduplication. The data archive intentionally stores derived aggregates rather than reproducing the newly compiled individual-person register. Duplicate or contradictory attribution, fractional credits, uncertainty and same-day multi-person claims require manual case checks against enemy aircraft actual loss reports. RAF-hosted Canadian personnel must not also be added as a separate Canadian air-force operational total.

**Canadian POW personnel fate lead.** The Association's separate [62-page *RCAF Prisoners of War*](https://www.rcafassociation.ca/wp-content/uploads/2026/06/RCAF-PoWs-Second-World-War.pdf) is an alphabetic list with POW identifiers, names, ranks, service numbers, hometowns, **but generally not squadron or event date**. It explicitly warns that no 1945 POW list was available and that some previously repatriated POWs were left out of official lists. Its cover header specifies **10 December 1944**, while its explanatory prose says “end of December 1944”; retain this internal time-window conflict. **POW is not KIA**, and this list is a person-linking research lead, not a casualty time series suitable for maps or pilot death counts. The linked [Bomber Command Museum of Canada POW list](https://bombercommandmuseumarchives.ca/canadaairwar/pow_list.pdf) is another independent cross-match lead.

**All RAF commands: annual sorties and own-aircraft losses.** [Rodrigo Aguilera's World Wars.net *Statistical Summary of the RAF*](https://www.theworldwars.net/stats/file.php?e=ww2&s=rafsummary), citing *Jane's Fighting Aircraft of World War II*, publishes annual rows for **six commands** with a printed overall **2,336,303 sorties and 22,220 aircraft losses**. Transcribed to [source-separated records](../transcriptions/raf-all-commands-secondary-annual-sorties-and-aircraft-losses-1939-1945.json). Bomber Command **392,137 / 9,163**, Fighter Command **700,226 / 3,558**, 2nd Tactical AF **327,713 / 2,115**, Coastal Command **235,749 / 1,579** printed, Mediterranean & Middle East **676,004 / 5,735**, Army Cooperation Command **4,474 / 70** (sorties / aircraft losses). The secondary source itself says *Bomber Command includes aircraft operating under Coastal Command*, so command sum may count overlaps and is not independently demonstrated to be a distinct-aircraft population. **Important original arithmetic problem:** the online Coastal annual loss entries sum to **1,539**, whereas the same author's command header prints **1,579**, a mismatch of **40**; both are preserved, not silently reconciled. The RAF's different original Coastal Command retrospective gives **238,313 sorties and 1,511 lost by enemy action/missing** through 8 May 1945; definitions and scopes differ. Secondary 1945 rows also lack a verified 9 May cutoff.

The [2025 research edition and analysis by Basilio Di Martino, *The Regia Aeronautica in September 1942*](https://www.nam-sism.org/Articoli/Articoli%202025/Nam%2024%20articoli/NAM24_art20_Insight_DiMartino_AirForce_Disillusion_WarMoment.pdf) (original report circa August–September 1942 in AUSMA Monographs b.27 f.18) quotes detailed **Italian General Staff historical strength, loss-cause and personnel** statistics; digitized as [`regia-aeronautica-1940-1942-original-general-staff-strength-losses-personnel.json`](../transcriptions/regia-aeronautica-1940-1942-original-general-staff-strength-losses-personnel.json). | --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| Bombers (available) | 759 | 423 | 422 | 398 |
| Fighters (frontline ready) | 632 | 719 | 880 | 901 |

Over **10 June 1940–21 August 1942**, the original author reports **3,395 own aircraft writeoffs**, consisting of **715 air-combat casualties**, **1,409 on-ground/irreparable/unknown-cause combined**, and **1,271 accidents**. This is the clearest route to a complete, unit-resolved Italian readiness time series, but access and permissions for individual archival records need checking; the indexed **365** are archival files, not verified digitally retrieved fortnightly observations.

## Royal Australian, New Zealand and South African wartime airforces: European service versus RAF double counting

[`commonwealth-raaf-rnzaf-saaf-europe-unit-and-national-statistics-1939-1945.json`](../transcriptions/commonwealth-raaf-rnzaf-saaf-europe-unit-and-national-statistics-1939-1945.json) preserves each denominator, scope and historic host service:

- **RAAF**: [Australian Bureau of Statistics official *Year Book 1944–45*](https://www.abs.gov.au/ausstats/abs@.nsf/featurearticlesbyCatalogue/6174449DF6ACC670CA2569DE0020331A) states the **15 Australian-designated squadrons operating from the UK at European surrender had cumulatively 65,341 sorties**, 59,419 tons of bombs and **109 aircraft victory *claims***. This is not all European Australian flying: more than 60% of Australians joining RAF operational commands served in regular **RAF-numbered squadrons**. [Australian War Memorial](https://www.awm.gov.au/wartime/66/crusade) reports **5,397 Australian airmen killed in the European war**, including **4,050 serving in Bomber Command**. National casualties and host command operational counts overlap, not additive forces.
- **RNZAF / New Zealanders flying in RAF**: [New Zealand government's war history](https://teara.govt.nz/en/1966/wars-second-world-war/page-4) documents **3,285 European air personnel killed**, **350 Pacific**, **3,635 worldwide combined**; as of **October 1944**, **6,127** New Zealand personnel served in RAF (snapshot), **10,950** cumulative members, **568 prisoners** (not killed), **138 seriously injured**. A [separate NZ government memorial](https://nzhistory.govt.nz/memorial/hall-memories) lists **3,250 killed while specifically in RAF**, *not* necessarily the same force denominator as 3,285 European. It also supplies full-war RAF-hosted squadron sortie counts: **75(NZ): 8,150; 485: 10,717; 486: 11,019; 487: 3,112; 488: 2,899; 489: 2,380; 490: 463**. **Critical theater filter**: 490 served in **Sierra Leone / South Atlantic** and its 463 are excluded from European aggregate. No.488 was **originally a different Malaya/Singapore fighter unit in 1941–42 and was reconstituted for Europe June 1942**: do not add its Far Eastern service to the European 2,899. These seven RAF-hosted squadrons were **already within RAF operations**, not extra RNZAF national sorties.
- **SAAF**: [André Wessels, *Scientia Militaria* 2012](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/276187943_The_South_African_Air_Force_1920-2012_A_Review_of_its_History_and_an_Indication_of_its_Cultural_Heritage), records **181 Warsaw-supply sorties from Italian bases by 31/34 SAAF Squadrons in Aug–Sep 1944, 11 aircraft lost, 38 aircrew killed**. He also reports **17,271 SAAF personnel in Italy in September 1944**, and **~82,401 sorties since the North African campaign**, with geographic/time scope not demonstrably all European; cumulative **~140,000 worldwide sorties**, **2,319 active-service dead**, **~280 POW** must NOT be presented as Europe-only. [SAAF historical service site](https://www.saairforce.co.za/the-airforce/history/saaf/world-war-two-arrives) reports **44,417 service personnel in Dec 1944**, including **2,349 pilots** (not all Italy). The East African campaign is outside the European Atlas and is retained only as context. Unit station leads include Malta/Sicily, Italian airfields, Yugoslav supply operations and Gibraltar.

## Unit-level Fifteenth Air Force comparative evidence: Mediterranean fighter escorts, 1944–45

[Daniel Haulman, *Air & Space Power History*, winter 2022, Table 1 (printed p. 45)](https://afhistory.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/11/Winter2022_Issue-AllRevised.pdf) uses wartime mission records and official postwar victory-credit studies to compare four USAAF fighter groups for the **same June 1944–April 1945 eleven-month period**. Stored in [`usaaf-fifteenth-air-force-four-fighter-groups-missions-claims-escort-losses-1944-1945.json`](../transcriptions/usaaf-fifteenth-air-force-four-fighter-groups-missions-claims-escort-losses-1944-1945.json).

| Group | Group missions | Of which bomber-escort group missions | Recorded aerial victory credits | Escorted bombers lost to enemy aircraft while group participated |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| USAAF 31st Fighter Group | 379 | 184 | 278 | 49 |
| USAAF 52nd Fighter Group | 349 | 193 | 224.5 | 88 |
| USAAF 325th Fighter Group | 359 | 192 | 252 | 68 |
| USAAF 332nd Fighter Group | 312 | 179 | 94 | 27 |

**Do not sum the rightmost column:** the original author explicitly counts the *same individual bomber losses against multiple participating escort groups*, e.g. 15 bombers of the 5th Bombardment Wing lost at Memmingen on 18 July 1944 were entered against three groups. **Group missions are not individual-aircraft sorties.** Victory credits are claims, not verified actual German destruction; the bomber loss column is *escorted bombers*, NOT lost USAAF fighters. The article's Table 1 lists **66** 52nd Fighter Group escort missions encountering enemy aircraft, while adjacent prose gives **55**, an unresolved discrepancy recorded in the file. Article Tables **2–5** contain many uniquely identifiable US Missing Air Crew Report (**MACR**) numbers with bomber group/date cross-links that can be de-duplicated and eventually tied to aircraft incident/personnel casualty records. Italian station leads include San Severo, Mondolfo, Madna, Piagiolino, Lesina, Rimini and Ramitelli; they are station names **without verified coordinates or precise relocation dates**.

## Belgium 10 May 1940: unit OOB and the danger of plotting peacetime bases

The [U.S. Army Command and General Staff College's two-page order of battle](https://cgsc.contentdm.oclc.org/digital/api/collection/p15040coll6/id/4811/download) (the US-hosted table attributes its values to **Weal & Weal 1977**, hence **secondary-derived**) identifies **16 Belgian Aéronautique Militaire combat/army-cooperation squadrons**, linked to aircraft types, regiments and named bases: **6 army-cooperation units / 62 aircraft**, **6 nominal fighter-regiment units / 79 aircraft** (including obsolete Fairey Fox), **4 reconnaissance-bomber units / 41 aircraft**. These **182 selected squadron aircraft** are **not all national aircraft** and have **no serviceability column**. See [`belgian-air-force-squadron-order-of-battle-aircraft-and-conflicting-bases-1940-05-10.json`](../transcriptions/belgian-air-force-squadron-order-of-battle-aircraft-and-conflicting-bases-1940-05-10.json) for every regiment/squadron/type/base as well as conflicting counts and site candidates. The [official Belgian government's campaign account](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/UN/Belgium/Belgium_1939-40/Belgium-4.html) says **over half the Belgian machines** were destroyed on the ground during the morning attack; this qualitative conclusion cannot be transcribed into exactly 91 or any other aircraft loss count.

A [specialist site's alternative Belgian 1940 OOB](https://www.nevingtonwarmuseum.com/belgium-airforce.html) distinguishes *peacetime base* and wartime dispersion, e.g. **Deurne → Hingene**, **Gossoncourt/Goetsenhoven → Glabeek/Jeneffe/Lonzee**, **Schaffen → Beauvechain**, **Evere → Neerhespen/Belcele**. However, **five unit aircraft allocations conflict** with the army-hosted table, and two unit designations are variant; site also has visible historical errors. **The Atlas must map neither source's base column as fact until original Belgian station orders resolve where each squadron physically was on 10 May.** [Belgian Royal Military Museum archives](https://militarymuseum.be/en/archives) and post-1940 Belgian Armed Forces documentation centre in Evere are potential original record routes.

## Czechoslovak and Polish RAF-hosted units, operations, casualties and claims

[`polish-czechoslovak-raf-hosted-air-forces-squadrons-personnel-claims-1940-1945.json`](../transcriptions/polish-czechoslovak-raf-hosted-air-forces-squadrons-personnel-claims-1940-1945.json) compares national museum sources against the original forces and their host RAF command structures.

- **Polish Air Force in RAF, May 1945**: [Royal Air Force Museum](https://www.rafmuseum.org.uk/our-collection/supporting-research/online-exhibitions/polish-squadrons/) reports **15 Polish-designated squadrons**, **14,000 men and women**, **957 credited enemy-aircraft fighter victories**, and **14,708 tons of bombs/mines** by RAF-hosted Polish bomber and coastal units. A **different Polish Institute and General Sikorski Museum bilingual exhibition catalogue**, [PDF direct link](https://pism.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/IPMS_EKSPONATY_SPREADS_LR.pdf), reports **972 claimed aircraft destroyed, 196 probable, 278 damaged, 190 flying-bomb credits, 73,524 fighter combat missions and 122,816 flying hours**. The PDF is ~11 MB and exceeded the search viewer full-download limit; its indexed bilingual text was checked, but not the page facsimile. **957 vs 972** (difference 15) may reflect independent definitions of national credit, dates, branches, or updates, so neither becomes a single unqualified national enemy-aircraft-lost figure. The [RAF Museum 303 Squadron Battle of Britain exhibit](https://www.rafmuseum.org.uk/research/online-exhibitions/the-polish-air-force-in-world-war-2/303-squadron/) credits **126 destroyed claims for that squadron** out of **203 for Polish pilots across several units**, with **29 Polish pilots killed** in the campaign. Enemy victory *credits* are not independently proven German destroyed aircraft.
- **Czechoslovak 310 RAF**: [RAF Museum curated exhibit](https://www.rafmuseum.org.uk/research/online-exhibitions/czechoslovak-squadrons-in-raf/) and [Czech historical reenactment research collection](https://www.276.cz/en/perute-ceskoslovenskych-prislusniku-royal-air-force-2/) report **9,027 sorties**, **15,673 hours**, **54.5 destroyed claims**, **20 probable, 32 damaged**, **31 killed**, **4 POW**, plus **938 Battle of Britain sorties / 4 pilots dead**. A **15 June 1945 fatal training accident** is included in the all-unit narrative and **postdates Atlas 9 May cutoff**.
- **Czechoslovak 311 RAF (bomber to Coastal Command in 1942)**: **3,140 sorties**, **~27,000 flight hours**, **246 aircrew killed OR missing combined**, eight destroyed / seven probable enemy aircraft claimed by gunners, two enemy submarines reported sunk and three probable. The two RAF commands cannot be assigned the entire 3,140 without a dated role split; Coastal sorties are already RAF totals.
- **Czechoslovak 312 RAF**: **17,472 reported combat sorties / 10,364 flying hours**, **14 credited destroyed, 9 probable, 16 damaged**; **17 pilots killed, six captured**. A separate Jan Rajlich-derived secondary gives **17,427 sorties** (difference **45**). The unusually low **0.59 hours per sortie** warrants checking original RAF AIR27 ORBs. [Original Czech-language RAF Museum page](https://www.rafmuseum.org.uk/research/online-exhibitions/czechoslovak-squadrons-in-raf/312-czechoslovak-squadron-ch) says **16 damaged aircraft**, while English translation erroneously has **1**; the [Czech Defence Ministry's own 2025 312 Squadron history](https://valecnehroby.mo.gov.cz/aktuality/zformovani-312-ceskoslovenske-stihaci-perute-raf) confirms **16** and the 17,472 count. Source formation dates also vary **29 August versus 5 September 1940** (order vs establishment).
- **Czechoslovak 313 RAF**: **8,944 sorties / 13,760 hours**, **12 credited destroyed, seven probable, 14.5 damaged**, **30 killed/missing/POW combined** (not 30 dead); its narrative includes **7 June 1945** post-war flight beyond cutoff. **No.68 RAF's Czechoslovak B Flight**: **1,905 Czech-participation sorties / 4,095 hours**, **21 destroyed/three probable/seven damaged claims**, **23 Czech and Slovak pilots plus 16 radar operators** served in that flight; those are not the entire British RAF 68 Squadron's sorties. Summary of these *five rows* is 40,488 but is **NOT a validated 1940–9May1945 Czech total** for reasons above.
- The [Czech Ministry of Defence's official historical overview (2006)](https://www.mo.gov.cz/images/id_7001_8000/7420/crapa-en.pdf) broadly states **over 40,000 combat sorties and more than 500 Czech air-service personnel killed**; these national statements overlap the specific RAF squadrons and other British mixed units, do not add together. [British National Archives RAF AIR27 squadron Operations Record Books](https://discovery.nationalarchives.gov.uk/) are the most direct day-level check for contradictory lifetime sortie/hour numbers, personnel incidents and dates. Other [Czech military cemetery records](https://valecnehroby.mo.gov.cz/) can corroborate individual fates but won't identify every POW without cross-matching.
