# June 1944 loss returns and mission evidence

The January–June timeline contains 1,980 dated records. This release adds 60 German daily all-aircraft totals, 189 Bf 109 and Fw 190 records by aircraft role, and 12 formation, airframe and encounter entries. It also adds 12 Cobra observations and four source-labelled Bodenplatte figures to the campaign explorer. ## German returns

The transcription uses all 45 table pages in the two RAF translations of German returns. Luftflotte 3 covers PDF pp. 2–23 and Luftflotte Reich pp. 2–24. The day headings, continuation pages, unit-role labels and outcome columns were checked on the page images. Missing aircraft are a component of enemy-action destruction. The other operational and non-operational columns distinguish destruction from damage. Air-combat subcolumns are included for selected mission dates. These returns cover the named command areas. The type rows identify the unit role, such as single-engine fighter, ground attack, bomber, night fighter or reconnaissance. The table gives no individual formation number or airframe serial. ## Six mission decisions

| Operation | Evidence and decision |
| --- | --- |
| **6 June, Eighth Air Force Mission 394** | The wartime daily report gives 1,077 aircraft attacking in the first wave and 37 B-24s attacking Argentan in the second. It reports no fighter attacks on the bombers and attributes the second-wave loss of two B-24s to collision. The first-wave dispatch counts differ between the report (1,365) and chronology (1,425), and the second-wave dispatch/return counts remain unresolved. The 384th’s Mission 128 summary describes its own bomb releases in cloud. Luftflotte 3 records 31 enemy-action destructions, including 14 missing, and 14 explicit air-combat destructions command-wide on 6 June. The Allied fighter report also covers area patrols and sweeps. The sources provide no individual German loss match for either bomber wave. [Eighth Air Force daily report, §B.1 and §C.1](https://458bg.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Missions-458BG-056-06Jun44.pdf) · [384th Bomb Group, Mission 128](https://www.384thbombgroup.com/_content/_pages/One384thMission.php?MissionKey=137) |
| **6 June, Eighth Air Force Mission 395** | The Caen B-24s bombed at 13:28–13:29 and the fourth wave at 19:51–20:33, times as reported. The daily report gives no fighter attacks on these bomber formations. It records the fourth-wave B-24 loss as cause unknown. The 384th’s Mission 129 confirms its Caen assignment was within the fourth wave and that its 12 completed sorties did not bomb. Day-wide German losses remain contextual. [Eighth Air Force daily report, §B.1, waves 3–4](https://458bg.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/09/Missions-458BG-056-06Jun44.pdf) · [384th Bomb Group, Mission 129](https://www.384thbombgroup.com/_content/_pages/One384thMission.php?MissionKey=138) |
| **9 June, Fifteenth Air Force Munich operations** | The 55th Bombardment Wing records 112 airborne and 89 bombing. The 460th, 465th and 485th group narratives give 37, 27 and 25 aircraft bombing respectively. The 485th combat narrative calls five aircraft missing at that stage; the wing summary gives two missing for the wing. Under separate 304th Wing escort between 08:30 and 09:25, B-24s 42-78219 and 42-52318 were lost to fighters at 09:05 and 09:07 as logged. MACRs 6317 and 6179 document those losses. The 332d’s five aerial credits and the Luftflotte Reich area-wide Bf 109 air-combat count of two destroyed and two damaged are independently scoped. German fighter identities for the 459th losses remain unresolved. [55th BW Mission Summary 42](https://15thaf.org/55th_BW/Missions/June/9th/PDFs/Text%20Mission%20Summary.pdf) · [AFHRA study and MACR table](https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/Portals/16/documents/Studies/AFD-141118-047.pdf) |
| **12/13 June, RAF Gelsenkirchen Nordstern** | Appendix 10 records 294 dispatched, 276 attacking and 17 missing. The RAF diary summary reports 303 aircraft and 17 lost. No. 115 Squadron detailed eight aircraft. Seven bombed and returned, one with flak damage. HK545 took off at 23:32 and was missing. A secondary reconstruction connects the loss with Ernst-Wilhelm Modrow’s 1./NJG 1 claim at 01:31 northwest of Duisburg. The crash locality is Gelsenkirchen and the timing and claim location still need primary reconciliation. The Atlas labels the individual connection possible. [RAF AHB vol. VI, Appendix 10](https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/our-history/air-historical-branch/second-world-war-campaign-narratives/raf-in-the-bombing-offensive-against-germany-vol-vi-the-final-phase-march-1944-may-1945/) · [115 Squadron ORB excerpt](https://www.115squadron-raf.be/crews/Lancaster%20Mk%20I%20-%20HK545%20-%20KO-E.html) · [Boiten attribution via the loss investigation](https://aircrewremembered.com/shadforth-wallace-gordon.html) |
| **27 June, Esternay** | James Jure of the 61st Fighter Squadron, 56th Fighter Group, claimed two Bf 109s at 20:30–20:45 as logged. The command return gives 17 single-engine Bf 109s destroyed in air combat, five damaged and seven missing for the wider day and area. The existing secondary reconstruction compares 14 claims and nine German losses for I. and II./JG 27. The 15./JG 27 record for WNr 412607 gives a separate combat loss with no location or time and does not place it in Jure’s encounter. [Jure’s report](https://www.8af.org/james-jure-56th-fighter-group-27-june-1944.cfm) · [West PDF p. 21](https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/our-history/air-historical-branch/ahb-translations-from-captured-german-documents/luftwaffe-losses-on-the-western-front-luftflotte-3-june-1944/#page=21) · [JG 27 loss register](https://www.luftwaffe.be/missing-in-action-jg-27/) |
| **25 July, Cobra / Mission 494** | The Eighth dispatched 1,581 heavy bombers and 500 fighters, with 483 fighters escorting the Eighth’s heavies and Ninth Air Force B-26s. Air claims were 12 destroyed, one probable and three damaged. The 406th Fighter Group’s four claims and one missing over Lisieux belong to a separate Ninth Air Force encounter. The image-checked Luftflotte 3 return records 33 enemy-action destructions including 11 missing, of which 22 were explicitly destroyed in air combat. Within single-engine fighter units, 15 Bf 109s and four Fw 190s were destroyed in air combat. The separate German operational calendar gives ten losses in its own reporting scope. Neither German tally identifies the Mission 494 encounters. The current estimate of about eight remains low confidence. [Eighth Air Force chronology](https://aircrewremembered.com/USAAFCombatOperations/Jul.44.html) · [Craven and Cate, pp. 231–237](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/AAF/III/AAF-III-8.html) · [German return, PDF p. 22](https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/our-history/air-historical-branch/ahb-translations-from-captured-german-documents/luftwaffe-losses-on-the-western-front-luftflotte-3-1st-july-31st-august-1944/#page=22) |

For Cobra, 843 + 647 + 13 gives 1,503 attacking in the chronology. Craven and Cate give 1,507 attacking, 559 fighter-bombers delivering 212 tons plus napalm, and one medium and five heavy bomber losses to ground fire. The AAF investigation figures for short bombing on 25 July are 102 killed and 380 wounded. The U.S. Army history’s 111 and 490 remain visible as an alternate count. The observations give the source and population for each number. CARL N5160 identifies field orders 913 and 917 and annexes for routes, target plans, bombing, weather and statistics. The file-level statistics and companion *Special Report on Operations, 24 and 25 July 1944* remain outstanding for individual escort matches. [CARL catalogue](https://cgsc.contentdm.oclc.org/digital/collection/p4013coll8/id/4574/) · [AAF history, chapter 8 and notes 1, 8, 9 and 26](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/AAF/III/AAF-III-8.html)

## Grok source review

- The Cobra and blocked-source passes supplied the catalogue references and the chronology and official-history leads used above. The 483 figure is escort participation, and the 1,503 figure comes from the chronology’s three attack counts. The AAF campaign history gives 1,507. [Cobra attribution pass](atlas-cobra-mission-494-attribution-2026-11-12.md) · [Deeper extraction](atlas-deeper-extraction-blocked-sources-2026-11-12.md)
- The Bodenplatte explorer now shows the separately sourced Manrho and Pütz reconstruction of 271 Bf 109/Fw 190 destroyed and 65 damaged, plus nine twin-engine aircraft destroyed and four damaged. The four rows cite the accessible quotation of p. 273. The two pairs yield 280 destroyed and 69 damaged within this reconstruction. Accessible summaries disagree over whether 305 Allied destroyed and 190 damaged already include the 15 destroyed and ten damaged in the air. The Allied candidates stay in the research record pending the book pages and the airfield-by-airfield check. The pilot figures 143 killed, 70 captured and 21 wounded describe separate outcomes. The catalogue identifies it as a study of Allied operations and communications in Normandy. Individual document contents remain outstanding.
