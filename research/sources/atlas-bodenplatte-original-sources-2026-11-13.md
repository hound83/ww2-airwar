# Bodenplatte source audit, 13 November 2026

This follow-up covers the 1 January raid at Sint-Truiden (A-92) and Brussels–Melsbroek (B-58), the JG 4 assignment to Le Culot and the German assessment of the operation. The [evidence file](../transcriptions/bodenplatte-archival-evidence-1945-01-01.json) records 22 aircraft by serial or Werknummer, 38 reported observations, a named casualty and six archival leads. It supplements the [operation ledger](../transcriptions/bodenplatte-operation-scope-1944-1945.json). Some observations describe the same losses at different reporting levels.

## Sint-Truiden and Le Culot

| Evidence | What the source records |
| --- | --- |
| XXIX Tactical Air Command A-2 Periodic Report No. 90, annex, 2 January 1945 | Seven attackers between 09:20 and 09:45, with three or four passes. The A-92 return counts **10 combat and administrative aircraft destroyed and 31 damaged**. The report records four attacking aircraft destroyed, one additional destruction claim and one damage claim. It reports no air personnel killed or seriously wounded and says one antiaircraft soldier was believed killed and several were wounded. Maj. Robert W. Manss reproduced the annex in the January 1945 404th Fighter Group diary, which Lowe quotes. [Published reproduction](https://dokumen.pub/bf-109-jabo-units-in-the-west.html) |
| January 1945 404th Fighter Group diary | Manss says the group had no personnel injuries and repaired aircraft damage quickly. Lowe records both resident fighter groups flying later that day. Capt. Floyd Blair, 507th Fighter Squadron, recalls making his scheduled 11:44 departure. [Diary and testimony in Lowe](https://dokumen.pub/bf-109-jabo-units-in-the-west.html) |
| A-92 tactical excerpt in Frederick Bealke's 1988 work, pp. G-1–G-4 | **Six destroyed and 33 damaged**. The destroyed aircraft are two P-47s, one UC-78, one B-24 and two B-17s. The damage rows are 11 P-47s category AC, 16 P-47s category A, four command aircraft and two B-17s category AC. The indexed passage gives the same time window and attacker count. The full page returned an access error. [412th Fighter Squadron archive](https://412th.org/archives/Boden_Platte.html) |
| Squadron breakdown in Lowe | 492nd Fighter Squadron 3 P-47s destroyed and 10 damaged, 494th 2 and 4, 506th 1 and 4. A further statement gives five damaged across the 506th and 507th. The last two statements may overlap. Lowe also mentions heavy bombers already unserviceable when hit. [Specialist reconstruction](https://dokumen.pub/bf-109-jabo-units-in-the-west.html) |
| JG 4 monthly summary and the A-92 annex | Gerhard Michalski's German formation summary, reproduced in English by Lowe, says all Gruppen were assigned Le Culot, with one or two Ju 88 guides for each Gruppe. The U.S. annex cites documents taken from crashed aircraft to identify A-89 as the original target and records no attack on A-89 that day. Lowe describes IV./JG 4 diverting to A-92 with some II./JG 4 fighters joining it. Rickard's later narrative describes a few aircraft reaching Le Culot. A-89 and the Le Culot East auxiliary strip were separate. [Lowe and the wartime excerpts](https://dokumen.pub/bf-109-jabo-units-in-the-west.html) · [Rickard](https://www.historyofwar.org/articles/operation_bodenplatte.html) |

The 10/31 figure can now be attributed to a dated wartime command annex. The available extracts leave the difference from 6/33 unresolved. Exact aircraft inclusion, damage grading and whether the accounts reflect revisions still require the original records. The group-only personnel statement and the command account of antiaircraft casualties have different scopes.

Lowe identifies **Bf 109 G-14/AS WNr 461200**, Yellow 13, as Arnolf Russow's aircraft, forced down by flak near the field, with Russow captured. Lowe's caption provisionally identifies the photographed runway-side wreck as **Bf 109 K-4 WNr 331473**, flown by Horst Grüner of 15./JG 4, killed in the crash. [St Trond narrative and photo caption](https://dokumen.pub/bf-109-jabo-units-in-the-west.html)

## German operation-wide report and published estimates

The RAF Air Historical Branch cites **AHB5/234, Box 485**, *Attack on Allied Airfields on 1st January 1945*, for a German photographic-reconnaissance estimate of **479 Allied aircraft destroyed in air and ground combat and 114 damaged**. The official study is available. The cited translation and the German original were unlocated in the published translation index and public searches. The index's hyperlink numbered 234 opens conference translation VII/56. [RAF Air Defence, ch. 7, n. 34](https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/our-history/air-historical-branch/second-world-war-thematic-studies/ground-based-air-defences/) · [Published translation index](https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/our-history/air-historical-branch/ahb-german-translations/) · [Translation VII/56](https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/our-history/air-historical-branch/ahb-translations-from-captured-german-documents/extracts-from-reports-of-fuehrer-conferences-held-at-the-beginning-of-january-1945-on-10th-january-1945-and-on-27th-january-1945/)

The RAF study cites Manrho and Pütz (2010, pp. 460–462) for **290 destroyed and 180 damaged on the ground**. English Wikipedia's operation article cites the 2004 edition p. 272 for **305/190 including 15/10 in air combat**. Its aircraft-loss list describes 15/10 as additional to 305/190. The RAF citation supports the ground-only scope of 290/180. The exact specialist pages and air-combat annex remain necessary to resolve the total. The RAF study also names HQ 2 TAF Intelligence Summary **2TAF/30256/1/Int, 8 January 1945** and **Regiment in Action Report No. 34, 2 July 1945**, which differ in the number of German aircraft credited to the Regiment. [RAF Air Defence, nn. 34–35](https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/our-history/air-historical-branch/second-world-war-thematic-studies/ground-based-air-defences/) · [English operation results](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Operation_Bodenplatte#Results) · [Aircraft loss list](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/German_and_Allied_aircraft_losses_during_Operation_Bodenplatte) · [2004 edition catalogue](https://obnb.uk/p13044166-bodenplatte-the-luftwaffes-last-hope-the-attack-on-allied-airfields-new-years-day-1945)

## January returns archive access follow-up

The Dutch archive catalogue lists the relevant items as physical holdings. Public searches recovered a parallel UK squadron book and precise wing files. The 1 January raid-day leaves and appendix images remain uninspected. Use freely accessible copies, published extracts and reading-room access for further retrieval. Paid bulk ordering is outside scope. Any paid request for an individual item needs a separate decision. The published evidence above retains its existing scope and confidence.
