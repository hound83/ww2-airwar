# Deployment and evidence coverage across 20 European campaigns

**Finding:** v10.0 gives each campaign a sourced historical question. No campaign-wide, all-role, all-side deployment roster was established by this audit. Several narrower populations are recoverable in full. Readers currently encounter a mixture of command labels, biographies, selected operations and compiled lists under headings about formations. Their counts answer different questions.

## Campaign-by-campaign findings

The middle column states what can be defended for a date and force. The next column identifies material that a user could easily overread, including strengths, stations, sorties, losses and effects. Each linked chapter gives primary evidence, historical reconstructions and the original source locators. A source mentioned only in a catalogue is marked as a lead there.

| Campaign | Deployment scale or dated control | Completeness of other evidence; first missing comparison |
|---|---|---|
| [Spain](deployment-audit/early.md) | Opening of Brunete, 5–6 July 1937: six Republican and three opposing Fiat fighter escuadrillas. At Tremp in December 1938 two named Republican escort squadrons actually flew. | The dossier's two figures concern airlift and personnel. Identify current squadrons by front and reinforcements, then match the escorted or opposed action. German, Italian, Spanish and Soviet participation require separate identification. |
| [Poland](deployment-audit/early.md) | Warsaw, 1 September: five Polish fighter escadrilles in the reinforced Pursuit Brigade; the 152nd assisted separately. Two Slovak escort units are documented outside the Polish/German binary. | Dossier strengths and retrospective loss figures do not resolve German twin-engine Gruppe counts. Reconcile the three named Bf 110 Gruppen with the five in a broader table, and examine Polish army aviation, Slovak participation and the Soviet entry from 17 September separately. |
| [Norway](deployment-audit/early.md) | April X. Fliegerkorps assignment includes II./JG 77 and I./ZG 76; I./ZG 1 enters from 15 April. On 28 May two named RAF fighter squadrons flew 42 and 53 operational flights at Narvik. | Distinguish Weserübung assignments, Fornebu's Norwegian aircraft, the late northern expedition and naval aviation. Link dated German raid and shipboard reports to claims about anchorage protection. |
| [France and the Low Countries](deployment-audit/early.md) | JG 26 alone has three Gruppen on 10 May. In the British Air Component, four fighter squadrons on 9 May became six by the afternoon of 10 May and seven on 11 May. The AASF reported separately. | The four dossier figures cover RAF operations. Separate the French five-group Groupement 23 subset, Belgian, Dutch, German and RAF records. Show aircraft received, aircraft ready, missions and bridge effects over matching days. |
| [Battle of Britain](deployment-audit/west.md) | On 7 September Luftflotte 2 alone lists 21 single-engine fighter Gruppen, plus eight HQ elements. No. 11 Group alone lists 23 squadron identities. The day view identifies zero at those respective echelons. | Five of seven British campaign biographies overlap the 23 by name. Duty on that date still needs its own record. Separate serviceability from interceptions, operational losses from claims and London attack outcomes from fighter strength. |
| [Malta](deployment-audit/med.md) | January 1942 RAF Malta has three fighter squadrons plus No. 1435 Flight in the cited table. In March Fliegerkorps II has four single-engine fighter Gruppen in Sicily; individual names remain to be reconciled for that month. | Four linked British biographies cannot stand for a siege spanning 30 dossier months. Pair Italian and German raid records with British fighter availability, convoy arrivals and cargo unloaded at specific dates. |
| [Barbarossa](deployment-audit/east.md) | Four regiments belonged to the Soviet 14th Mixed Air Division on 22 June. Three were equipped fighter regiments. The fourth was still forming without aircraft. Three JG 54 Gruppen and attached JG 53 Staffeln belong to one German sector. | Four dossier episodes span seven months. Separate sector strengths, Soviet holdings, German intelligence estimates and victory claims. Add dated Romanian and Finnish formations and different entry dates. |
| [Dieppe](deployment-audit/west.md) | Allied allocation of 74 squadrons includes 48 Spitfire air-cover allocations; the German fighter response included JG 2 and JG 26, with two separately identifiable fighter-bomber Staffeln. | The paired day-level loss display is a strength. It gives little indication of unit assignments, sortie repetition, cover over the withdrawal or which ships suffered damage. Reconcile the role list with squadron and ship records. |
| [Stalingrad](deployment-audit/east.md) | The participant chips omit Romania. The Romanian Air Force reports 26 squadrons in the Stalingrad–Don Bend area at the end of September, including six fighter and two fighter-bomber squadrons. | A three-figure dossier on cargo planning and delivery misses this earlier combat phase. Later, match transports serviceable and dispatched to cargo actually received by Sixth Army. Separate dates, weather and contested airfields. |
| [Defence of the Reich](deployment-audit/reich.md) | The first German scramble on 6 March 1944 alone involved six Gruppen under three JG parents. The US escort report says 19 groups while its table contains 17 distinct group numbers across 22 entries. | The two headline figures in the general dossier are German monthly losses. The specialist studies go further. Resolve split groups and repeat sorties, then pair intercept records with bomber penetration, losses and target evidence. |
| [Sicily](deployment-audit/med.md) | Specialist selector matches **0/4** island-based German single-engine Gruppen on 10 July, **1/23** squadrons in five 211 Group wings and **2/5** groups in XII Air Support Command. No Italian unit is selectable; at least five Italian fighter Gruppi are identifiable for 9 July. | Separate island basing from mainland intervention. The six German island rows including HQs total 136 held, 55 serviceable. No. 92 Squadron documented escorts to Messina on 16 August; the case has no linked formations. Its diary can establish an escort, while ferry losses need independent evidence. |
| [Kursk](deployment-audit/east.md) | At the opening of Citadel the northern and southern German commands list eight numbered day-fighter Gruppen under four parents. A separate compilation introduces conflicting German group identities. | Nearly half of the 343-row register is Kursk, much of it concentrated on one formation. Two dossier strength estimates cannot establish fighter effort or losses by sector. Resolve German, Soviet and Hungarian populations before comparing outcomes. |
| [Schweinfurt](deployment-audit/reich.md) | The 14 October B-17 task-force tables name 16 groups; two further B-24 groups flew a diversion. In August, nine of those 16 B-17 groups were tasked to Schweinfurt and seven to Regensburg. | Preserve escorts, diversions, German command footprints and two different days. Resolve German destroyed, written-off and damaged figures before attributing loss ratios. Use industrial records to establish disruption. |
| [Mainland Italy](deployment-audit/med.md) | At Salerno on 9 September 1943 the German close-support table lists five day-fighter Gruppen plus other categories. On 20 August 1944 the German fighter category in Italy really contains only II./JG 77. | Four dossier figures cluster on the Cassino bombardment. Trace northward base movement and Italy's divided air arms, serviceability, weather, ground support and results across later phases. |
| [Big Week](deployment-audit/reich.md) | I. Jagdkorps counted 362 fighters employed on 20 February and 155 entering combat. On 24 February the cited Italian-based wing account identifies four bomb groups and four supporting fighter groups. | A campaign total for US bombers misses RAF night effort, the Fifteenth's diversions and opposing intercepts. Separate fighter employment, combat contacts and production/dispersal measurements. |
| [Normandy](deployment-audit/west.md) | D-Day's operation view shows four Allied commands and one German air fleet. A German wartime report records **21 fighter Gruppen transferred to the Western Front on 7–9 June**. | The 39 dossier episodes provide the richest chronology. Distinguish initial beach cover from reinforcement and missions flown inland. German command losses and Allied daily sorties cover different populations. Link ground requests and unit reports for dated effects. |
| [Bagration](deployment-audit/east.md) | Specialist index matches **5/5** Soviet front-line air-army commands in the allocation to four fronts. The 6th Air Army supported the later-active left wing. I./JG 51's station on 26 June is disputed. | No Soviet station is in the specialist station list. Use front records for ground-observed support, distinguish bombing/assault sorties from fighter cover and reconcile German movements by detachment. |
| [Market Garden](deployment-audit/west.md) | The Luftflotte 3 diary says six formations prepared on 17 September; three operated in the Arnhem–Nijmegen area. No. 271 Squadron reported 19 satisfactory glider releases from 24 detailed crews. | Neither return establishes all fighters or cargo received. Separate aircraft, gliders, released loads, recovered loads and the beneficiaries at the correct drop zone; match the second lift and later resupply separately. |
| [Bodenplatte](deployment-audit/reich.md) | Elements of at least 11 fighter-wing parents are traceable. At Ghent the Polish 131 Wing report names three squadrons, which sent 8, 12 and 11 aircraft. | The 37-entry attacker list is source-specific. JG 4's group numbering and JG 26's targets need reconciliation. Separate planned, flew, turned back, visited airfield and sustained loss. Check the opposing unit/field return before assigning a claim. [Screen and roster conflict](deployment-audit/surfaces.md) |
| [Berlin](deployment-audit/east.md) | Wartime reports identify four Soviet air armies across different dates; Polish aviation is missing from the summary. Two German JG 4 Gruppen were at Rechlin on 19 April. | A station record cannot prove a flight over the city. Soviet returns independently confirm 4,054 sorties on 20 April; 2,133 were fighter sorties. Preserve 3,037 serviceable versus 3,033 combat aircraft as different source accounts. |

## Using the evidence pack

The [population CSV](../transcriptions/deployment-benchmark-populations.csv) records a count, what was counted, the date, force, echelon, source class, locator, URL and scope. The [member CSV](../transcriptions/deployment-benchmark-members.csv) joins on population ID. Its 163 rows are unit memberships in 27 selected comparisons across 20 campaigns. A named member set can be shorter than a reported count when the source gives a total without identities. Aircraft returned, squadrons allocated, parent wings represented and groups reaching an area cannot be added. Blank stock, serviceability or Atlas match fields mean unentered or untested. Explicit zero Atlas matches apply only to the named interface.

The [dossier inventory](../transcriptions/atlas-v10-coverage-inventory.csv) can be checked with `node scripts/audit-campaign-coverage.cjs --check` at this revision. It uses the chronology's loaded Normandy override and the literal side tags in the older register. The “Allied” tag in the register for the Spanish Republic illustrates why historical service and parent command need to be recorded separately. Chromium checks exercised the normal interface against this revision. The public formation page, dossier and renderer assets, and Sicily data were also compared to the baseline. This research adds no formations to the deployed selectors.

The previous [320-row candidate reconciliation](../transcriptions/campaign-candidate-reconciliation.csv) tracks data already accepted, superseded or outside scope. PR #5's quantitative tables and station candidates, #15's Narvik work and #20's bilateral records are acquisition leads. Preserve their original scopes and check their underlying documents before using them to close a roster. A citation to a diary transcription and a history quoting the same diary share a witness. Independent aircraft losses and operational effects require evidence from outside that chain.
