# Wikipedia references data extraction — Cobra / Mission 494 — 12 November 2026

Focused extraction of Atlas-relevant operational data (fighter participation, claims, losses, air-phase statistics) from references cited in the Wikipedia articles already covered by the earlier sweep. No live calculator changes. Complements the prior Cobra attribution note.

## Sources checked and data extracted

### 1. Eighth Air Force Combat Chronology / American Air Museum Mission 494
- Mission 494 (25 July 1944): 1,581 bombers and 500 fighters dispatched for the Cobra saturation bombing in the Marigny–Saint-Gilles / Périers–St-Lô area.
- 843 of 917 B-17s and 647 of 664 B-24s attacked the primary target area; 13 B-17s hit targets of opportunity.
- Escort and participation: 483 of 500 fighters (P-38, P-47, P-51) flew; they also provided escort for Ninth Air Force B-26s and conducted strafing/bombing runs.
- Fighter claims: 12-1-3 Luftwaffe aircraft in the air and 2-0-0 on the ground.
- Fighter losses: 2 P-51s (pilots MIA); 5 fighters damaged.
- Bomber losses: 1 B-17 and 4 B-24s lost; 2 B-24s damaged beyond repair; 41 B-17s and 132 B-24s damaged.
- Friendly-fire note: bombs from 35 bombers fell within US lines (102 US troops killed including Lt Gen McNair, 380 wounded).

These figures align with and refine the existing Atlas low-confidence Cobra notes (approx. 480–483 escorts, ~12 claims → ~8 German air-combat losses).

### 2. Official histories and secondary citations (Breakout and Pursuit, AAF Vol. III, Sullivan Parameters article)
- Air phase on 25 July: ~600 fighter-bombers opened, followed by ~1,500–1,800 heavy bombers, then medium bombers. Total ordnance roughly 4,000+ tons.
- Fighter-bomber numbers in the planning phase: ~350 for the initial strip attack, another ~350 after the heavies.
- German ground effect (Panzer-Lehr): estimates of ~1,000 casualties and heavy equipment losses from the bombardment (not air-to-air).
- Sullivan (Parameters 1988) addresses command responsibility for the short-bombing and the perpendicular approach; it does not add fighter-claim or airframe-matching data.

### 3. Jean-Bernard Frappé, *La Luftwaffe face au débarquement* (cited in the Wikipedia sweep)
- Specialist secondary covering Jagdgeschwader day-by-day from 6 June to 31 August 1944.
- Includes annexes of claims, personnel/material losses, and wreck inventories.
- Catalogue and reviews confirm it is a detailed unit-level source for the period; the specific 25 July Gruppe entries were not transcribed in this pass (book not fully accessible). Remains the best secondary lead for matching the 12 US claims against German unit activity.

### 4. Other Wikipedia-cited works
- Davis, *Bombing the European Axis Powers* (and workbooks): already flagged in the prior sweep; access to 1944 sheets remains blocked.
- 2nd TAF ORB and AIR 24 series: still the primary for RAF/Commonwealth fighter-bomber activity; not newly transcribed here.
