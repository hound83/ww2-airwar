# Evidence for the connected European air war

The [seven chapters](../../europe.html) follow changes in what aircraft could contribute between 1936 and 1945. Each passage has its own source list. The [chapter data](../transcriptions/europe-chapters.json) holds the text, source locators, links and representative places. Existing campaign passages and [balance-study sources](operational-balance.md) retain their original definitions.

| Comparison | Evidence and limits |
| --- | --- |
| Spain to the early campaigns | Juntunen, *U.S. Army Attachés and the Spanish Civil War*, p. 32, supports the early transport flights. The Bundesarchiv dates the decision to send transports. They do not measure troops delivered by a particular date. The Gernika Peace Museum chronology distinguishes the bombing, occupation and later evacuations. The RAF Museum identifies Polish personnel evacuated through France. They include ground staff. |
| Poland, Norway and France | The Polish Aviation Museum identifies a sortie from Klimontów. The effect on the German column remains unmeasured. The RAF Norway history records the continuing use of the Harstad anchorage. The British 12th Infantry Brigade account reproduced by the RAF Museum describes a temporary check near Nieuport on 31 May. Its account applies locally. |
| Britain, Malta and Dieppe | Fighter Command's immediate issue reserve is a stock of aircraft. The Northolt observation covers overnight repair at one squadron. Malta's arrivals and harbour receipts concern ships and unloaded supplies, while the Canadian account at Dieppe describes the withdrawal close to shore. Each record identifies its own beneficiary. |
| Barbarossa, Stalingrad and Kursk | Kreis, *Air Warfare and Air Base Air Defense*, ch. VI, distinguishes the initial losses on exposed fields from Soviet ability to replace or move aircraft later. The US Army German Report Series, *Military Improvisations*, pp. 61–62, describes loss of transport bases and crews diverted from training. The fall of Pitomnik is a dated episode in the campaign dossier. Kursk vehicle claims still need ground-unit checks. |
| Sicily, Schweinfurt and Big Week | No. 92 Squadron's Form 540 gives the sequence from beach patrol to Pachino and shipping cover. The Reich campaign sources distinguish successive targets and escort limits. The 445th's mission sheets record dispatches, aborts and aircraft over the primary target on successive days. They do not identify all replacement crews. |
| Cassino, Normandy, Bobruisk and Arnhem | US Army accounts place assault troops among the rubble and follow the advance after Cobra. Connor's *Analysis of Deep Attack Operations* dates the ground link-up near Bobruisk. The 16th Air Army's history describes the subsequent evening strike. The RAF Museum chronology distinguishes lift effort from supplies received at Arnhem. No common casualty or delivery ratio follows from these accounts. |
| Fuel, airfields and Berlin | Compare the manpower conference in May 1944 with the USSBS retrospective on oil. Ritchie's *The Royal Air Force and Airfield Air Defence*, pp. 100–101, distinguishes Allied airframe damage from German personnel losses at Bodenplatte. The 16th Air Army's report at Alt-Malisch gives the account from the air. Weinberg's *Stalingrad and Berlin*, pp. 18–20, describes the ground fighting in the city. |

## Map and navigation

The locator uses [Natural Earth 1:110m public-domain land](https://www.naturalearthdata.com/downloads/110m-physical-vectors/110m-land/) clipped to 16° W–56° E and 32–72° N. It shows physical coastlines and one named place from each chapter. The map pins are approximate town centres or, for Malta, an island location. Campaign maps and dated operations supply the finer geography. The narrative periods overlap where wars were being fought simultaneously.

Direct links identify the originating chapter independently of each campaign's date, case, formation or local chapter filter. Source citations remain attached to the relevant passages. The [campaign review](../../docs/CAMPAIGN_EVIDENCE_REVIEW.md) describes the twenty individual dated cases and the outstanding receiving-side records.
