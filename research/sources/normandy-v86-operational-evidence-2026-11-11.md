## Findings

Five connected operational studies now have 16 explicitly dated map snapshots, 24 new quantitative observations, 14 formation profiles (including ground formations and higher commands) and four illustrative dated airfields. The existing 9 chapters, 39 chronology episodes and 135 overview observations are preserved. Additional operation records are a separate collection; their units and populations are not summed into a synthetic campaign total.

## Interpretation and useful negative findings

- D-Day: Hallion p.8 records a task allocation of 171 fighter squadrons. Its five component tasks sum to 171; neither aircraft nor sorties may be substituted for squadrons.
- Goodwood: Hallion pp.20–21 reports 1,676 heavy plus 343 light/medium bombers. Blumenson p.191 rounds these classes differently and gives more than 8,000 tons. Both keep their stated source classes; no metric-ton conversion is imposed.
- Cobra: the six participation/tonnage figures are lower bounds as printed in Blumenson p.234. The 111 killed/490 wounded are ground personnel from 25 July (p.235), not aircraft losses; 24 July was a separate incident. The added map shows schematic ground directions, not an exact bomber approach.
- Mortain: the original AEAF despatch separates 294 local Typhoon sorties on 7 August from 2,193 wider Typhoon sorties on 7–11 August and 4,012 IX TAC sorties on 8–14 August (p.66, paras.290–293). The latter includes virtually all effort in cooperation with Mortain-region ground action, but is not an exact-day or Typhoon-only count.
- Falaise: Stacey p.243 records 417 Lancasters, 352 Halifaxes and 42 Mosquitoes plus 3,723 tons for Tractable on 14 August. Their 811-aircraft sum can be derived, but is not a fifth independent observation. We did not adopt the unverified 391-casualty claim found in secondary search results: the official text gives a provisional return with different categories. No enemy tank/vehicle destruction total is inferred from wrecks.
- Hallion contains a conspicuous 1941/1944 date typo in its pre-invasion fighter-loss discussion. That paragraph supplies none of the new records. Its broad retrospective claims of effectiveness are not used as measured kill totals.
- B-1 first strip use (7 June), A-1 operational use (13 June) versus group arrival (17 June), A-3 missions (14 June) versus runway completion (20 June), and A-9 month-level reconnaissance association retain the prior field audit distinctions. These do not establish current serviceability or the departure field of every strike.

## Map method and evidence

The basemap remains physical Natural Earth land. Approximate locality coordinates support navigation; straight connections depict documented planned ground directions, advances or retreat directions. Dashed versus solid and Allied versus German colours have explicit legends. No continuous frontline, air track, bomb footprint or measured loss coordinate was recovered or fabricated. Every snapshot links its historical source and locator; supplementary dates such as Carentan retain the previously sourced Army chronology. Bases appear only after their recorded first-use date and remain labelled as illustrative basing context. A declaration that a base opened does not prove it stayed serviceable on every subsequent date.
