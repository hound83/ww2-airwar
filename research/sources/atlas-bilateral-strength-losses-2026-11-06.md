# Atlas research audit: bilateral strength, operational availability and aircraft losses
Date: 6 November 2026. Baseline: PR #16, 75 campaign facts and 80 researched events.

## Method and definitions
This milestone prioritizes **two-sided evidence without false bilateral ratios**. Every numeric record has a date, source, side, denominator/reporting chain, kind (on strength, serviceable, sorties, loss, claim, estimate) and airframe scope. Unless two sources share the same theater/time/unit/measure, the Atlas displays juxtaposition with an explicit warning, **not** a mathematically invalid exchange ratio.

### Battle of Britain: RAF Fighter Command versus Luftwaffe, early August 1940
**RAF Air Power Review**, Air Commodore Peter Dye, *Logistics and the Battle of Britain*, vol. 3 no. 4, 2000, pp. 24–27:
https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/centre-for-air-and-space-power-studies/aspr/apr-vol3-iss4-2-pdf/

The table reproduced from earlier German Quartermaster and RAF Fighter Command order-of-battle records gives:
- **10 August** Luftwaffe all-types **on strength 3,358**, **serviceable 2,550**. Single-engine fighters (Bf109-dominant category) **on strength 934**, **serviceable 805**. Both fighter numbers are subsets of all-types counts.
- **11 August** RAF Fighter Command Hurricanes+Spitfires **on strength 1,095**, **serviceable 990**; serviceable Hurricanes **656** + Spitfires **334** = 990. Both fighter types are subsets of the RAF Fighter Command total.
- RAF paper also reports **over 900 RAF Hurricanes/Spitfires destroyed on operations** versus **600 Bf 109s recorded by German Quartermaster** for July–October 1940. Treat 900 as LOWER THRESHOLD, not exact, and distinguish both single-fighter categories from the wider Luftwaffe **1,733 destroyed** of all types from postwar German records already in Atlas.
- The comparison is a **one-day offset**. RAF 2000 analysis cites historical disagreement on what immediately ready aircraft count means; at least one other secondary history uses a substantially narrower 'immediately ready' denominator. We do not overwrite the original table or pretend that on-strength equals operationally serviceable, or airframe ready equals trained pilots.

### Normandy: Allied coalition air assets versus Luftflotte 3, June–July 1944
**German-source study preserved by U.S. Navy NHHC**, *The GAF and the Invasion of Normandy*:
https://www.history.navy.mil/research/library/online-reading-room/title-list-alphabetically/g/gaf-invasion-normandy.html

The document reports Luftflotte 3 **481 fighting-strength aircraft including 100 fighters on 5 June**, but **319 available aircraft for day/night operations on 6 June**. Dates and categories differ, and German regional Luftflotte 3 is NOT the entire Luftwaffe. Over a later **6 June–7 July western command reporting window**, the German study gives **826 aircraft destroyed**, divided into **458 fighters/fighter-bombers/recon, 224 bombers, 7 supply, 137 destroyed on ground** (458+224+7+137=826; these are classified non-overlapping categories as presented by the study). Does not include Reich and Eastern Front losses and is not a single-day D-Day return.

**Eisenhower**, original Report to Combined Chiefs of Staff on Allied Expeditionary Force, printed p. 20:
https://history.army.mil/portals/143/Images/Publications/catalog/70-58.pdf
For **6 June**, he reports Strategic Air Forces **5,309 sorties** and Tactical Air Forces **5,276 sorties**, each belonging to a separate command population. Their sum **10,585** is NOT all categories of the existing broad Atlas **14,674 Allied sorties**. It does not include all transport, airborne and other missions counted by other reporting schemes.

**Official United States European Command** D-Day historical figure: **11,590 Allied aircraft involved**; an aircraft count, not sortie count or directly measured serviceability:
https://www.eucom.mil/organization/history-of-useucom/operation-overlord-d-day

**Allied D-Day losses: 127 aircraft** comes from a secondary historical summary reproduced by the Royal Pioneer Corps Association (October 2014 newsletter):
https://www.royalpioneercorps.co.uk/rpc/newsletters/2014_Oct_Newsletter.pdf
Scope **6 June only, all Allied aircraft/all causes**; NOT comparable as a loss ratio with **826 German aircraft destroyed over more than 30 days**. Also recovered secondary USAF historian Williamson Murray, *Strategy for Defeat*, reporting first-week Luftflotte 3 losses **362** and second-week **232**, subsets within the longer period:
https://www.airuniversity.af.edu/Portals/10/AUPress/Books/B_0012_MURRAY_STRATEGY_FOR_DEFEAT.pdf

### Barbarossa, Eastern Front: combat allocation, intelligence estimates, opposing losses/claims
**USAF Office of Air Force History**, *Case Studies in the Achievement of Air Superiority*, Barbarossa case:
https://media.defense.gov/2010/Oct/12/2001330116/-1/-1/0/AFD-101012-038.pdf
Gives German **2,000 combat aircraft committed** including 60 Army Command Norway aircraft, plus **1,270 support/transport/liaison**. German pre-invasion intelligence estimated **5,700 Soviet aircraft** in facing western region, **not verified actual Soviet strength or serviceability**. The official USAF study explicitly calls the estimate incomplete/low. Do not display a false 2000-versus-5700 combat-power ratio.

Specialist historian Nigel Askey's reconstruction, citing the official German Research Institute's *Germany and the Second World War* vol. IV:
https://www.operationbarbarossa.net/the-period-22nd-june-to-5th-august-1941/
Gives **330 Luftwaffe aircraft losses 22–30 June** (reconstructed from own records) versus **4,614 German-claimed Soviet aircraft destroyed** across the same nine days. These are NOT paired confirmed losses. Soviet actual opening-loss estimate already in the Atlas is a different period and recorded source. Original 1941 German QM and Soviet VVS air-division daily return scans remain unaudited.

### Sicily, 11–17 July 1943: German versus Italian aircraft and losses
**Official UK military history**, *The Mediterranean and Middle East*, vol. V (postwar UK government history drawing on German and Italian original documents), Sicily section printed p. 98 and table footnote:
https://www.generalstaff.org/WW2/Hist_UK/MedMiddleEastVol5.pdf

Daily aircraft committed to action over Sicily (NOT sortie counts and NOT complete on-strength inventory):
| Date | German | Italian | Same-day sum |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| 11 July | 283 | 198 | 481 |
| 12 July | 202 | 171 | 373 |
| 13 July | 164 | 197 | 361 |
| 14 July | 156 | 88 | 244 |
| 15 July | 85 | 76 | 161 |

**Certain aircraft destroyed in the air over Sicily**: daytime **11–16 July** German **59**, Italian **32**; nighttime **10/11–16/17 July** German **44**, Italian **12**. Separate AA losses are mentioned in the source but intentionally not added to these four air-to-air categories. A 'certain' enemy loss as reconstructed by postwar official historians is more robust than a contemporary RAF victory claim, but this is NOT an independently re-transcribed German/Italian daily aircraft-by-aircraft loss ledger.

The existing original MAAF HQ campaign table gives **177 Allied aircraft losses** in the *entire 10 July–17 August* campaign; NOT the same date window/aircraft cause categories as the German and Italian first-week records, and must not be used for a direct loss ratio. Allied victory claims of 293 remain distinct.

- UK National Archives AIR 27 Form 540/541 original daily returns for select RAF squadrons; public official RAF histories cited in prior Atlas source audits.
- RAF Fighter Command contemporary Park August–September 1940 report and original strength returns to reconcile narrower ready-to-fly versus 990 squadron-serviceable definitions.

**Verification caution:** Digitized source text and official-source excerpts were available, but this research session could not obtain successfully rendered full-page visual screenshots of several hosted PDF scans via the browser service. Accordingly this milestone labels published tables and postwar syntheses accurately, but does NOT claim all original ledger images have been independently visually audited. Avoid invented precision or implying full archival scan verification.
