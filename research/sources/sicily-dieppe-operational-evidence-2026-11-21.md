# Sicily airfields and the Dieppe withdrawal

## Sicily, 10–19 July 1943

The [campaign records](../transcriptions/sicily-air-campaign-1943.json) extend the [v9.9 Sicily audit](atlas-sicily-air-campaign-2026-11-14.md). Three source types cover different events. The RAF narrative follows the advance parties and the flying units. The tactical air force's construction return dates work and operational status. Squadron diaries describe what the pilots actually flew.

| Field | Observation | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pachino | The RAF narrative dates capture to 10 July. On 12 July visiting Spitfires refuelled en route back to Malta. On 13 July units of No. 244 Wing landed after patrols. Appendix H gives 10 July for the start of work and 14 July for operational status. | Visiting aircraft, unit arrival and the engineering return provide three different indicators of use. |
| Pachino, No. 92 Squadron | Form 540, 13 July, records 11 pilots departing Malta and arriving after a patrol. Its 41 sorties cover the whole day. On 14 July the diary records two patrols before ground personnel arrived, followed by three more. On 16 July eight aircraft covered ships off Syracuse. | The daily total includes flights originating elsewhere. Patrol counts have a different unit from individual aircraft sorties. The earliest take-off from the strip is still open. The aircraft covering shipping cannot by themselves establish damage prevented. |
| Ponte Olivo | The RAF narrative gives air echelon arrival on 13 July. Appendix H row 13 dates work to 12 July and operational status to 15 July. Appendix I groups the 307th, 308th and 309th Squadrons under the 31st Fighter Group on 16 July. | The return describes the grouped build-up. Retain the difference with the RAF arrival account. The earliest flight from the field needs a unit mission record. |
| Biscari | RAF occupation on 12 July and U.S. Military Academy capture on 14 July remain separately sourced. Appendix H row 11 dates work to 16 July and operational status to 19 July. | The engineering return adds a new sequence. It identifies neither the first unit sortie nor the reason for the earlier disagreement about occupation. |

## Dieppe, 19 August 1942

The [Dieppe study](../../dieppe.html) follows the cover of the ships, observations from shore, the withdrawal and the competing loss totals. The Canadian squadron diary describes four squadron operations that day. Its first three give a sequence from close cover over Dieppe to observation of the homeward convoy.

| Source and locator | Scoped observation |
| --- | --- |
| [No. 403 Squadron diary transcription, 19 August](https://rcaf403squadron.wordpress.com/2015/12/14/august-1942/) | First patrol left at 06:45 and reached Dieppe at 07:15. Three aircraft failed to return. Second patrol left at 11:15 and all twelve returned at 13:15. The third patrol left at 16:20 and observed the returning ships. Times follow the transcription. Each patrol was a squadron operation. |
| [Canadian AHQ Report 10, Basic Observations of the Commander-in-Chief West, no. 8, 23 August, §2](https://publications.gc.ca/collections/collection_2016/mdn-dnd/D63-5-10-1946-eng.pdf) | The translated German report describes smoke concealing craft during the landing and attributes the screen to ships or aircraft. It supplies evidence for the effect observed ashore. Allocation of that effect to an individual sortie remains open. |
| [Canadian Army, Six Years of War, pp. 383–385](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/UN/Canada/CA/SixYears/SixYears-11.html#page383) | The shift to 11:00 allowed reinforcement of air cover and more time to contact the Camerons.
