# Targeted search results for remaining Atlas gaps (12 November 2026)

## 1. Bodenplatte unit-level loss lists and Allied squadron reports

**What is available**
- Manrho & Pütz *Bodenplatte: The Luftwaffe’s Last Hope* (Hikoki) contains appendices with Luftwaffe order of battle and strength report for 31 Dec 1944, German fighter losses on 1 Jan 1945, Allied order of battle, Allied material losses, and known personnel casualties on the airfields. Full extraction requires the book.
- Secondary sources give unit-level fragments:
  - JG 3 at Eindhoven: claimed 53 single-engine + 11 twin-engine destroyed; lost ~15 of 60 fighters (25 %), 9–10 KIA / 5–6 POW.
  - JG 11 at Asch: 61 aircraft committed, 28 lost, 24 pilots (20 KIA, 4 POW). Aviation Safety Network and Studiegroep Luchtoorlog list individual Bf 109 losses (e.g. 8./JG 11 Oblt A. Engel near Opoeteren).
  - JG 26 at Evere: destroyed ~11 Spitfires + transports; lost ~20 pilots.
  - JG 53 at Metz-Frescaty: 30 Bf 109s lost + 8 damaged.
- Studiegroep Luchtoorlog (verliesregister.studiegroepluchtoorlog.nl) and Aviation Safety Network hold individual German loss records with WNr, unit, pilot and crash location.

**Gap status**: Airfield-level skeleton can be expanded with these fragments. Complete airframe-by-airframe ledger still needs Manrho & Pütz or equivalent specialist extraction.

## 2. Post-15 August 1940 German claim series

**What is available**
- RAF AHB translations of Luftwaffe Führungsstab IC situation reports stop at Part 3 (1–15 August). No further AHB parts are listed.
- The National Archives holds HW 41/18 (Daily summaries of German air force activity, October 1940) — not digitised, Kew only.
- Michael Balss *Deutsche Luftwaffe Losses & Claims: Part 4-I September – 15 October 1940* exists as a specialist day-by-day register based on GQM and NVM files.
- Secondary sources continue to confirm the overclaim pattern (e.g. 15 Sep 78 claimed / 29 actual) but do not supply a consistent weekly series after mid-August.
- German serviceable strength tables (10 Aug – 5 Oct) are available in secondary literature.

**Gap status**: Still blocked for a free, consistent weekly claim series. Next practical steps are Balss Part 4 or a Kew visit / copy request for HW 41 series.

## 3. Eastern Front unit time-series stationing and strength returns (Prien et al.)

**What is available**
- Wikipedia unit pages give campaign-scale figures:
  - JG 54 (22 Jun – 5 Dec 1941): 1,078 Soviet aircraft destroyed for 46 aerial losses + 1 on ground.
  - JG 51 (same period): 1,881 destroyed for 84 aerial losses + 1 on ground.
- FalkeEins blog summaries of Prien et al. give later snapshots (Jan 1944 ~300 serviceable fighters on the entire Eastern Front; Luftflotte 6 middle sector ~50–75 at Bagration).
- The multi-volume *Die Jagdfliegerverbände der Deutschen Luftwaffe 1934 bis 1945* (Prien, Stemmer, Rodeike, Bock) is the definitive source. Relevant volumes include Teil 9 (1942–early 1943) and Teil 12 (1943). They contain unit-by-unit operational histories, airfield maps and strength returns. Not freely available online.

**Gap status**: Campaign anchors exist. Full time-series unit stationing still requires the Prien volumes.

## 4. Full Ninth Air Force July report and Davis May German-target window

**What is available**
- CARL Digital Library holds “Ninth Air Force, April to November 1944” (AFHRA PDF, 389 pp) and “Ninth Air Force invasion activities, April thru June 1944”. Both are catalogued; full text not freely extractable in this pass.
- Internet Archive holds the *Condensed Analysis of the Ninth Air Force in the European Theater of Operations* (USAF Warrior Studies) and Davis *Bombing the European Axis Powers* (with CD-ROM of bombing data spreadsheets).
- Secondary sites (9af.org) publish daily Ninth AF sorties/losses for late June–early July (e.g. 6 July: 509 bomb / 2 lost / 110 damaged; 1,159 F-B / 7 lost).
- Eighth AF Mission 494 tactical report is at CARL but not freely downloadable in full text.

**Gap status**: Sources are identified and partially accessible via Internet Archive. Full July command returns and the Davis May German-target spreadsheet still need direct extraction from the PDFs / CD-ROM data.

## 5. CAMO document-level access

Unchanged. Site continues to return queue-full / heavy-load errors. No new access path found in this pass.

## Summary
The largest near-term gains are the Bodenplatte unit fragments (expandable from Manrho & Pütz + Studiegroep records) and the Internet Archive copies of the Ninth AF condensed analysis and Davis bombing digest. The post-15 August claim series and full Eastern Front unit time-series remain blocked by specialist volumes or archive-only material.
