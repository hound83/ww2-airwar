## Operational phases: the missing interpretive level

[The Museum of Polish History](https://muzhp.pl/wiedza-on-line/polska-w-ii-wojnie-swiatowej%20%E2%80%8B) explicitly divides the September Campaign into **three phases**. The Atlas presents these as an operational reading path before its individual dated aviation evidence:

1. **1–6 September: border battles.** German attack, Luftwaffe support and strikes, Polish dispersal, fighter interception and reconnaissance/attacks. The museum's 'first days' phrasing is operationally bounded for navigation at 6 September, not claimed as an exact original order boundary.
2. **7–16 September: interior defence and retreat.** The museum describes 7–17 September as fighting deeper inside Poland and attempted reorganization farther east. For the non-overlapping UI, the decisive Soviet entry on **17 September** starts the next chapter; the 9 September 121st Squadron engagement demonstrates that Polish flying did not stop on invasion day.
3. **17 September–6 October: two-front collapse and remaining resistance.** The Eastern invasion, siege of Warsaw, later surrender and end of organized resistance. Ground campaign milestones are not relabelled as individual air operations.

These phases structure *interpretation*. The existing four campaign-wide retrospective aircraft figures remain attached only to the **whole campaign**, never apportioned by phase. A click on a chapter opens its ground context, air-operations role, military consequence, uncertainty and primary museum/archive links; only after that can the reader open a dated incident.

## Explicit conflict: Polish ground losses in the opening days

- RAF Museum states that the Polish fighter squadrons had dispersed to forward airstrips and **were not destroyed on the ground during the first days** as the familiar story has it.
- USHMM's historical summary states that **most Polish aircraft were destroyed during the first few days** and is commonly interpreted as an early destruction claim. Its wording does not itself provide a verified *ground-destruction count*. These museum syntheses may use different categories or traditions of source evidence.
- **Decision:** show this disagreement in the campaign dossier without selecting one account as a quantified aerial/ground loss ledger. Further original Polish combat diaries and German daily losses are necessary to reconcile it.

## Geographic and temporal limits

- The map points locate a historical region or place for navigation, **not measured unit positions or georeferenced combat strikes**. The 1 September invasion and 6 October conclusion use explicitly symbolic broad campaign points.
- All four visible numerical observations are retrospective summaries of the **1939 Poland campaign**; do not combine them with 1943–44 Luftflotte Reich/West losses, post-1942 USAAF ETO/MTO force sizes or total-all-front Soviet VVS snapshots.
- Events requiring verification still say which units are named, which are unknown and what can **not** be inferred from the source. A source URL at campaign level alone is not treated as event-specific proof. The extra 9 September engagement is a **museum attribution**, not an independently verified aircraft kill.
- This pilot improves the depth of one campaign. Spain, France 1940, the Battle of Britain and Normandy now also have source-backed chapter narratives, but the other 15 campaigns remain unphased until sources support them. They are **not** silently declared research-complete.
