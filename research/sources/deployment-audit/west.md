# Britain, Dieppe, Normandy and Market Garden

These are checks against specific tables or war-diary entries. Each count applies to the place, role, date and echelon stated. The audited baseline is Atlas v10.0, main at `9e25892`. The two event files inspected were `research/transcriptions/britain-v87-operations.json` and `normandy-v86-operations.json`. ## Battle of Britain

**A same-day, two-sided comparison is possible for 7 September 1940.** The current `britain-7-sep` operation links RAF Fighter Command and a combined Luftflotten 2/3 record. Neither link identifies an operational flying unit. The separate campaign formation histories describe seven Allied squadrons and no German flying formation.

The matching secondary sources are Basil Collier's British official history, *The Defence of the United Kingdom*, Appendix XX, pp. 472–474, and Appendix XVII, pp. 463–467. The latter explicitly excludes short-range tactical reconnaissance. Its note describes JG 2 as interchangeable between two air fleets. Its online Luftflotte 3 table repeats the I./JG 2 designation for two rows. Consult a scan or independent German return before normalising the second. Preserve split commands, headquarters, flights and naval units. The No. 11 Group list illustrates the collision between two different No. 1 Squadrons. **Wartime commander's evidence.** Dowding's Battle of Britain despatch, Appendix A, provides the 8 July order of battle and war stations. No. 10 Group lists 87 and 213 at Exeter, 92 at Pembrey and 234 at St Eval. The appendix separates eight forming or reforming squadrons, including 1 (Canadian), from the operational roster and lists A and B Flights of No. 603 separately at Turnhouse and Montrose. This makes the despatch a check on readiness, historical identity, and duplicated squadron names. It is an earlier date than Collier’s September table. [RAF reprint of Dowding’s despatch, Appendix A, pp. 116–117](https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/centre-for-air-and-space-power-studies/aspr/apr-vol18-iss2-4-pdf)

**Contribution and coalition checks.** The RAF Museum identifies 11 specific No. 11 Group squadrons ordered up between 11:05 and 11:20 on 15 September. This is a timed commitment against London-bound raids. Its later entry records five No. 12 Group squadrons airborne at 11:25, including 302 (Polish) and 310 (Czechoslovak). Collier also lists Italian operations inside the conventional campaign window on 25 and 29 October, including 15 bombers and 73 escort fighters dispatched to Ramsgate on 29 October. A Germany-only opponent label misses an actual later participant. Night sorties in the same appendix continue beyond the campaign end. ## Dieppe

The three campaign participant labels are RAF Fighter Command, Luftwaffe and Royal Navy aviation. The dedicated formation-history file has no Dieppe entry. The 19 August 1942 operation is sufficiently short to test a complete one-day, one-role roster.

| Historical scope | Verified figure or names | Interpretation |
|---|---|---|
| All Allied air squadrons supporting the operation | Veterans Affairs Canada gives 74, including eight RCAF. Mahoney's Chart I.1 counts 48 Spitfire air-cover, 3 Typhoon cover, 6 Hurricane close support, 2 Hurricane direct support, 4 Mustang reconnaissance, 4 Boston bombing, 4 B-17 diversion, 2 Blenheim smoke and 1 Boston smoke. | The 48 are a subset of the 74. Neither number counts aircraft or squadrons overhead simultaneously. |
| A verified three-squadron sortie | 485 (New Zealand), 411 (RCAF) and 610 (RAF) took off from West Malling at 07:30. | Evidence for actual activity with time, base and national identities; each is one squadron. |
| German fighter and fighter-bomber presence | Mahoney identifies JG 2 and JG 26 as the two fighter Geschwader. Separate research identifies 10./JG 2 and 10./JG 26 in action against shipping. | Two fighter-wing parents; the two Staffeln are children. Bomber formations, reconnaissance and flak require separate evidence. |
| German headquarters' appraisal of air effort | Luftflotte 3 reported 206 of its own fighters employed with repeat sorties. Its 31 August intelligence appreciation estimated approximately 50 enemy squadrons during the operation and a peak of about 15–20 at a time. | The German intelligence estimate has a different scope and basis from Allied task allocations. Preserve the three quantities separately. |

Mahoney's Appendix 1, starting on printed p. 170, gives units, bases and roles from AIR 20/5186 Appendix A plus Franks. Two checks before bulk import: 308 (Polish) and the USAAF 308th Fighter Squadron shared a number and served in different formations, and the US 31st Fighter Group's squadron at Kenley must retain its USAAF identity even under British operational control. The older South African Military History Society study labels its fighter list as incomplete, repeats 133, and offers speculative bomber unit identifications. It is a useful candidate list. Its own wording does not certify a complete bomber roster. Where sources report 69 RAF squadrons, retain the service qualifier and reconcile it to Allied-wide figures before comparing totals. ## Normandy

The D-Day event's five formation links identify IX Troop Carrier Command, Eighth Air Force, Ninth Air Force, RAF Second Tactical Air Force and Luftflotte 3. Five formation biographies for the broader Normandy campaign all cover Allied units. This leaves German formations below air-fleet HQ invisible in these two particular lists.

| Scope and date | Primary or source-derived evidence | What it establishes |
|---|---|---|
| Selected German fighter dispositions under II. Jagdkorps, 3 June | W. J. A. Wood’s copy of AHB 6's translated enemy table includes Stab/JG 2 (Creil), I./JG 2 (Cormeilles-en-Vexin), Stab/JG 26 (Lille-Nord), I./JG 26 (Lille/Vendeville and Denain), III./JG 26 (Lupcourt). | Two fighter Geschwader represented; at least three numbered Gruppen in this table. It is a Reich air-defence table with western entries. Being listed three days before D-Day does not prove a sortie over a beach. |

| German reinforcements transferred to the Western Front, 7–9 June | German 8th Abteilung, report dated 27 August 1944, p. 3, records **21 fighter Gruppen**, each of 23–39 aircraft, and separately lists elements of two night-fighter Geschwader, one short-range reconnaissance Gruppe and one long-range reconnaissance Staffel. An independent USAAF historical account of the landings discusses why air cover allowed ground-support work but preparatory bombardment left many Omaha defences intact. This is a test of contribution: protecting the approach, helping the troops inland, and destroying beach defences had different results. A formation's presence cannot demonstrate all three. [USAF, *D-Day 1944*, “Air Support on the Beaches”](https://www.ibiblio.org/hyperwar/AAF/AAF-H-DDay/)

## Market Garden

The dossier's four participant labels are RAF Transport Command, IX Troop Carrier, RAF Second Tactical Air Force and Luftwaffe. The cross-campaign formation-history file holds no Market Garden entry. Distinct scopes are needed for Eindhoven, Nijmegen and Arnhem, and for transport, escort, air defence, and tactical ground support.

| Scope | Wartime record or official-history finding | Limit or corroboration |
|---|---|---|
| Arnhem first lift, 17 September | 1st Airborne Division HQ, diary WO 171/392 and 393, 10:15, identifies 38 and 46 Groups and IX Troop Carrier Command airfields. At 13:00 onwards it records landings of 1st Airlanding Brigade and divisional elements, followed by 1st Parachute Brigade at 14:40. | Paired dispatch and receiver records establish that forces landed. Only the divisional record's named sector and troops are in scope. |
| No. 271 Squadron, Down Ampney, Arnhem landing zone S, 17–18 September | On 17 September this squadron detailed 24 glider-tug crews. The 271 record confirms successful release for its 24 and cites 132 troops in that day's gliders. | 19/24 is a bounded first-lift release outcome for one unit, not a division-wide delivery rate. ### Research acceptance checks

1. Reconcile one complete, dated RAF No. 11 Group roster and Luftflotte 2 single-engine fighter roster for 7 September. Require every source row to have a named match, documented alias, headquarters or other exclusion, or explicit discrepancy. Link actual operations separately.
2. Resolve the 48 Spitfire air-cover assignments at Dieppe against AIR 20/5186 Appendix A and unit operational records. Reconcile all 74 Allied allocations across roles and identify the German bomber Gruppen and reconnaissance participation independently. Use a single country/service/formation identifier even when unit numbers collide across services.
3. Give Normandy separate 3–6 June dispositions, 7–9 June reinforcements, and mid-June reassignment. Reconcile the 21 transferred Gruppen to named movement orders and arrivals; an originating-airfield record alone cannot prove arrival. The 54 beach-cover allocations and 171 broader allocations provide scoped Allied research controls.
4. For Market Garden, match British and US lift serials to the three different ground formations and each day's escort assignments. Reconcile with German operational evidence and ground diary accounts of supply receipt. Distinguish planned, launched, released, landed or recovered; leave unidentified German ad hoc formations unnamed until a dated subordinate record identifies them.
