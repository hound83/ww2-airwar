# Reich defence, Schweinfurt, Big Week and Bodenplatte

These are checks against named historical populations. Each count below has its own date, echelon and inclusion rule. Three of these operations also appear within the longer Reich or Western air war. A shared event needs one identity wherever it is displayed.

## Defence of the Reich

**Berlin, 6 March 1944.** Alfred Price traced the first German scramble at 11:00 to six Gruppen. I. and II./JG 1, I., II. and III./JG 11 and III./JG 54 supplied 107 aircraft between them. That gives three parent Jagdgeschwader in the first scramble alone. The later fighting also involved twin-engine Zerstörergeschwader 26 and Nachtjagdgeschwader 5. Price cites the Freiburg operational maps Kart 44/1632–1633 for the times, numbers, types and fields. This sweep inspected his reconstruction. The original maps remain to be checked. [Price, *Target Berlin*, pp. 113–114 and 122, notes 8–9, 31 and 43](https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/centre-for-air-and-space-power-studies/aspr/apr-vol3-iss1-7-pdf)

The wartime US headquarters narrative counted fourteen bomber wing formations, nineteen US fighter groups and two RAF Mustang squadrons. Its own fighter table has **22 rows, 17 distinct numbered US groups and 796 aircraft entries**. The IDs are 4, 20, 55, 56, 78, 352, 353, 354, 355, 356, 357, 358, 359, 361, 362, 364 and 365. The 56th and 78th have A/B entries, and three groups returned for second sorties. Keep the report's nineteen alongside the seventeen identifiable group numbers. The table supports reconciliation by flight and role, while the numbers alone do not explain headquarters' counting convention. The table flags four Ninth Air Force groups within the force supporting the Eighth. [HQ Eighth Air Force, 250th operation, narrative and “Fighter Operations” table](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5c69f429d745627864a2059c/t/607e00be2f0fbe4985c7c5b9/1618870481768/440306092a.pdf)

**Test the night and late-war coverage separately.** The Australian War Memorial identifies Australian dead serving in **17 different RAF, RAAF and RCAF squadrons** during the 30/31 March 1944 Nuremberg raid. This is a minimum number of participating squadrons, selected through casualties. In the 306th Bomb Group's own 3 February 1945 Berlin report, its crews saw P-51 escorts and recorded flak damage while reporting no opposing fighters. That observation belongs to this formation and flight. Existing Atlas research already documents thirteen selected Reich cases. A campaign roster still requires distinct day, night and late-war returns alongside those cases. [Australian War Memorial, Grant, “On a Nuremberg moon”](https://www.awm.gov.au/wartime/on-a-nuremberg-moon) · [306th BG intelligence narrative, 3 February 1945, §§2–3](https://www.306bg.us/MISSION_REPORTS/3feb45.pdf)

## Schweinfurt and Regensburg

**17 August 1943.** Mission 84's command tables enumerate the nine US heavy bombardment groups on the Schweinfurt task: 91, 92, 303, 305, 306, 351, 379, 381 and 384. The Regensburg task lists seven: 94, 95, 96, 100, 385, 388 and 390. The headquarters totals are 230 and 146 B-17s dispatched, with 36 and 24 missing respectively. Its support section records 18 US P-47 squadrons and 16 RAF Spitfire squadrons across the operation. The support assignment varied by task and flight leg. [VIII Bomber Command, mission 84, Parts I–II and “Fighter Support and Diversions”](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5c69f429d745627864a2059c/t/607c4be012650f14725a4c87/1618758641398/430817026a.pdf)

**14 October 1943.** The same sixteen named B-17 groups appear in the two Schweinfurt task-force tables, now both targeting Schweinfurt. The 1st Division dispatched 149 B-17s, the 3rd 142. A separate table accounts for **29 B-24s from the 93rd and 392nd** on the diversion, after weather broke up assembly. The report states that the planned withdrawal escort could not take off. The B-17 and diversion lists can each be reconciled in full at group level against this HQ return. A whole-day Allied fighter roster requires the separate fighter command report. [VIII Bomber Command, 115th operation, pp. 1–2 and mission 3 diversion table](https://static1.squarespace.com/static/5c69f429d745627864a2059c/t/607ce2b2d5cba32f723adea1/1618797256167/431014046a.pdf)

**German comparison.** The postwar German-side USAF study draws on the I. Jagdkorps war diary. It identifies all five numbered Jagddivisionen from 1 through 5 supplying eligible day or night fighters, with **567 aircraft committed** in the corps account. It then reproduces a conflicting 15 October staff return, Ic Nr. 45/15.10.43 geh., giving **833** for Luftwaffenbefehlshaber Mitte including 7. Jagddivision, plus **49** for Luftflotte 3. Record the reporting authorities, date, and different footprints. These are aircraft commitments rather than numbers of Gruppen or confirmed contacts. The US official history separately gives 38 German fighters destroyed in combat and 20 damaged from the records available to its authors. The National WWII Museum gives 31 destroyed, 12 written off and 34 damaged. Neither set can simply be substituted for an American gunner claim or harmonised without the underlying returns. [German Air Defense, vol. I, section 11, pp. 50–52](https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/Portals/16/documents/Studies/151-200/AFD-090518-056.pdf) · [US official history, vol. II, p. 704, footnote](https://media.defense.gov/2010/Nov/05/2001329887/-1/-1/0/AFD-101105-006.pdf) · [National WWII Museum, “Black Thursday,” loss comparison](https://www.nationalww2museum.org/war/articles/black-thursday-october-14-1943-second-schweinfurt-bombing-raid)

## Big Week

**20 February 1944.** Murray gives **16 bomber combat wings** and **17 fighter groups** with 835 US escorts from England. Josef Schmid's 1954 reconstruction from the I. Jagdkorps diary distinguishes 1., 2. and 3. Jagddivision. The first two contributed against northern penetrations, the second and third against the main attack. I. Jagdkorps counted **362** single-engine, twin-engine and night fighters employed, of which **155 entered combat**. These two figures describe the same reporting population at different stages. On 24 February Schmid names **ZG 26** from 2. Jagddivision, alongside 3. Jagddivision units. He explicitly reports that the diary gives no record of the 7. Jagddivision or Ostmark response at Steyr and Linz. The 336 aircraft assigned to that day's I. Jagdkorps figure therefore cannot establish a southern German total. [Murray, *Strategy for Defeat*, p. 242](https://www.airuniversity.af.edu/Portals/10/AUPress/Books/B_0012_MURRAY_STRATEGY_FOR_DEFEAT.pdf) · [Schmid, vol. II, foreword pp. 1–2 and ch. III, pp. 105–107, 115–117](https://www.dafhistory.af.mil/Portals/16/documents/Studies/151-200/AFD-090519-008.pdf)

**Night effort and Italy-based forces.** No. 419 (RCAF) Squadron's Form 541 identifies its Halifax II crews on the Leipzig raid on the night of **19/20 February**, including an unsuccessful sortie. A 5th Bomb Wing daily compilation for **24 February** names the US 2nd, 97th and 301st Bomb Groups attacking Steyr, with the 325th, 1st, 14th and 82nd Fighter Groups providing withdrawal support. Weather separated the 99th BG, which attacked Fiume. The 5th Wing website supplies a dated transcription/compilation without a facsimile or archival identifier on the page. These are four named bomber and four named fighter groups in this wing account. Other Fifteenth Air Force forces and other days require independent returns. Compare the contemporary aircraft and crew records with dated factory production or repair records before attributing an industrial effect to a formation. [No. 419 Squadron, Form 541, February 1944, p. 118 and adjacent sheets](https://www.bombercommandmuseumarchives.ca/squadronorb_419_f541/419_1944-02.pdf) · [5th BW daily account, February 24](https://15thaf.org/5th_BW/Missions/February%201944/February%201944.html)

## Bodenplatte

**1 January 1945.** The attack accounts identify elements of **at least eleven distinct fighter-wing parents**: JG 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 11, 26, 27, 53, 54 and 77. JG 54's III. and IV. Gruppen appear separately with other commands. This is a lower bound on represented Jagdgeschwader identities, not eleven complete wings. The wider force included a ground-attack unit, III./SG 4, and other supporting elements. NIMH traces JG 1's I., II. and III. Gruppen in the operation, including aircraft lost or turned back before reaching target airfields. The Atlas already holds 37 formation-list leads, 20 locations and 11 RAF ground-defence reports. Its opportunity is to reconcile each lead to a dated departure, engagement, documented diversion, or unresolved status, and each location to a resident, visiting, airborne, or grounded unit. [Rickard, sections JG 1 to 5. Jagddivision](https://www.historyofwar.org/articles/operation_bodenplatte.html) · [NIMH, “Geheime verrassingsaanval”](https://www.nimh.nl/militaire-geschiedenis-van-nederland/webthemas/bevrijding-van-nederland-1944-1945/achtergronden/jagdgeschwader-1-twente/na-market-garden)

**Ghent and Asch illustrate why one count per airfield fails.** The Polish wartime report gives the complete named three-squadron set for 131 Wing's cited departures at B-61. No. 302 sent eight aircraft, 308 sent twelve, 317 sent eleven: **31**. It records two Spitfires and two pilots lost in air combat, **18** Spitfires destroyed and **five** damaged on the ground. At Asch, the 487th Fighter Squadron's Capt. Henry Stewart recorded his own encounter and claims. His report confirms squadron participation, while a one-pilot report cannot enumerate all defenders. The RAF study's 335 attackers observed at eleven fields are a subset of its estimated 790–870 fighters involved. Follow contested Volkel separately: defence observers reported an attack, whereas Rickard describes JG 6 missing its objective. An observer's sighting, a JG assignment and a damage return answer three different questions.
