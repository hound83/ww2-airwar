# Early campaigns • deployment evidence

The published formation-history index at v10.0 (9e25892) includes none of these four campaign IDs. Their campaign narratives, participants, facts and map layers are separate evidence. A blank formation-history index cannot establish absence from battle. The checks below identify historical populations that can be audited at a stated place, time and echelon.

## Spain, July 1936–April 1939

**Brunete, opening on the night of 5–6 July 1937.** The Spanish aviation-history journal describes six Republican fighter escuadrillas initially available in this sector (four I-16 and two I-15), opposed by three Fiat fighter escuadrillas. The next arrivals it distinguishes are one Bf 109 escuadrilla, another three Fiat escuadrillas, and fighters of Grupo Morato coming out of the Seville depot. The last entry carries no squadron count. Six and three are useful denominators for opening *fighter escuadrillas at Brunete*; the Republic's I-16/I-15 equipment does not establish pilots' nationality. The source does not identify every opening squadron by number. Its holding of 36 aircraft per Condor or Italian group is establishment; it separately describes lower holdings in this period. [Spanish Ministry of Defence, *Aeroplano* 29, “Batalla de Brunete”, printed p. 183](https://publicaciones.defensa.gob.es/media/downloadable/files/links/a/e/aeroplano_29.pdf)

**Llimiana/Tremp, 24 December 1938.** The reproduced Republican fighter-wing operations diary records that 6ª and 7ª Escuadrillas of Grupo 21 took off with ten and nine aircraft respectively to escort one escuadrilla of Grupo 30. These are two named escort squadrons and 19 aircraft flown on this one mission. The opposing Grupo 2G3 diary identifies two of its pilots/aircraft attached to Grupo 3G3 for protection and patrol in the Tremp sector. Those two aircraft document a detachment, and the enemy-strength estimate in the Republican diary is an observation. Neither yields the whole Nationalist force. The wartime entries are two opposing witnesses printed within one later article, not two independent publications. [Jesús Salas Larrazábal, *Aeroplano* 3, “El día más aciago de los Natachas”, printed pp. 80–81, sections “Punto de vista de la Escuadra de Caza Republicana” and “Los partes de la Escuadra de Caza Nacional”](https://publicaciones.defensa.gob.es/media/downloadable/files/links/a/e/aeroplano_03.pdf)

**Identity and geographic gaps.** In May 1938 the German archival inventory assigns the attacks on four Castellón villages to 5. Staffel of Jagdgruppe 88 and identifies contemporary material in RL 35/34. Its role was dive bombing. Counting all of J/88 as fighter sorties from the parent name would introduce a second error on top of the missing unit count. This is a collection description and unit attribution; the individual attack sheets were not collated here. Preserve separate series for the Republic, Nationalist Spanish aviation, German Condor Legion, Italian Aviazione Legionaria, and foreign personnel serving inside Spanish units. Brunete and Tremp leave Madrid, the northern front, the Ebro and transfers between fronts open. [Bundesarchiv, *Digitalisierung im Militärarchiv*, “Bombenangriffe zu Testzwecken der Legion Condor”, RL 35/34](https://www.bundesarchiv.de/assets/bundesarchiv/de/Downloads/Berichte/2019-12-31_digitalisierung-ma-uebersicht.pdf)

## Poland, September–October 1939

**Warsaw, 1 September.** Col. Stefan Pawlikowski's 16:00 situation report records two Pursuit Brigade dywizjony, III and IV, in action that morning. The Polish Institute of National Remembrance identifies their four constituent fighter escadrilles (111, 112, 113, 114) and the additional 123 from Kraków, attached on 1 September. This gives five fighter escadrilles in the reinforced brigade. The 152nd, from the Modlin Army, assisted separately. Recording six as brigade members would be incorrect. The brigade commander's German aircraft sightings and victory reports are contemporary observations. They cannot supply the Luftwaffe roster or verified write-offs. Army aircraft outside Warsaw and the Bomber Brigade require separate returns. [Polish Institute and Sikorski Museum, Pawlikowski report, LOT.A.II.11/1d doc. 2, pp. 104–105](https://pism.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2024/04/IPMS_ALBUM_ARCHIWUM_SPREADS_LR_corr.pdf) · [IPN historical notes, pp. 16–17 and 123rd Escadrille pilot note](https://ipn.gov.pl/download/1/352230/111instrukcjaweb.pdf)

**German forces.** A Polish archaeological/historical study identifies three specifically Bf 110-equipped Gruppen ready on 1 September: I./ZG 1, I.(Z)/LG 1 and I./ZG 76. It gives 95 aircraft across the three and identifies 1., 2. and 3. Staffeln plus the command element of I.(Z)/LG 1. On 3 September they flew from Jessau on escort duty for III.(K)/LG 1. Single-engine fighter formations sit outside that population. An American official history's Table 1 instead lists five single-engine and five twin-engine fighter Gruppen across Luftflotten 1 and 4 (3+2 each). These are differently described populations and conflicting evidence about the twin-engine category. Verify inclusion, dates, equipment and attached formations against German returns before producing percentages from either table. [Karasiewicz et al., *Raport* 16, “Samolot Bf 110 w ataku na Polskę” and “Dzień 3 września 1939 r.”](https://raportarcheologiczny.nid.pl/wp-content/uploads/2022/07/Raport-16_Karasiewicz-et-al.pdf) · [John F. Kreis, *Air Warfare and Air Base Air Defense*, Table 1 “Luftwaffe Order of Battle, Losses in the Polish Campaign”](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-D301-PURL-gpo113228/pdf/GOVPUB-D301-PURL-gpo113228.pdf)

**Slovakia and the Soviet Union.** The v10.0 Polish participants list omits the Slovak air arm. Igor Baka documents the Slovak escort deployment to Kamenica nad Cirochou, letka 39 (also 12) on 7 September, elements of letka 37 (also 11) on 9 September. They undertook escort duties for German dive bombers, and the account also records Slovak reconnaissance/courier work. Its footnotes identify German orders in BA-MA RH 24/18. The Soviet invasion opened another front on 17 September. The Polish Air Force Museum describes aircraft mobilized and local encounters, including a flight near Rokitno. Its approximately 2,000 mobilized aircraft and IPN's 3,300 combat aircraft in its account of the aggression have no reconciled reporting populations here. Neither establishes the number of Soviet aviation regiments deployed, sorties flown or available aircraft over one locality. [Baka, *Slovensko vo vojne proti Poľsku v roku 1939*, discussion and notes 115–122](https://www.vhu.sk/data/att/276/19836.861607.pdf) · [Air Force Museum in Dęblin, 17 September operations](https://muzeumsp.pl/aktualnosci/polskie-lotnictwo-wojskowe-przeciw-sowietom-we-wrzesniu-1939-r/) · [IPN, history of the Soviet invasion](https://ipn.gov.pl/pl/aktualnosci/247716,Uroczystosci-w-87-rocznice-agresji-ZSRS-na-Polske.html)

## Norway, April–June 1940

**German assignments, April.** The specialist transcription of the X. Fliegerkorps force schedule credited to Oberst Gaul lists II./JG 77 and I./ZG 76, with I./ZG 1 only from 15 April. The entries are individual Gruppen; representing JG 77 as a complete deployed Geschwader would overstate that assignment. The schedule also lists bomber, dive-bomber, reconnaissance, coastal and transport elements. For example KG 4 changes from three Gruppen to III./KG 4 only from 15 April. The geographical remit was Weserübung, including Denmark, transport and sea approaches. Preserve “assigned” separately from “flew over Norway”. The transcription identifies its compiler and month; its original archival signature and individual flying records remain open. [SIG Luftwaffe in Norway, Gaul, “Gliederung des X. Fliegerkorps und der zugeteilten und unterstellten Verbände”, April 1940](https://www.luftwaffe.no/SIG/veserubung/veser1.html)

**Norwegian and British fighters are different populations.** The Norwegian Army Air Service's Jagervingen at Fornebu had seven operational Gladiators on 9 April, compared with twelve received in 1938–39. No. 263 Squadron RAF's Form 540 explicitly acknowledges reconstruction from W/C Atcherley's report and other material. It records a disagreement between 20 and 21 May on the flight from *Furious* to Bardufoss. The RAF Air Historical Branch records two RAF fighter squadrons over the Narvik battle area on 28 May, with 42 operational flights by No. 263 and 53 by No. 46 as French and Polish ground forces advanced. These 95 flights do not imply 95 aircraft or round-the-clock cover. Carrier aircraft, UK-based Coastal and Bomber Command aircraft, and Norwegian naval aviation remain outside the two-squadron count. [Norwegian *Offisersbladet* 2/2021, Fornebu/Jagervingen account and individual Gladiator serials](https://www.dittmagasin.no/offisersbladet/utgivelser/offisersbladet022021bc6xkzkt) · [No. 263 Squadron, AIR 27/1547, Form 540, May preface](https://263squadron.weebly.com/uploads/9/2/1/7/92171046/air271547f540.pdf) · [RAF AHB, *The Campaign in Norway*, Narvik, 26–28 May](https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/our-history/air-historical-branch/second-world-war-campaign-narratives/campaign-in-norway-april-june-1940/)

## France and the Low Countries, May–June 1940

**German formation scale, 10 May.** Kreis Table 5 names JG 3, 26, 51, 27, 52 and 53 and separately I./JG 21 under Luftflotten 2 and 3. Six distinct Geschwader designations are a lower bound for represented parent identities, never a count of complete wings in France. Nafziger’s dated disposition gives Stab and II./JG 26 at Dortmund, I./JG 26 at Bönninghardt, and III./JG 26 at Essen-Mülheim, demonstrating three separately identifiable fighter Gruppen even within this one wing. The same compilation includes home/coastal and Norway stations. Filter by assigned command and phase, then verify actual participation. Kreis and Nafziger also disagree on the aircraft recorded for JG 51 (Table 5 gives Me 110, the Gruppe table gives Bf 109 E). A reference title or aggregate order alone cannot settle every row. [Kreis, Table 5, “The Luftwaffe in the West, 1940”](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-D301-PURL-gpo113228/pdf/GOVPUB-D301-PURL-gpo113228.pdf) · [G. F. Nafziger, *German Luftwaffe, Invasion of France, 10 May 1940*, Jafü 2 table and bibliography](https://www.generalstaff.org/NAF/Pt_I_1939-1940/940geal.pdf)

**Allied force definitions, 9–16 May.** Immediately before the offensive, six Hurricane squadrons were in the British Air Forces in France: Nos. 85, 87, 607 and 615 in the BEF Air Component and Nos. 1 and 73 in the Advanced Air Striking Force. The RAF appendix dates the four Air Component squadrons to 9 May, adds Nos. 3 and 79 on 10 May and No. 504 on 11 May. The pre-offensive six across two commands and the six in the Air Component alone on the evening of 10 May are distinct rosters. Dowding's 16 May letter gives *ten squadron equivalents* dispatched in recent days; this is a flow measured in equivalents, not ten additional named complete squadrons. France’s Groupement de Chasse 23 alone lists five fighter groupes on 10 May (GC II/2, III/2, I/4, I/5, III/7) plus separate escadrille ECMJ 1/16. The RAF 9 May map no. 103 itself excludes three Alpine and two Southern French fighter groupes. Do not silently drop them or include them in the map's denominator. [Kreis, Table 4, “Strength Summary, British Air Forces in France”](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-D301-PURL-gpo113228/pdf/GOVPUB-D301-PURL-gpo113228.pdf) · [Dowding letter, 16 May, para. 8, reproduced with facsimile](https://oldcranwellians.info/ewExternalFiles/Guidebook%20L.pdf) · [French unit-history register, 10 May, Groupement Chasse n°23](https://www.traditions-air.fr/unit/chrono/1940-05-10.htm) · [RAF AHB, *Campaign in France and the Low Countries*, appendices, RAF Component fighter-strength appendix and map 103](https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/our-history/air-historical-branch/second-world-war-campaign-narratives/campaign-in-france-and-the-low-countries-sep-1939-jun-1940-appendices-and-maps/)

**Belgian and Dutch presence.** The Belgian opening disposition lists six numbered escadrilles in the 2e Régiment, including 1/I/2 and 2/I/2 at Schaffen. This is the regimental list according to one secondary compilation and does not establish serviceability on 10 May. The Dutch military history institute records the 1e Jacht Vliegtuig Afdeeling at De Kooy and active resistance from that field on 10 May. Army and naval flying units and day-by-day withdrawals must be checked independently for each country. The air forces defending the Low Countries cannot be represented by RAF and French records alone. [Nafziger, Belgian Air Force, 10 May 1940, 2e Régiment](https://www.generalstaff.org/NAF/Pt_I_1939-1940/940ebdd.pdf) · [Nederlands Instituut voor Militaire Historie, “De Kooy”, mobilisation and 10 May entry](https://www.nimh.nl/militaire-geschiedenis-van-nederland/historische-overzichten/vliegvelden-in-oorlogstijd/de-kooy)
