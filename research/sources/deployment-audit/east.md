# Eastern Front — deployment and coverage checks

These are dated checks against the v10.0 dossiers and the Bagration specialist register. Each figure below carries its own population. “Assigned”, “serviceable”, “flew” and “achieved” answer different questions. Source counts refer only to the named command, role, time and geography.

## Barbarossa, June–December 1941

- **A Soviet roster with an explicit exclusion.** The Southwestern Front air commander’s 21 August report reconstructs his command on 22 June. Section I.1 lists 11 divisions and two separate reconnaissance regiments. Within the 14th Mixed Air Division it names the 17th, 46th and 89th Fighter Regiments and the 253rd Assault Regiment. The readiness table says the 253rd was still forming, had no aircraft and took no part in combat. Four regiments belonged to this division on paper, three had aircraft. This does not establish that all three flew on 22 June. The 17th Mixed Air Division table separately flags its 242nd and 244th bomber regiments as still forming with no aircraft. Retain the report’s roster and table independently where labels differ. Primary wartime report in a postwar document edition, vol. 36, no. 77, §§ I.1–2, TsAMO f.229 op.181 d.25 ff.1–12, 30–33. [Text with the tables](https://ru.wikisource.org/wiki/Сборник_боевых_документов/36/077) · [digitized 1959 edition, pp.109–111](https://djvu.online/file/vMemxydb0IeMB).
- **Four German commands, only part of the fourth.** The Bundesarchiv identifies Luftflotten 1, 2, 4 and 5 and the operational reports of the Luftwaffe General Staff in RL 2-II. Askey’s 21 June reconstruction limits the fourth to the eastern portion of Luftflotte 5. Within Luftflotte 1 alone the fighter table lists the staff and three Gruppen of JG 54, and two Staffeln of JG 53. Its three JG 54 Gruppen have respectively 34, 33 and 35 serviceable aircraft, out of 40 available each. Adding the HQ's three serviceable aircraft to the three Gruppen gives 105 for those four JG 54 entries. A JG 53 attachment does not make the entire wing present. This is a northern-sector sample. [Bundesarchiv guide and holdings](https://www.bundesarchiv.de/im-archiv-recherchieren/archivgut-recherchieren/nach-themen/unternehmen-barbarossa-der-deutsche-ueberfall-auf-die-sowjetunion-in-den-quellen-der-luftwaffe/) · [Askey, sample pp.2 and 8, 21 June table](https://operationbarbarossa.net/wp-content/uploads/2013/03/Luftwaffe-OOB-sample.pdf).
- **Coalition scope.** The Atlas mentions Romania and Finland. The Romanian 7th Fighter Group lists 56th, 57th and 58th Squadrons on 22 June, with the 5th and 8th Groups also represented in the combat grouping. The Finnish 22 June deployment map places LLv 24 at Vesivehmaa/Selänpää and LLv 26 at Joroinen. Their presence belongs on a station map, with separate evidence for operations on 25 June. Publish sector, boundary date and flying status separately for northern, central and southern forces. The German intelligence estimate in the dossier has a different population from a Soviet return. Sterrett’s Table 1 counts 7,133 aircraft in five western military districts on 21 June plus 1,339 long-range aircraft, with readiness detailed separately. [Romanian unit reconstruction](https://www.worldwar2.ro/arme/index.php?article=23) · [Finnish historical society, distribution map dated 22 June](https://www.sshs.fi/content/uploads/2023/02/Ilmavoimat_iskee.pdf) · [Sterrett, Table 1, displayed p.156](https://kclpure.kcl.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/2925857/429305.pdf).

## Stalingrad, August 1942–February 1943

- **An entire air arm missing from the dossier participant line.** The Romanian Air Force’s institutional history explicitly counts **26 Romanian squadrons** in the Stalingrad–Don Bend area at the end of September 1942. The count comprises 6 fighter, 2 fighter-bomber, 7 bomber, 6 observation, 2 reconnaissance, 2 transport and 1 medical squadron. Its next paragraphs describe support for German Sixth Army and Romanian Third Army. The specialist Romanian register identifies Fighter Groups 7 and 8, each with three fighter squadrons, plus Fighter-Bomber Group 6 with two. The six fighter squadron IDs are 56, 57, 58, 41, 42 and 60, and the two fighter-bomber IDs are 61 and 62. The eight align numerically with the official end-September categories. Validate unit-level dates against returns before plotting all eight on the same day. The Atlas participant field lists the Luftwaffe and Soviet VVS. [Romanian Air Force, opening paragraphs](https://roaf.ro/?cpt_services=activitatea-fortelor-aeriene-in-zona-stalingrad-si-cotul-donului) · [Romanian specialist register, Stalingrad section](https://www.worldwar2.ro/arme/index.php?article=23).
- **Soviet report and measured population.** Sterrett reproduces an on-strength table on 22 November for 8th, 16th and 17th Air Armies. Their fighter counts were 284, 114 and 82, giving 480 fighters across a total of 1,267 aircraft. The source gives readiness separately and says that the 2nd Air Army was operationally subordinated to the Southwestern Front while remaining under Voronezh Front administratively. The three-row table is a complete sum for those rows and an incomplete operational roster for Uranus. The 8th Air Army's 16 November employment plan is an archival lead for subsidiary formations. Its catalogue establishes authorship, date and remit; squadron execution requires daily returns. [Sterrett, displayed table on p.187 and note 394 citing wartime study no.6 p.148, discussion on following page](https://kclpure.kcl.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/2925857/429305.pdf) · [primary plan, f.346 op.5953ss d.4 ff.89–97](https://docs.historyrussia.org/ru/nodes/221309-plan-boevogo-primeneniya-8-y-vozdushnoy-armii-ot-16-noyabrya-1942-g-v-kontrnastuplenii-pod-stalingradom-16-noyabrya-1942-goda).
- **German phase change.** A German command reconstruction for VIII. Fliegerkorps names JG 3, ZG 1, StG 2 and KG 27 on 10 November. A wing name alone does not establish which Gruppen were serviceable. On 25 November Richthofen’s diary records 30 Ju 52s available, nine making sorties, and 75 tons delivered against a 300-ton order. Those are different units of measure. Treat the September air battle, November counteroffensive, December relief and January airlift separately. [10 November command snapshot](https://www.lexikon-wehrmacht.de/Gliederungen/Fliegerkorps/Fliegerkorps8-R.htm) · [Hayward, *Airpower Journal*, German command diary excerpts and notes 40–42](https://www.allworldwars.com/Stalingrad-An-Examination-of-Hitlers-Decision-to-Airlift-by-Joel-Hayward.html).

## Kursk, July–August 1943

- **Auditable opening-day German denominator.** Karl-Heinz Frieser’s chapter in the German official history reconstructs forces committed on 5 July, using the 30 June operational status reports (BA-MA RL 2-III/725) updated with Ulf Balke’s documents. The fighter category names four numbered Gruppen with 1. Fliegerdivision in the north (I., III., IV./JG 51 and I./JG 54) and four with VIII. Fliegerkorps in the south (I., III./JG 52 and II., III./JG 3). **Eight numbered day-fighter Gruppen under four parent designations** across those two commands. The northern list also includes the JG 51 staff, Stabsstaffel and Spanish 15. Staffel. The southern list includes Stab/JG 52. With those additional entries, the source reports 186 actual and 145 operational northern fighters, plus 153 actual and 118 operational southern fighters. Its night fighters, heavy fighters and tank-attack Staffeln have separate categories. Footnote 8 excludes 41 Hungarian operational aircraft stationed in the southern sector from the German corps total. The 4 July command tables independently expose the same eight group IDs. The dossier's rounded 2,000 aircraft allocated to Citadel has a different scope. [Frieser, *Germany and the Second World War*, VIII, Part II.I.2(a), pp.87–92, status tables and notes 7–8](https://ebrary.net/116699/political_science/german_potential_kursk_salient) · [north, 4 July](https://lexikon-der-wehrmacht.de/Gliederungen/Fliegerdivision/Fliegerdiv1-3-R.htm) · [south, 4 July](https://www.lexikon-wehrmacht.de/Gliederungen/Fliegerkorps/Fliegerkorps8-R.htm).
- **A genuine source disagreement.** The Nafziger-labelled German compilation hosted by the US Army CGSC library assigns three Gruppen of JG 54 and three of JG 1 to 1. Fliegerdivision. The more closely dated German table identifies just I./JG 54 and no JG 1 fighter Gruppen in that command. Keep the discrepant assignments out of the verified 5 July numerator pending direct record comparison. The fact that a library hosts a compilation does not establish the quality of a particular row. [Nafziger collection, *German Forces, Battle of Kursk*, Luftflotte 6 section](https://cgsc.contentdm.oclc.org/digital/api/collection/p15040coll6/id/6043/download).
- **Soviet coverage changes by phase.** Central Front order 00366/op, 5 July, addresses 16th Air Army and **2nd Tank Army**. The second addressee must not become “2nd Air Army” through abbreviation. The Soviet OOB compilation does place 2nd Air Army with Voronezh Front. Under 16th Air Army it lists 6th Fighter Aviation Corps and its 273rd and 279th Fighter Aviation Divisions, among others. The 14 July 16th Air Army order belongs to the counteroffensive. Record tactical command, frontage and date before bringing in later air armies, air-defence (PVO) formations or long-range forces. [5 July order, TsAMO f.62 op.329 d.23 f.20, catalogue](https://docs.historyrussia.org/ru/nodes/189939-boevoe-rasporyazhenie-komanduyuschego-voyskami-tsentralnogo-fronta-locale-nil-00366-op-komanduyuschim-2-y-tankovoy-i-16-y-vozdushnoy-armiyami-na-nanesenie-kontrudara-5-iyulya-1943-g) · [14 July order 0050, f.368 op.21854s d.2 ff.64–65, catalogue](https://docs.historyrussia.org/ru/nodes/221314-prikaz-komanduyuschego-16-y-vozdushnoy-armiey-locale-nil-0050-ot-14-iyulya-1943-g-na-boevye-deystviya-aviatsii-v-kontrnastuplenii-pod-kurskom) · [Soviet compilation, 2nd, 16th and 17th Air Army sections](https://cgsc.contentdm.oclc.org/digital/api/collection/p15040coll6/id/5407/download).

## Bagration, June–August 1944

- **One slice passes.** The specialist Bagration register contains 1st, 3rd, 4th, 6th and 16th Air Armies. All **5/5 named Soviet frontal air-army commands** match Lt Col William Connor’s June force comparison. He assigns 3rd to 1st Baltic Front, 1st to 3rd Belorussian, 4th to 2nd Belorussian, and 16th and 6th to the right and left wings of 1st Belorussian. He dates the left-wing offensive to 18 July. The same study treats long-range aviation separately. This establishes command-name coverage. It leaves regimental coverage, daily availability and mission attribution open. The Atlas already has long-range aviation and French GC 3 Normandie as separate records. [Connor, US Army Combat Studies Institute, *Analysis of Deep Attack Operations*, Annex C and planning timeline](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/GOVPUB-D110-PURL-LPS68395/pdf/GOVPUB-D110-PURL-LPS68395.pdf).
- **A dated contribution from the ground command.** The 3rd Belorussian Front report at midnight on 23 June records 1st Air Army providing cover, reconnaissance and support, and 877 bombing/assault sorties, including 105 at night. Its 27 June report gives 234 bombing/assault sorties, explicitly mentioning fighter cover separately. Neither number counts fighter cover. This pair adds an observable activity for the beneficiary and highlights the limits of a simple sortie comparison. The 3rd Air Army’s separate June review is catalogued at f.311 op.4495 d.229 ff.1–58. [Wartime front reports reprinted in *Liberation of Belarus*, pp.303–305, TsAMO f.241 op.2593 d.232 ff.104–105 and 141–143](https://militera.org/books/pdf/h/sb_osvobozhdenie-belarusi.pdf) · [3rd Air Army June review, catalogue](https://docs.historyrussia.org/ru/nodes/217760).
- **A single German parent can be genuine within a narrow selection.** John Weal’s 26 June Bf 109 table names I. and III./JG 51 under Luftflotte 6. The Atlas has both Gruppen and separately includes attack, bomber and reconnaissance units. The table’s type filter excludes non-Bf 109 forces. More importantly, Weal places I./JG 51 at Orsha, whereas the German unit chronology puts its staff, 1. and 3. Staffeln at Dokudowo on 25 June and Bojary on 26 June, and sends 2. Staffel to Germany. Mark the 26 June station disputed. British official history describes reinforcements from Germany, the southern front, Italy and Normandy by early July. A static 26 June fighter list cannot cover the full campaign. [Weal, *Bf 109 Aces of the Russian Front*, p.87, 26 June appendix](https://www.ospreypublishing.com/us/bf-109-aces-of-the-russian-front-9781841760841/) · [JG 51 station chronology, 1944, I. Gruppe](https://www.lexikon-der-wehrmacht.de/Gliederungen/Jagdgeschwader/JG51-R.htm) · [British Air Ministry, *The Rise and Fall of the German Air Force*, ch.16 §§2–6](https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/our-history/air-historical-branch/second-world-war-thematic-studies/rise-and-fall-of-the-german-air-force-1933-1945/).

## Berlin, April–May 1945

- **Primary reports separate four armies across the operation.** Wartime documents 136–143 include the 16th Air Army’s 17 April order to 6th Bomber Aviation Corps, the 18th Air Army’s 26 April report, the 2nd Air Army’s own 26 April report and the 4th Air Army’s 29 April order to 325th Night Bomber Aviation Division. Their presence across these dates does not make every army’s resources city-wide on 16 April. On the first day the 16th Air Army AAR attributes 5,342 sorties to its aircraft plus 766 by 18th Air Army and 440 by 4th on the reporting front’s sector, giving 6,548. The 16th Air Army itself records 1,188 day fighters flying 2,545 sorties that day. Its 4,054 sorties on 20 April independently confirm that dossier figure; they include 2,133 fighter sorties. [*Battle for Berlin*, documents 136–143, particularly report of 19 May, TsAMO f.233 op.2356 d.776 ff.432–565](https://militera.lib.ru/docs/da/berlin_45/07.html).
- **Retain a numerical disagreement.** That same AAR lists 3,037 serviceable aircraft before the offensive, including 1,548 fighters and separate entries of 51 reconnaissance and 67 spotting aircraft. A later order-of-battle sheet for 16th Air Army gives 3,188 total, of which 3,033 combat, with 114 reconnaissance/spotting. The other four role figures align; the combined reconnaissance/spotting count differs by four (118 versus 114). The populations are labelled differently. The compilation also says 28 divisions and seven separate regiments and attributes sub-divisional information to unspecified additional sources. Treat these as verification targets. A Polish General Staff Academy study documents the Polish 1st Air Corps and 4th Aviation Division changing airfield clusters while supporting the offensive. The v10 overview lists only the Soviet VVS and Luftwaffe. [Soviet 16th Air Army AAR, *Indicators*, pp.201–203](https://militera.lib.ru/docs/da/berlin_45/07.html) · [compiled 16th Air Army roster, pp.1–endnote](https://www.generalstaff.org/NAF/Pt_I_1943-1945/945rdad.pdf) · [Krzemiński, Polish General Staff Academy, 1965, pp.38–39](https://pbio.akademia.mil.pl/wp-content/scans/usb1/PARTIA_6_I_EKSPORTOWA/03_PDF/61111.pdf).
- **German stations are insufficient.** II. and III./JG 4 station tables record both moving to Rechlin-Roggenthin (rendered Rechlin-Roggethin by this source) on 19 April. I./JG 4 had been dissolved on 19 March. IV./JG 4 is described as flying over Berlin, with dissolution dated only to April. Do not assign all four Gruppen to daily missions. The British Air Ministry warns explicitly that by the second week of April a German base could no longer be classified reliably as operating only against the Soviets or only against the Western Allies. Full German mission roster remains unknown in this audit. [JG 4 unit histories and date tables](https://www.lexikon-der-wehrmacht.de/Gliederungen/Jagdgeschwader/JG4-R.htm) · [British Air Ministry, ch.18 §§16–18](https://www.raf.mod.uk/what-we-do/our-history/air-historical-branch/second-world-war-thematic-studies/rise-and-fall-of-the-german-air-force-1933-1945/).
