# What the formation and operations screens actually count

Baseline `9e25892cba20b1a4908f04ef0e2521cd3b941f4e`, Atlas v10.0. Chromium loaded the served, unmodified checkout. I opened the Normandy D-Day and Britain 7 September operation controls, selected the Sicily readiness case and its formation filter, opened the Bodenplatte formation table, and counted the Bagration directory. External map tiles were excluded from this test. Counts below describe the identified Atlas layer. Several dossiers have further sources and studies beyond that layer.

### A faithfully copied roster can still omit a participant

For 1 January 1945, Nafziger's JG 4 block on p. 1 prints I., III. and IV. Gruppen. The Babenhausen Fw 190 entry is labelled III. The Atlas faithfully reflects that source. A separate unit chronology places **II.** Gruppe and parts of its 5th–8th Staffeln at Babenhausen/Schafstädt and places III. Gruppe at Darmstadt-Griesheim. On the same date the Atlas' airframe file records Fw 190s of 5./JG 4 (WNr 682676) and 7./JG 4 (WNr 682296) in the Netherlands. These lower-level records support the presence of aircraft from II. Gruppe's constituent Staffeln. The strength of the Gruppe, participation by every Staffel and arrival over any one target remain open. Preserve Nafziger's original row and place the disagreement beside it; silently turning III into II would erase the separate III. Gruppe question. [Nafziger, item 945GALA, pp. 1–2, citing Girbig](https://www.generalstaff.org/NAF/Pt_I_1943-1945/945gala.pdf) · [Jagdgeschwader 4, 1945 station table](https://www.lexikon-der-wehrmacht.de/Gliederungen/Jagdgeschwader/JG4-R.htm) · [Atlas transcription of SGLO, printed p. 127, 5./JG 4 and 7./JG 4](https://github.com/hound83/ww2-airwar/blob/9e25892cba20b1a4908f04ef0e2521cd3b941f4e/research/transcriptions/bodenplatte-operation-airframes-1945-01-01.csv) · [SGLO register maintained by the compilers](https://www.verliesregister.studiegroepluchtoorlog.nl/)

The Bodenplatte compiler produces 20 site records (15 “attacked”, 2 intercepted, 3 disputed or unresolved), 210 source/measure records, and 175 named airframe entries. It classifies 164 of the 210 records as Allied and 46 as German. Those numbers describe density of documentation and include overlapping witnesses and measures. Individual loss extracts also have explicit geographic limits. Of the 11 references in the separate archival follow-up's `archive` array, the access notes flag unresolved raid-day leaf, scan, original-piece or reference verification in every case. The wartime evidence already quoted in other fields and published official histories remains independently usable. ## Coverage outside the roster

The separate [November campaign source register](https://github.com/hound83/ww2-airwar/blob/9e25892cba20b1a4908f04ef0e2521cd3b941f4e/research/transcriptions/campaign-evidence-audit-2026-11-12.json) has 66 series across all 20 campaigns and 343 observations. Kursk supplies 162 rows (47.2%); 73 of those are one division's airframe entries. Fourteen campaigns have at least one series for each of the register's two side codes. Malta, Barbarossa, Reich defence, Italy and Berlin have only Allied-coded series; Dieppe only Axis-coded. Of all 66 series, 15 carry the register's `recorded` tag, 30 `source-compiled`, 21 `secondary-reconstruction`. France alone has a `recorded` series for each side, still with different reporting populations. These are counts of one archived register. Specialist live pages and later research include material beyond it (for example Malta has Axis activity in its specialist data). The Spanish Republic's “Allied” label and Nationalist side's “Axis” label are database bins that flatten actual belligerents.

The [operations timeline](https://hound83.github.io/ww2-airwar/operations-1944.html) includes 2,103 records for a file scope of 1 January–31 July 1944. Of those, 1,248 are indexed as RAF Bomber Command and 13 as US Ninth Air Force. The latter span only 6 distinct dates; 5 RAF Second Tactical Air Force records span 3 dates. Only 35 rows carry the `unit-action` kind, whereas 197 carry `loss-type` and 165 `loss-return`. This mix is valuable for comparing particular records. It cannot describe relative historical sortie volume or the share contributed by each command. The timeline's own monthly reconciliation table already demonstrates how to publish a comparison only for compatible measures.

## Reproduction on the audited revision

- Sicily: inspect `research/transcriptions/sicily-air-campaign-1943.json`, arrays `formations` and `cases[].formations`; read the note in `corkscrew`. The dropdown and detail use these same keys in `dist/sicily.js`.
- Operation cards: read `normandy-v86-operations.json` and `britain-v87-operations.json`, keys `formations` and `operations[].formations`; the renderer joins these same keys in `dist/grand-air-war-v86-operations.js`. Catalogue histories are in `formation-histories.json`, `units[].campaigns`.
- Bagration: group `bagration-air-campaign-1944.json` arrays `stations` and `balances` by `unit`; resolve each ID through `units`.
- Bodenplatte: `dist/bodenplatte-data.js` exports `inputFiles` and `compile`. Load each named file under `research/transcriptions`, leaving the CSV string intact, then inspect `compile(inputs).formations.rows`, `fields`, `records` and `airframes`. In particular compare the site's parent-name match to the earlier `unitLinks` specific to II./JG 26. The source transcription in `wikipedia-followup-evidence-2026-11-12.json` contains 37 Nafziger rows; it credits the compilation.
- November register: group `series` by `campaign`, then count distinct `side` values and `rows.length` with and without `status === "recorded"`. January–July operations: group `operations-january-june-1944.json.records` by the literal `force`, `kind` and `date` fields. The file's `period` covers July despite its historical filename.
