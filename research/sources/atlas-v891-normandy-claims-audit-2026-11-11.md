# Normandy aircraft claims and losses, expanded audit

## Cobra on 25 July 1944

**Working estimate: about 8 German aircraft destroyed in air combat from 12 Eighth Air Force escort claims.** Three selected pre-Cobra comparisons give 23 reconstructed airframe losses from 34 destruction claims, or 67.6 per 100. Multiplying by 12 gives 8.12 before rounding. v8.9 used 15 losses from 25 claims and displayed about 7. The estimate applies to the escort activity reported under Mission 494, including fighter cover beyond the St-Lô bombing area.

The comparison uses destruction claims in air combat and reconstructed aircraft lost in the corresponding engagement. Count one airframe for a shared claim and carry uncertain forced landings separately. A pilot casualty or emergency landing on its own leaves the aircraft disposition open. The case count is small and available reconstructions favor notable fights. One pre-Cobra comparison covers Eighth Air Force fighters in an evening engagement involving several groups. The other two comparisons concern Ninth Air Force fighters. The pooled rate is a proxy for the Mission 494 escorts. The model carries low confidence.

- **22 June.** Caldwell describes four downed Bf 109s in the Caen sequence with the 365th and four in the Vire encounters with the 368th. The first four are two pilots killed, one bailout and a further Bf 109 shot down after a turning engagement. The Vire sequence gives one pilot killed and three wounded bailouts. Reischer's damaged aircraft crash-landed and its final disposition is uncertain. The 5th Jagddivision diary count of 12 pilots missing by evening includes emergency landings and bailouts. It measures personnel accountability.
- **27 June.** Jure corroborates the time and participation of the 56th Fighter Group. The 14 claims and nine losses come from the Clark-based reconstruction. The [AAF chronology](https://b24.net/8thAAFcombat.htm) combines additional missions on that date, so it supplies a separate day total.
- **18 July.** The eleven claims count the shared Bf 109 once. The six baseline aircraft are from I./JG 26, III./JG 54 and III./JG 1. Blake's higher reconstruction includes I./JG 5 pilot Günther Wimsche, who force-landed. His aircraft's final disposition remains uncertain. See the [v8.9 source notes](atlas-v89-regional-claims-and-losses-2026-11-11.md).

At Clastres, the initial 367th Group squadron tally gives 16 destroyed. Returning pilots added seven claims to the revised tally. Its group history reconstructs 16–18 II./JG 6 losses, and the published German participant account gives 16. A separate tally gives 20 US claims, which would yield a prediction of 14. Applying the same rate to the 16 initial claims would yield 11. The choice of reporting stage affects the apparent accuracy of this check. Keep the separate 474th Fighter Group engagement and the afternoon ground claims out of this comparison. The mean absolute daily gap between the Allied claim column and the German total loss column was 9.1 aircraft. The reverse gap averaged 9.4. In each direction the claim count exceeded the opposite total loss count on seven days. On 14 July the Allied column gives 35 claims against 12 German losses. On 8 July the German column gives 68 claims against 23 Allied losses. Both directions can look close in an aggregate when daily differences cancel and loss causes are mixed. These are reporting comparisons. A country-, role- and cause-matched conversion would require the component returns.

## Opponents, roles and claim stages

The six reverse comparisons in the calculator keep the formation and the claim stage attached to each row. The cases against US fighters show two losses from four awarded credits at Beauvais–Compiègne, two air-combat losses plus one uncertain cause from 12 claims near Évreux–Mantes, three from eight filed claims at Rambouillet, and seven from 14–16 claims at Clastres. The last range spans a gun-camera-based tally and Frappé's pilot list. Together they show how much individual fights and counting conventions can differ.

- **RAF and US, 6 June.** Six German claims correspond to five reconstructed losses, two US P-47s and three RAF Typhoons. The mixed encounter supplies one comparison. [Clark-based reconstruction, p. 42](https://forum.dcs.world/topic/144168-the-air-battle-over-normandy/).
- **RCAF, 18 June.** Caldwell reconstructs two German victories over the Canadian 414 Squadron reconnaissance pair. The Canadian register gives two lost airframes, AM220 and AM251. [Caldwell, ch. 11, 18 June](https://electronicsandbooks.com/edt/manual/Publischer/G/German%20Miltary%20WW2/JG%2026-Top%20Guns%20Of%20The%20Luftwaffe%20c20091007%20%5B255%5D.pdf) · [CASPIR, AM220 and AM251](https://caspir.warplane.com/aircraft/serial-search/aircraft-no/200001102).
- **Polish 315 Squadron, 18 August.** The squadron's 16 destruction claims can be compared with eight identifiable II./JG 26 Fw 190 losses in a compiled list, seven fatal crashes and one bailout. The list also shows two emergency landings with different dispositions, while Caldwell gives eight German pilots killed and two wounded. A possible additional German formation leaves the engagement's full aircraft tally open. On the reverse side the list has five German Mustang claims, two marked confirmed, and one Polish Mustang loss. One separate German P-38 claim is outside the Polish Mustang comparison. [Polish squadron and German loss reconstruction](https://www.geocities.ws/ratuszynski/44/aug_18_44.html) · [Caldwell, ch. 11, 18 August](https://electronicsandbooks.com/edt/manual/Publischer/G/German%20Miltary%20WW2/JG%2026-Top%20Guns%20Of%20The%20Luftwaffe%20c20091007%20%5B255%5D.pdf) · [Polish discussion of the claim records](https://stratusonline.pl/historia/eugeniusz-horbaczewski/).
- **Belgium, 3 September.** The 38th Squadron of the US 55th Group claimed four Fw 190s near Grimbergen. Caldwell describes four wrecks from 2./JG 26. A separate fight with the 338th Squadron has three US claims and two clear losses plus a forced landing of uncertain final disposition. Caldwell's later diary also identifies RAF activity in the latter fight. Use the Grimbergen fight as a geographically adjacent check and keep both out of the pre-Cobra fit. [Caldwell, ch. 11, Grimbergen and Melsbroeck](https://electronicsandbooks.com/edt/manual/Publischer/G/German%20Miltary%20WW2/JG%2026-Top%20Guns%20Of%20The%20Luftwaffe%20c20091007%20%5B255%5D.pdf) · [Reconstruction citing Caldwell's War Diary, vol. II, p. 343](https://forum.dcs.world/topic/144168-the-air-battle-over-normandy/).

## Bobruisk, 26 June

The source gives 13 Soviet claims, 19 Soviet own aircraft lost and 29 engagements on 26 June. The two German unit returns supply 12 June aircraft removals by enemy action. At the existing midpoint assumption that 75% of those removals occurred in the opening battles, alternative shares of activity give the following results. [16th Air Army history, ch. 3](https://militera.lib.ru/h/16va/03.html) · [German unit returns and basing dates](atlas-v89-regional-claims-and-losses-2026-11-11.md#bobruisk-26-june-1944).

The claims-share calculation inherits the bias it would be used to investigate. The displayed estimate uses the engagement share and covers two German groups' removals by enemy action. Actual Luftwaffe daily loss returns, the groups' sortie locations and a separation of air, ground and flak causes are needed for a Soviet-versus-German loss conversion.
