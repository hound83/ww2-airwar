# German and Soviet air-operations source ledger: Normandy and Bagration
Date: 10 November 2026
Scope: original war reports, operational readiness, flight sorties, aircraft losses, claims, and explicit source disagreements. Linked from the Atlas campaign observations.

## Recoverable Soviet evidence: 16th Air Army, 24 and 26 June 1944
1. Original wartime 16th Air Army headquarters report dated **22 July 1944** concerning June 1944, published as excerpt in Soviet General Staff *Sbornik boevykh dokumentov Velikoi Otechestvennoi voiny*, issue 26 (1956), original **TsAMO/AMO USSR Fond 368, opis 207698s, delo 10, sheets 46–47**. Text: https://ru.wikisource.org/wiki/Сборник_боевых_документов/26/33 . The excerpt documents operational coordination but **does not itself reproduce all daily numeric returns**. It is the primary provenance trail, not a direct source for every figure below.
2. Soviet 16th Air Army published historical narrative, chapter 3, *On the Bobruisk–Warsaw Direction*: https://militera.lib.ru/h/16va/03.html . This **secondary retrospective** reproduces operational values with unit and day context:
   - **24 June 1944**: 16th Air Army **2,319 serviceable combat aircraft**, comprising **331 day bombers + 149 night bombers + 661 ground-attack + 1,108 fighters + 70 reconnaissance/spotting**. Sum = **2,319**. **1,846 combat-ready crews**, which are PEOPLE/crew teams, not more planes. Separately, **6th Air Army 321 aircraft / 248 ready crews** on another sector, not part of 16th Army's number.
   - **24 June** first-day operational flights **3,191**. Published task partition: **1,846 strike sorties, 860 escort sorties, 264 troop-cover sorties, 102 reconnaissance, 99 free-hunt, 20 airfield attacks**, summing exactly **3,191**. Flight counts are not distinct serviceable aircraft.
   - **26 June**: **2,950 combat sorties**, **19 Soviet aircraft lost** in 16th Air Army's own record. **13 German aircraft claimed shot down** in 29 air engagements: claims NOT German archival loss confirmations.
   - The June 24 'combat-ready crews' number coincides numerically with '1,846 strike sorties' (a *different quantity*). Do NOT infer identity of records due to equal digits.
3. Original related wartime June 1944 reports published as contemporaneous excerpts: 4th Assault Air Corps, 2nd Guards Assault Aviation Division, 299th Assault Aviation Division, all in *Sbornik*, issue 26. Index: https://ru.wikisource.org/wiki/Сборник_боевых_документов_Великой_Отечественной_войны/26 . The excerpts add operational narrative, NOT independently reconciled own losses by aircraft subtype.

**Publication status:** The original scanned RAF German-table PDFs were discovered and text-indexed, but independent row-by-row visual transcription of all scans was NOT completed. The web PDF screenshot source returned errors. Accordingly, **do not** invent daily strength/loss data from ambiguous OCR columns. Retain all URLs as catalogued future primary data rather than pretending that their entire long series is ingested.

**German operating data already legible in official postwar German-source study**: U.S. Navy NHHC, *The GAF and the Invasion of Normandy*: https://www.history.navy.mil/research/library/online-reading-room/title-list-alphabetically/g/gaf-invasion-normandy.html .
- **6–13 June 1944:** Luftflotte 3 **2,588 fighter sorties + 1,683 bomber/torpedo sorties**. Eight-day population, German regional command, not unique aircraft. The two series are covered within the longer report, not independent new counts.
- **6 June–1 July:** German LF3 **3,568 bomber/mining/torpedo + 9,151 day fighter/fighter-bomber/recon + 1,101 night fighter + 153 supply sorties**. These category values sum **13,973** yet the document *prints 13,983*. The **ten-sortie inconsistency remains unresolved**; all four categories carry a warning in Atlas; published headline is not forced as a corrected value. The same source compares to Allied 96,000 over its own accounting population, which is NOT necessarily comparable with D-Day 14,674 or different Allied theater definitions.
- These German sortie counts are not aircraft on strength, aircraft losses or verified German opponent claims. The loss denominators documented previously (826 LF3 destroyed 6 June–7 July) have different calendar coverage than 6 June–1 July sorties; no attrition or loss ratio is automatically computed.

## Explicit gaps and release policy
- Need original 16th Air Army day-by-day mission/loss Forms and reciprocating German air loss diaries for June 24/26 to corroborate own-loss versus claimed victories. Published original excerpts alone are too thin for this.
- Need independently validated printed cells from AHB VII/136, VII/152 and VII/107 for long German daily/monthly serviceability series; raw text extraction of multi-column scans is unsafe.
- 16th Army 2,319 serviceable subset of wider Bagration Soviet air forces, cannot add it to pre-existing 5,300 theater estimate; 6th Army 321 counts a different army and cannot silently become 16th Army total.
- Original-document search was expanded to relevant adjacent campaigns but no unrelated unverified aircraft loss figures were imported.

**Confidence:** direct archive URLs = verified provenance; published 16th Army quantities = secondary reproduction (not independent original tabulation); official US Navy German-source quantities = reliable retrospective account with one known arithmetic discrepancy. All data retain reporting population/time/scope in the Atlas.
